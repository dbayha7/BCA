    ThrusterChain · Bayesian Conformal Aggregation on the Unifloral TD3+BC host

    Does calibrated uncertainty make an offline critic choose better?

    ThrusterChain is a small, fully inspectable control task: four thrusters hold five floats still against moving water, and a propeller pushed past 80% throttle stalls. A controller learned only from a fixed recording (offline reinforcement learning) must decide what to do where the recording is thin. This interface collects what the current Unifloral-based experiments have and have not shown about Bayesian Conformal Aggregation (BCA), one claim at a time, with every number traced to its report.

    Five claims, kept apart

    BCA subtracts a calibrated uncertainty width U from the critic's value Q1 inside the actor's objective. That is one mechanism, but it is tested against five different claims, and passing an earlier one does not establish a later one. Each row links to the evidence section that judges it.

    1. Lower Q values. Subtracting κ·U lowers the score. This is arithmetic, true by construction.
    See error charts
    2. Error detection. Is U larger where Q1 is actually more wrong against the simulator reference? Requires a per-action association, not a lower average.
    See alignment
    3. Proportional correction. Does subtracting U remove overestimation without pushing values below the reference (overshoot)?
    See removed versus added
    4. Better action choices. On a fixed bank of candidate actions, does Q1 − κU pick higher-reference actions than raw Q1 and than simple controls? Improved and harmed switches are counted separately.
    See decisions
    5. Better policy returns. Does training the actor with the penalty produce a policy that earns more in the simulator, under matched data and budgets, including under controlled shifts?
    See returns

    Where things stand on 27 October 2026. Claim 1 holds by construction. Claims 2 and 3 are not supported on the development banks: within a state the width barely tracks the residual it is calibrated to, and that residual barely tracks the signed error against the simulator; raising the strength removes overestimation but adds underestimation. Claim 4 is not established: the penalty changes few choices, helps slightly more than it harms at strength 3, and a cheap actor-distance control does as well. Claim 5 has one positive but limited result: two continuation streams from one checkpoint beat native continuation and a distance control on 32 shared starts; the independent-seed test with a support gap did not complete on this machine. Nothing here tests an out-of-distribution start law yet.

    How to read the numbers

    Less negative is higher

    Every return and every Q value on this page is negative, because the reward is minus a cost. A value of −30 is higher than −60. If a critic predicts −30 where the simulator reference is −60, it overestimates by 30. Every chart axis says so again.

    Signed error

    signed error = predicted − reference. Positive means overestimation (too optimistic), negative means underestimation. A penalty that reduces a positive error toward zero is a correction; one that drives it negative has overshot.

    Three different "targets"

    • Simulator reference V. The candidate action once, then that model's own frozen actor for the remaining horizon, discount 0.98, 100 steps in total, no terminal bootstrap. It is a policy-specific value, not the optimal value Q*, and different models have different references because their actors differ.
    • Native training target y. One-step bootstrap with target networks, clipped action smoothing and the minimum of two critic heads. BCA's width is calibrated to the absolute residual between y and the twin-min critic at logged actions.
    • The earlier gamma-1 oracle. The archived 21 October pages used a 100-step undiscounted continuation on a different host. Those values are not comparable and are not shown here.

    Heads are not draws

    The host has two critic heads. BCA's calibration takes 1,000 Bayesian-bootstrap draws over a threshold. A head-count sweep is deferred and has no receipt.

    Forced probes versus chosen actions

    The decision diagnostics impose nine candidate actions at a state and ask which one each score would pick. A trained policy chooses freely. Only the policy pilots measure the second.

    Strength, not count

    κ ("kappa") multiplies the width. The trained checkpoint used 0; the pilots used 3; the one-sided pilot's frozen ranking used 1; the development grids swept 0.03 to 10 and selected nothing.

    Part 2 · The benchmark, from the canonical contract

    ThrusterChain, step by step

    Five floats sit on a chain in moving water. Four thrusters, one between each pair of floats, push on the speeds of their two neighbors. The goal is to keep every float's position and speed near zero while spending as little throttle as possible, for exactly 100 steps. Everything on this page comes from the frozen benchmark contract (ThrusterChain core v4) that the Unifloral-era experiments used; the units are dimensionless model units, one transition per step.

    Watch one episode

    Five floats sit in a row across a channel; the water flows down the screen and pushes every float downstream, below the dashed rest line, while the four thrusters between neighboring floats push back upstream. The model has one spatial axis per float, so "position" means how far downstream of rest it sits. These five episodes were exported by the canonical plant and are replayed here as saved sequences; no simulation runs in the browser. They are demonstrations of the physics, not policy results: a zero command, a fixed command that counters the current, a command past the stall knee in the stalling world, the same command in the no-stall world, and a pulsed command.

    One step, worked with the real numbers

    Pick a float and, if you like, move a command slider. The panel recomputes a single transition from the saved state with the frozen plant matrices: command → thrust (with the stall), the reward on the state before the step, and the next position and speed of the chosen float. With the saved command the result matches the exported next state exactly.

    The matrix A couples the ten float coordinates to one another (each float is a lightly damped oscillator; neighbors interact weakly), keeps the current constant and rotates the two wave coordinates by 0.27 radians per step. B puts each thruster's effective thrust, times 0.3, on the speed rows of its two neighboring floats. The water's push, 0.3·(current + wave1), lands on every speed row. The exact matrices are in contracts/dynamics_fixtures.json and are embedded in this page.

    State and observation

    The exact state has 13 float64 numbers: for each of the five floats a position p and a speed v, then the current c and two wave coordinates w1, w2. The policy and the critic do not see that exact state. They see a 14-number float32 observation: the same 13 values rounded to single precision, plus the remaining fraction of the episode (100 − t)/100, so the horizon is never hidden.

    Observation coordinates and their declared bounds (environment_contract.json). Only the current and the waves are outside the controller's power: commands cannot change them.

The benchmark declares that exact states, pushes, thrusts and stall flags are diagnostic channels only: a method that used them would be in a different information tier.

    Actions, thrust and the stall

    An action is four commands in [−1, 1], one per thruster, cast to float32 and clipped before the plant sees them. In the stalling world (the one the data were recorded in) a command past the knee at 0.8 produces less thrust, not more: thrust = clip(a, −0.8, 0.8) − 1.5·sign(a)·max(|a| − 0.8, 0), so full throttle gives 0.5. The energy cost always uses the command itself, so pushing past the knee costs more and achieves less. The no-stall world is identical up to 0.8 and linear beyond it; it is a separate configured task, and no observation bit says which world you are in.

    This is why the data's action support matters. A critic that has rarely seen commands beyond 0.8 cannot know that they achieve less; an optimistic value there is exactly the kind of error BCA is meant to penalize. Whether the recorded data actually lack such commands depends on the collection law described below.

    Reward, return and the end of the episode

    r_t = −( Σ_{j=1..10} s_j² + 0.3 · Σ_{k=1..4} a_k² ) state before the step, command after float32 clipping G_t = Σ_{k=0}^{99−t} 0.98^k · r_{t+k} the finite objective: 100 decisions, discount 0.98, no terminal value

    The hundredth transition ends the task: terminated = true, bootstrap mask 0, continuation value 0. The remaining-time coordinate makes this a proper finite-horizon problem rather than a time limit hidden from the learner. The simulator reference V used in all Unifloral-era diagnostics is exactly this objective from the queried time onward, following a frozen actor.

    Start laws and the shift families

    A reset draws the ten float coordinates from a Gaussian with spread "kick", the current uniformly from a range, the wave amplitude uniformly from a range with a uniform phase. Four laws exist. The data and every Unifloral-era pilot used the recorded law; the three shifted families are held out of data collection and have not been used in any Unifloral-era result on this machine. State-distribution shift is therefore defined but not yet tested here.

    How the data were collected, and what "support" means here

    The recording controller is a box sampler

    The recorder does not try to solve the task. At every step it draws a command from two boxes: with probability ε it draws the first coordinate a1 uniformly from (0.5, 1] (a region of volume 4), otherwise uniformly from [−1, 0.5] (volume 12); the other three coordinates are uniform on [−1, 1]. With ε = 0.25 the result is uniform over the whole cube, so the recorded data cover every command, including the stall region past 0.8. The density is known exactly, which is what the benchmark's weighted diagnostics need. The dataset used by the checkpoint and the pilots is one such recording of 1,280 episodes of 100 steps, split into 768 training episodes, 256 tuning-validation episodes (128 of which supply BCA's one-row-per-episode calibration bank), 128 selection episodes and 128 final-calibration episodes that remain unread.

    The support restriction used by the pilot

    To create a real action gap without changing the plant, the support pilot keeps only logged transitions whose executed first coordinate satisfies a1 ≤ 0.5: 57,752 of the 76,800 training rows remain, 62 to 89 per episode. The matched "broad" condition keeps the same number of rows per episode drawn from the full pool, so update counts and source-pool sizes match and only the support differs. The protocol is careful about what this is: a count-matched, action-conditioned restriction of logged transitions, not a pure action-only intervention, because retained states come from trajectories whose earlier actions could be excluded.

    Part 3 · Host algorithm first, then each BCA component from the source

    What BCA computes, line by line

    Every equation below was read from the pinned source in this checkout (the full audit with line numbers is docs/thrusterchain/site/BCA_AUDIT.md). The host is unchanged Unifloral TD3+BC. Original BCA adds an auxiliary model that predicts how large the critic's own training residual tends to be, calibrates a threshold for it with a Bayesian bootstrap, and subtracts the resulting width from the critic's value inside the actor's objective. It touches nothing else.

    The host: Unifloral TD3+BC, exactly as run

    Identity. Unifloral revision f2dc1278eae18ed3e22c92255119c54885414d17, file algorithms/td3_bc.py with sha256 6f5ab6f9…, loaded as a hash-checked slice of eight definitions and nothing else. Settings for ThrusterChain: discount 0.98, batch 256, Adam at 3·10⁻⁴, two critic updates per actor update, target smoothing noise 0.2 clipped at 0.5, Polyak step 0.005, BC weight α = 2.5.

    Networks. Two independent critic heads, each: normalized observation and action → 256 → 256 → 1 with ReLU. One deterministic actor: normalized observation → 256 → 256 → 4 → tanh. Normalization is (obs − mean)/(std + 0.001) with the training set's statistics, inside every network.

    One outer step of the host

    1. Sample one minibatch of 256 logged transitions (s, a, r, s', done).
    2. Twice on the same minibatch: smooth the target action a' = clip(π_target(s') + clip(N(0, 0.2²), ±0.5), ±1); form y = r + 0.98·(1 − done)·min(Q1_target, Q2_target)(s', a'); take one Adam step on mean over batch and over both heads of (Q_h(s, a) − y)².
    3. Once: the actor's action a_π = π(s); the first head's value q = Q1(s, a_π); the detached normalizer λ = 2.5 / (mean|q| + 10⁻⁷); the behavior-cloning term BC = mean over the 4 coordinates of (a_π − a)²; one Adam step on L_actor = −λ·mean(q) + mean(BC).
    4. Polyak-average the actor target and the critic targets with step 0.005.

    Source: td3_bc.py lines 174–279 (train step), 65–114 (networks), 38–46 (settings). The actor sees head 1 only; the target uses the minimum of the two heads. done is 1 on the hundredth transition, so nothing is bootstrapped past the horizon.

    The BCA addition in one picture

    Five components, in the order data flow. The first four run on every critic update or at a refresh; only the fifth changes what the actor learns.

    1 · Residual and unit

    In: minibatch, current and target critics.
    Out: residual y − q per row; a running scale u.
    Purpose: define the error the width is about.

    2 · Local scale network η(s, a)

    In: observation, action.
    Out: a positive number per row.
    Purpose: predict where residuals are large, in units of u.

    3 · Calibration threshold R

    In: 128 held-out rows, frozen η and u.
    Out: one number R, or a refusal.
    Purpose: scale η so the band covers 90% of held-out residuals with 95% posterior credibility.

    4 · Width U and the frozen reference

    In: R, u, η frozen at the last refresh; a live action.
    Out: U(s, a) = R·u·max(η(s, a), 10⁻⁶).
    Purpose: the quantity the actor subtracts.

    5 · Actor-side penalty

    In: Q1, U, κ.
    Out: the actor objective with Q1 − κU.
    Purpose: steer the actor away from actions whose value is uncertain. Critic loss and target are untouched.

    Component 1: the residual and its unit

    y = stop_gradient( r + (1 − done)·0.98·min_h Q_h,target(s', a') ), a' = clip(π_target(s') + clipped noise) q = stop_gradient( min_h Q_h(s, a_logged) ) current critic, twin minimum, LOGGED action residual = y − q u ← 0.99·u + 0.01·max( std_batch(y − q), 10⁻⁶ ) starts at 1.0; updated after every accepted fit

    This is the host's own temporal-difference error, measured at the logged action with the twin minimum on both sides and one fresh smoothing draw per row. Three things it is not: it is not the error of Q1 against the simulator reference; it carries no sign once the absolute value is taken in the next steps; and it is measured at logged actions, while the penalty will be read at the actor's actions. The pipeline validation makes the gap concrete with a fixture in which Q1 = 10, Q2 = 0, reward 0 and target 0: the twin-min residual is 0, so the width is 0, while Q1 is wrong by 10.

    Source: uncertainty_source/historical/td3_bc_bca.py lines 146–156 (target), 170–174 (q), 196–198 (unit); pipeline check 17.

    Component 2: the local scale network and its fit

    η(s, a) is a small network: normalized observation and action → 64 → 64 → 1 with ReLU, then η = softplus(·) + 0.01, so it is always above 0.01. It is fitted once per critic update on the same 256-row minibatch, after that critic update, with Adam at 10⁻³.

    1. Compute y and q as in component 1 (both detached).
    2. Draw Bayesian-bootstrap weights: E_i ~ Exp(1), p_i = E_i / Σ_j E_j, one draw per fit.
    3. Soft two-sided coverage per row: c_i = σ(20·(y_i/u − (q_i/u − η_i))) · σ(20·((q_i/u + η_i) − y_i/u)).
    4. Loss: (Σ_i p_i c_i − 0.9)² + 0.005·Σ_i p_i η_i², so the band aims at 90% coverage and is otherwise as narrow as possible.
    5. One Adam step; then the unit update of component 1. Accept only if every input, the loss, the gradient, η > 0 and u > 0 are finite; otherwise keep the previous state.

    Source: calibration/network.py lines 8–46; td3_bc_bca.py lines 159–231; training.py (order: microstep, then fit on the same batch). The checkpoint made 20,000 such fits.

    Component 3: the calibration threshold, a Bayesian bootstrap over 128 held-out rows

    At critic updates 10,000, 15,000 and 20,000 (and at 21,000 in the continuation pilot) the scale network and the unit are frozen and a threshold is selected on the held-out bank: one logged row from each of 128 episodes that never train.

    1. For each held-out row: a fresh target y_j, the twin-min q_j, the frozen η_j; score S_j = |y_j − q_j| / (max(η_j, 10⁻⁶)·u), in float64.
    2. λ̂ = the smallest sorted score at which the cumulative count reaches 90% of the 128 (an exact-rational empirical quantile).
    3. For each of 1,000 draws: weights E_1 … E_128 plus one extra E_129 for the unseen test point, all Exp(1); the draw's crossing is the smallest score at which the cumulative weighted mass reaches 90% of the total including the test atom, or +∞ if the test atom alone exceeds 10%.
    4. λ_hpd = the 950th smallest crossing (95% credibility).
    5. R = max(λ̂, λ_hpd); n_eff = 128 with unit weights. Store R, λ̂, λ_hpd, u and the frozen η parameters as the reference, with ready = true.

    Meanings: α = 0.1 is the target miscoverage, β = 0.95 the posterior credibility, and the 1,000 are draws over the threshold, each a reweighting of the same 128 scores. They are not critic heads, not model samples and not samples of Q. With unit weights this is the exchangeable case of weighted Bayesian conformal prediction (BQ-CP). The code accepts importance weights, but no caller in the current work passes any, so importance-weighted BCA remains a proposal. If fewer than 950 draws cross, R is +∞ and the refresh is refused; the trainers never commit a non-finite reference.

    What the threshold promises. With posterior probability 0.95, at least 90% of exchangeable held-out one-step twin-min residuals at logged actions fall within R·u·η. It promises nothing about Q1's error against the simulator, about the actor's own actions (which are not logged actions), about starts outside the recorded law, or about return.

    Source: calibration/reference.py lines 17–20, 124–194; calibration/wbcp.py lines 43–117; td3_bc_bca.py lines 234–280. Pipeline check 4 on a test snapshot recorded R = 4.8746, λ̂ = 4.5344, λ_hpd = 4.8746; the trained checkpoint's own values at update 20,000 live in the runtime assets and are not shown here.

    Component 4: the width and the frozen reference

    U(s, a) = R · u · max( η_frozen(s, a), 10⁻⁶ ) parameters R, u, η frozen at the last refresh; the action a stays live not ready (before the first refresh): U = 0 and "usable" = false unusable or non-finite in the continuation adapter: the step is refused, never trained with a silent zero

    The actor always consumes the reference frozen at the last refresh, not the live scale network, so a refresh changes what the next actor step sees. Because the parameters are frozen but the action is not, dU/da reaches the actor through η: this is what makes the penalty action-specific. Pipeline check 15 measured the gradient through the frozen reference at exactly 0 and the same gradient with the freeze removed at 1.76.

    Source: historical/direct_extension.py lines 47–70; continuation.py lines 72–77 and 247–249.

    Component 5: the actor objective with the penalty

    L_actor,BCA = −λ · mean_B( Q1(s, a_π) − κ · U(s, a_π) ) + mean_B(BC) = L_actor,host + λ · κ · mean_B U(s, a_π) λ computed from the UNPENALIZED Q1 and detached; κ = 0 reproduces the host bit for bit (check 7) critic loss, target y, data, BC term: unchanged

    Only the actor's gradient changes. The critic parameters are constants inside the actor loss, but dQ1/da and dη/da are live, so the actor is pushed toward actions with higher value and smaller width. Checks 8 to 14 confirm the identity grad(BCA) = grad(host) + λκ·grad(mean U) for κ in {0, 0.03, 0.1, 0.3, 1, 3, 10}.

    What calibration does not buy

    • A calibrated band on the training residual is not a bound on Q1's true error at the actor's action. The operator audit measured how far apart they are.
    • An absolute band has no sign. It cannot say whether an action is over- or underestimated; it can only make every value smaller.
    • A width that is constant across a state's actions has zero action gradient and cannot move the actor or change a ranking. Only the contrast between actions matters for decisions.
    • Exchangeability holds for held-out logged rows under the recorded start law. The actor's own actions, and any shifted start law, are outside that guarantee.

    The development banks: forced probes at saved states

    • States. 64 recorded-family episodes per batch, one query each at time 0, 25, 50 or 75 (16 per time), reached by the frozen actor's own prefix. Batch 1 seeds 2026102400–2026102463; batch 2 seeds 2026102700–2026102763; confirmation seeds 2026102500–2026102563 untouched.
    • Candidates. Nine per state: the frozen actor's action, the zero command and seven seeded uniform commands, deduplicated after float32 deployment. Every selector sees the same bank.
    • Reference V. The candidate once, then the same frozen actor to the end of the 100-step horizon, discount 0.98, exact float64 state, no terminal bootstrap. Deterministic, so no sampling error; the same frozen actor continues every candidate, so V is this checkpoint's own-policy value.
    • Sealed order. All scores and selections are hashed before any continuation return is computed.
    • Endpoints. Paired first-action gain in V over raw Q1 (primary); candidate-bank regret, gain over the actor and improved/harmed counts (secondary). Intervals: 2,000 bootstrap draws over episodes, stratified by query time, pointwise 95%, not corrected for the 31 selectors.
    • Controls. Batch 1 used RMS-matched disagreement and actor-distance penalties; batch 2 used contrast-matched factors fixed from the 256-row fitting bank (BCA 31.37, disagreement 4.93, actor distance 19.30). Strength grid 0, 0.03, 0.1, 0.3, 1, 3, 10 for every family.
    • Budget actually used per batch. 576 continuations, 36,000 transitions, zero optimizer or calibration updates.

    Why "inconclusive" is a possible verdict. If raw Q1 already picks the best candidate at a state, no score can improve it there. Regret of raw Q1 is therefore reported first: a bank without raw mistakes cannot test correction ability. In both batches raw Q1 had mean regret above one return unit and made a wrong pick in most states, so opportunities existed.

    The operator-alignment audit

    One evaluator step per saved candidate (1,152 transitions), four prespecified smoothing draws, and the algebraic decomposition of the signed error against the saved reference:

    y_j = r + 0.98·(1 − done)·min(Q1_target, Q2_target)(s', a'_j) four draws j R_j = min(Q1, Q2) − y_j signed native residual (U is calibrated to |R|) H = Q1 − min(Q1, Q2) critic-head gap G_j = y_j − V target-versus-continuation gap E = Q1 − V = R_j + H + G_j signed Q1 error against the simulator reference

    The reported correlations are Pearson within each state's nine candidates, averaged over states, then over draws. That is the decision-relevant question: can the width tell the actions at one state apart? Containment |R| ≤ U is reported separately because it can be high while discrimination is poor.

    The fresh-initialization support pilot

    This is the experiment designed to address the pilot's two limitations at once: independent initializations and an explicit action gap. Approved scope, frozen on 27 October 03:08 UTC:

    • Conditions. Restricted: keep logged transitions with executed a1 ≤ 0.5 (57,752 of 76,800 rows). Broad, count matched: the same number of intact rows per episode from the full pool (14,269 of them above 0.5). Identical row plans across seeds.
    • Seeds. Fresh initializations 2026103001 and 2026103002, paired across conditions; auxiliary 2026103011 and 2026103012. Normalization statistics fitted per condition on its retained training observations and frozen.
    • Shared warmup. 10,000 critic, 5,000 actor, 10,000 auxiliary updates per cell, first calibration at 10,000.
    • Branches. From each warmup: native; BCA κ 3; distance control with coefficient c = G_B / G_D, the ratio of RMS action-gradient norms of 3U and D on 768 logged probes, computed once before branching. Each branch: 2,000 critic, 1,000 actor, 2,000 auxiliary updates, one calibration at 11,000.
    • Evaluation. Twelve final policies plus four untrained reference actors on the same 32 nominal starts; record the fraction of chosen actions above 0.5 + 10⁻⁶ (actual extrapolation exposure).
    • Gates. Learning: native must beat its untrained reference in all four cells, else "inadequate learning for this budget". Go: BCA beats native and distance in all eight comparisons. A restricted-cell win with no baseline visiting the excluded region is labeled "mechanism untested".
    • Budget. 64,000 critic, 32,000 actor, 64,000 auxiliary updates, 16 calibrations, 512 episodes, 150 minutes.

    What happened on Zeus. Attempt 1 was stopped by the user during the first warmup (last saved 200 critic updates). Attempt 2 completed the two seed-0 warmups (10,000 critic updates each, sealed) and was canceled during the third; no branch trained and no evaluation ran. A Titan continuation importing the two sealed warmups was proposed with a 7,594-second ceiling. The user reports a completed Titan run whose learning gate passed and whose BCA gate failed, with restricted policies never selecting beyond the cutoff; that report is unavailable here and is shown as such.

    Fairness and validity rules used throughout

    • Benchmark conditions come from environment mechanisms (the stall knee, the a1 cutoff, the start laws), never from BCA's performance.
    • Every arm receives the same data rows, the same optimizer budgets, the same auxiliary work and the same evaluation seeds. Controls are strength matched by a rule fixed before labels.
    • A bank without raw-Q mistakes is inconclusive for correction ability; a bank with mistakes that the penalty fails to fix is informative negative evidence. Regret of raw Q1 is always reported.
    • Development banks are reused and known; nothing on them is confirmation. Confirmation seeds remain untouched.
    • Calibration of residuals is never read as a guarantee about true Q error, selected actions, shifted starts or return.

    Does the width point the right way?

    The mechanism audit asked a sharper question on the same banks. Among states where raw Q1 picked a worse candidate, it compared the width at the raw pick with the width at the better candidate. In batch 1 the width supported the better candidate in 11 of 46 such states and opposed it in 35; in batch 2 it supported it in 12 of 44 and opposed it in 32. Over both batches, the width supported the better candidate in 23 of 90 wrong-pick states and opposed it in 67; its pairwise sign agreement with the signed-error contrast was +0.4412, below the 0.5 of chance. The width does correlate with how bad a candidate is within a state (Pearson +0.5435 with candidate-bank regret), yet negatively with the signed error (-0.1613): it tends to be large on actions that are poor and underestimated, not on the overestimated ones a correction must lower. A penalty that more often sits on the better action cannot correct the raw pick by subtraction.

    Verdict on claim 4. At the trained strength the penalty changes few choices, improves slightly more than it harms, and reaches a regret no better than a cheap actor-distance control at strength 0.1 on batch 2. The user's Titan held-out evaluation (64 banks, three changed choices, one better and two worse) points the same way and is listed as unavailable here. Decision advantage is not established.

    Predictive association is real but small, and it did not reach decisions

    Two saved-bank diagnostics separate "the width knows something" from "the width helps choose". Grouped eight-fold prediction of the Q1-minus-V discrepancy: adding the training-residualized width lowered held-out squared error by +0.1183 (base 3.205), while a within-episode permuted width changed it by -0.0014. Using those out-of-fold corrected scores to choose candidates, the width-informed score beat the width-free one by +0.1068 with the two scores disagreeing in 15 episodes, split 8 / 7 wins to losses. The interval includes zero.

    The scale audit explains the size of the effect: on the 256-row fitting bank the width's level is about 5.083 Q-units, but its variation across a state's candidates is only 0.150, against 4.697 for Q1 itself. At κ = 1 the penalty's actor-gradient norm is 0.0406 of Q1's. A penalty that barely varies across actions acts mostly as a level shift.

    Containment is high: 98.2% of development candidates have |R| ≤ U. That is what calibration promises, and it coexists with near-zero within-state association between the width and the signed error against the simulator. Pooled correlations across states are positive (0.22 on fitting rows), which is why earlier "incremental information" findings and this negative decision picture do not contradict each other: the width separates states by difficulty better than it separates actions at one state.

    The independent-seed, support-gap test

    On this machine the test did not complete. Attempt 1 was interrupted at a lower bound of 200 critic updates. Attempt 2 completed two of four warmups (10,000 critic updates each, sealed) and was stopped at the third; no BCA branch trained and nothing was evaluated. The Titan continuation is a proposal.     Failed, negative and inconclusive findings, kept

    • One-sided BCA (Zeus, 26 Oct): refitted the scale network to a one-sided event on 128 updates and recalibrated (threshold 1.6866). Over all 128 states it gained +0.0629 over Q1 (7 / 4 helpful to harmful switches), beat original BCA at κ 1 by +0.1442 in batch 1 but lost by -0.0164 in batch 2; the exploratory screen failed and it was not promoted.     • Width direction (Titan, 26 Oct): the width more often opposed than supported the better candidate where raw Q1 was wrong.
    • Operator alignment (Zeus, 26 Oct): within-state correlation of the width with its own residual near 0.07; of the residual with signed error near 0.01.

    • Held-out evaluation (Titan, reported): raw Q wrong in 25 of 64 banks; BCA changed three choices, one better, two worse; penalty contrast favored the better action in 9 of the 25 and the wrong way in 16. Descriptive, not a tuning set. Unavailable here.

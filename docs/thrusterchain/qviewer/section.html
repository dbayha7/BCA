The critic in action

weighting test data This section uses the data and trained runs of the weighting test, not the recorded data described in the sections above, and three things differ. Its recording controller never pushes past 80%: it drives normally with every throttle below 80%, and in hard water, the states where holding the floats against the water would ask thruster 1 for more than 65% of full throttle, a coin flip in each state chooses the 80% hold, thruster 1 at exactly 80% upstream. (The controller of the section The recording controller gives full throttle when the water pushes hard.) This section's removed ball therefore takes in the 80% hold; it is not the ball of the section The distribution shift. And its points run from uniformly random commands (0 points) to the stall-aware controller (100 points), while elsewhere on this page 0 points is the recording controller.

The learner of the weighting test is TD3+BC, short for twin delayed deep deterministic policy gradient plus behaviour cloning. It learns from the recorded data only and ends with two networks. The actor picks the four throttles in each state. The critic scores commands: the actor climbs the critic's score while it learns, and BCA (Bayesian Conformal Aggregation) puts its band around that same score. This section shows the critic at work: it replays one real episode of a trained actor and, at every state, asks the critic how good each throttle of thruster 1 would be, next to the exact answer from simulation.

The critic's value, Q(s, a)

The critic's estimate of the return if the actor gives command a in state s and then keeps driving as it does. The return is the sum of all the rewards that follow, each step discounted by a factor of 0.98. The critic is two networks, and Q(s, a) is the smaller of their two estimates, min(Q1, Q2), the value BCA and this section use; TD3+BC takes the same kind of minimum when it sets the critic's training targets. Every reward is a cost, so every value is negative; higher is better.

The true value, Qπ(s, a)

The same quantity, computed exactly. The physics has no randomness, so the simulator gives command a in state s once, lets the actor π drive from there, and adds up the discounted rewards. It stops after 600 steps; running 1,000 steps instead changes no value by more than of itself.

Why the 80% limit matters

Past 80% throttle a propeller stalls: more throttle gives less push (0.8 at 80%, 0.5 at 100%) and costs more. These data barely show this. The weighting test's recording controller never pushes past 80%, so the only recorded commands past 80% are random ones, and the expert data have none. The critic trained with "the 80% hold removed" has also almost never seen the 80% hold, the best move when the water pushes hard. Where a critic rates a stalled throttle highly, the actor, which climbs the critic, is pulled toward the stall.

How to use it: pick a dataset quality, the critic's data and the situation, then press Play or step through the episode's 100 states.

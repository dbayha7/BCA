Does the width point the right way?

The mechanism audit asked a sharper question on the same banks. Among states where raw Q1 picked a worse candidate, it compared the width at the raw pick with the width at the better candidate. In batch 1 the width supported the better candidate in of such states and opposed it in ; in batch 2 it supported it in of and opposed it in . Over both batches, the width supported the better candidate in of wrong-pick states and opposed it in ; its pairwise sign agreement with the signed-error contrast was , below the 0.5 of chance. The width does correlate with how bad a candidate is within a state (Pearson with candidate-bank regret), yet negatively with the signed error (): it tends to be large on actions that are poor and underestimated, not on the overestimated ones a correction must lower. A penalty that more often sits on the better action cannot correct the raw pick by subtraction.

Verdict on claim 4. At the trained strength the penalty changes few choices, improves slightly more than it harms, and reaches a regret no better than a cheap actor-distance control at strength 0.1 on batch 2. The user's Titan held-out evaluation (64 banks, three changed choices, one better and two worse) points the same way and is listed as unavailable here. Decision advantage is not established.

Predictive association is real but small, and it did not reach decisions

Two saved-bank diagnostics separate "the width knows something" from "the width helps choose". Grouped eight-fold prediction of the Q1-minus-V discrepancy: adding the training-residualized width lowered held-out squared error by (base ), while a within-episode permuted width changed it by . Using those out-of-fold corrected scores to choose candidates, the width-informed score beat the width-free one by with the two scores disagreeing in episodes, split wins to losses. The interval includes zero.

The scale audit explains the size of the effect: on the 256-row fitting bank the width's level is about Q-units, but its variation across a state's candidates is only , against for Q1 itself. At κ = 1 the penalty's actor-gradient norm is of Q1's. A penalty that barely varies across actions acts mostly as a level shift.

Containment is high: of development candidates have |R| ≤ U. That is what calibration promises, and it coexists with near-zero within-state association between the width and the signed error against the simulator. Pooled correlations across states are positive (0.22 on fitting rows), which is why earlier "incremental information" findings and this negative decision picture do not contradict each other: the width separates states by difficulty better than it separates actions at one state.

The independent-seed, support-gap test

On this machine the test did not complete. Attempt 1 was interrupted at a lower bound of critic updates. Attempt 2 completed two of four warmups ( critic updates each, sealed) and was stopped at the third; no BCA branch trained and nothing was evaluated. The Titan continuation is a proposal. The user's report of a completed Titan run says the learning gate passed, BCA's advancement gate failed, and restricted-data policies never selected actions beyond the cutoff, so the extrapolation mechanism was not activated. By the protocol's own rule that outcome is a failed advancement screen with an untested mechanism, not evidence that the gap was exploited and missed. It is shown as unavailable here until its receipt is present.

Failed, negative and inconclusive findings, kept

  • One-sided BCA (Zeus, 26 Oct): refitted the scale network to a one-sided event on 128 updates and recalibrated (threshold ). Over all 128 states it gained over Q1 ( helpful to harmful switches), beat original BCA at κ 1 by in batch 1 but lost by in batch 2; the exploratory screen failed and it was not promoted. Original BCA at κ 1 itself moved the mean by on this bank.
  • Width direction (Titan, 26 Oct): the width more often opposed than supported the better candidate where raw Q1 was wrong.
  • Operator alignment (Zeus, 26 Oct): within-state correlation of the width with its own residual near 0.07; of the residual with signed error near 0.01.
  • Adaptive signed signal (Titan, reported): 48 probes across 16 development states, all overestimated; the signal abstained everywhere; no held-out states. Did not establish adaptivity or underestimation detection. Unavailable here.
  • Held-out evaluation (Titan, reported): raw Q wrong in 25 of 64 banks; BCA changed three choices, one better, two worse; penalty contrast favored the better action in 9 of the 25 and the wrong way in 16. Descriptive, not a tuning set. Unavailable here.
  • Earlier locked confirmation (Titan): inconclusive against actor distance; retained on Titan, not included in the handoff.

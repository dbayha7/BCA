## 1. What the module does, in plain words

The **harm switch** (module M10) decides, per seed and world, whether the critic promises too much at full throttle,
and if so by how much: its **height** h, in critic value units. Module M11 turns that height into caution during actor
training. It is a **penalty on throttle**: zero up to where the recorded throttles stop (**t_lo**, the gap rule's lower
end), rising in a straight line across the empty stretch of throttle to the height h at **t_up** (where the survivors,
the few full-throttle commands kept in training, begin), and flat beyond. The **throttle index** of a command is the
largest absolute thruster command, t(a) = max_k |a_k|.

    ramp(t) = clip((t - t_lo) / (t_up - t_lo), 0, 1)
    U(a)    = h * ramp(t(a))

During the actor-only phase (20,000 steps against a frozen critic, from the cloned actor), each training row charges
the actor k · [U(π(s)) − U(a_rec)]_+ : the actor pays only for going *further up the ramp than the recorded command*
of that row did. With the switch off, h = 0, and the penalty and its gradient are exactly zero, so the actor is the
no-caution actor. k is the **dose**: 1 for BCA's arm, 2 for a readout arm. There is no error-size network, no unit and
no weight in M11: the ramp reads the command's throttle, and nothing else.

**The settings and arms** (the rival heights, all with the same ramp ends): BCA's switched ramp (`obca_k1`); the same
at k = 2 (`obca_k2`); the always-on ramp (`obca_forced`, BCA's stalling-world height in both worlds, the world-blind
rival); the fixed height 1.0 with BCA's switch (`fixed_gated`); the plain conformal gap, the 90% quantile of the
full-throttle overestimates minus that of the rest, on when above 0.10 (`conformal_k1`); the mean gap, on when a rank
test says p < 0.05 (`meangap_k1`); the near-80 run adds the mean gap on when above 0.10 (`meandiff_k1`) and, in the
near-80 setting, the ramp started at the stall, 80% (`knee_ramp`). Three data settings: **main** (the densest 15% of
commands removed; the ramp starts at about 0.79, which is also where the stall begins), **near-80** (also every
throttle from 70% to 90% removed; the ramp starts at 0.70, ten points below the stall) and **random-rows** (as many
random rows removed instead of the band; BCA's arm alone).

**Points**: 0 is the recording controller and 100 the world's best scripted controller, in strong current unless said
otherwise. **Net across worlds**: the gain over no caution in the stalling world minus the loss in the no-stall world.

## 2. What "works as intended" means (checkable statements)

- **C1, plumbing.** An arm with h = 0 must equal the no-caution actor on every parameter within 1e-5 (the recorded
  checks `b_w0` and `h0_reproduces_reference`; `near80_constants.W0_TOL`). Equivalent: the penalty and its gradient are
  exactly 0 at h = 0.
- **C2, the charge.** The penalty is 0 wherever the actor's throttle is at or below the recorded command's, never
  negative, never above k·h, exactly linear in h and in k, and its gradient is 0 outside the ramp (t ≤ t_lo or
  t ≥ t_up).
- **C3, the table.** Each arm's switch, height and k are read from the switch records with no refit, and a switched-on
  arm with an infinite height is refused (the actor phase of that seed-world stops).
- **C4, the size.** On recorded rows, the no-caution actor would pay a sizable penalty under BCA's ramp (it climbs past
  t_lo in 10-17% of states), the trained ramp actor pays almost none (it learned to stay below t_lo), and in the
  no-stall worlds the penalty is exactly 0.
- **C5, the outcome.** The switched ramp beats the best world-blind rival by at least 10 points of net across worlds
  (step 2's G1; near-80 claim 1 and claim 4), keeps its gain in the other situations (G2) and beats the matched even pull
  (G3). *Calibration*: a calibrated height would beat both a smaller and a larger height; the record never showed this
  (step 2's attribution), and the test below reads what the dose arms say about it.

**S5, three toys with the truth known (C5, what an outcome can and cannot show about the height).** One state; a
throttle m on a grid of 0.005; the frozen critic *promises* q(m); the *true* value equals q(m) up to the knee (0.80)
and falls by d past it (the stall); the recorded command sits below t_lo, so U(a_rec) = 0 and the actor picks

| toy | critic's promise | ramp | what happens as h grows | lesson |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) main-setting pattern | straight line q(m) = m | 0.80 to 1.00 (starts at the knee) | h < 0.2: full throttle (harm); h ≥ 0.2: the pick is 0.80, the true best, for every larger h | an outcome can only show "large enough": every height above the critic's over-optimism along the ramp (0.2) gives the same choice |
| (b) near-80 pattern | straight line | 0.70 to 1.00 (starts 0.10 below the knee) | h < 0.3: full throttle (harm); h ≥ 0.3: the pick is 0.70 (true value 0.70, the true best is 0.80 at the knee) | the ramp charges by throttle, not by harm: when it starts below the harm, every height that removes the harm also gives up the useful 0.70-0.80 stretch, and no height recovers it |
| (c) curved critic | q(m) = 1 − (1 − m)², flattening toward full throttle | 0.70 to 1.00 | the pick falls smoothly, m = 1 − h/0.6, reaching the knee at h* = 0.12 exactly (the critic's slope at the knee, 0.4, times the ramp's length, 0.3) and t_lo at h = 0.18; true value 0.50 (h = 0), 0.478 (0.09), **0.960 (0.12)**, 0.938 (0.15), 0.910 (h ≥ 0.18) | this is the shape under which "a calibrated height beats both a smaller and a larger one" can be seen; the best height is a property of the critic's curvature, not of the harm's size |

All five S5 checks PASS. The toys say what to look for in the records: a peak in points against height (toy c) would be
evidence of calibration; a plateau (toys a and b) can only say "large enough".

## 8. What to say to the advisor

1. **Module by module, the ramp does what the code says.** The penalty is zero below the data's edge, rises in a
   straight line to BCA's height, is charged only for climbing past the recorded command, and vanishes exactly when
   the switch is off: 104 recorded and 70 recomputed height-0 checks are zero to the last bit, and in all 12 no-stall
   seed-worlds the switched arm is the no-caution actor itself.
2. **The penalty's values are reasonable and consistent.** The critic's preference for full throttle is worth 53-60%
   of states; a ramp of 1.4-6 value units (about 10-40% of |Q1|) at full throttle removes it in 92-96% of them. The
   no-caution actor would pay 0.1-0.5 units on average; the trained actor pays 0.001-0.003, because it learned to stay
   below the ramp's start. No seed-world departs from this pattern.
3. **The outcome, where the switch is on, is a plateau, not a peak.** In the main setting every rival with a smaller
   height did worse and twice BCA's height did slightly better (6 of 7 seeds, +2.7 points); in the near-80 setting
   twice the height did slightly worse (4 of 5, −1.9) and a smaller height won twice. So the records show BCA's height
   is large enough, and they cannot show it is calibrated. (The toys say why: with the ramp starting at the harm, any
   height above the critic's over-optimism gives the same actor; a peak needs a curved critic and a ramp that starts
   below the harm, which is the near-80 case, where the curve is indeed flatter and turns down.)
4. **The exceptional case is the height, not the ramp.** The switch is right in 40 of 40 seed-worlds (M10), but its
   height is a difference of two thresholds that is +infinity when the full-throttle group has fewer than about 29
   rows; that happened in 3 of 5 fresh seeds (18-22 rows), the ramp refused them, and the pre-registered claim 4
   failed by rule. A finite fallback height (for example the plain 90%-quantile gap, which did nearly as well on seeds
   0-4) would have let those seeds run; whether that is allowed is a design decision, not a bug.
5. **On the weighting question:** M11 carries no importance weights at all (the switch's posteriors use weights of 1),
   so "IID versus IW" does not arise here; the case to debug is the small-group refusal, and the way to reproduce it
   is `M11_test.py` section R1 (the three stops rebuilt from the switch records in under a second).

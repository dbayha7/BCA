## 1. What the module does, in plain words

The host is TD3+BC, an offline reinforcement learning method. It trains a **critic**, a network that estimates how
much return a command earns from a state. The critic has two heads, Q1 and Q2, and the host keeps slowly trailing
copies of them, the **target copies** Q̄1 and Q̄2 (and a trailing copy π̄ of the actor, the **target actor**). At every
training step the host moves the critic toward a **target** y: the recorded reward plus the discounted value that the
target copies give the next state under the target actor's command, with a little clipped noise on that command. This
is the standard TD3 recipe.

The **residual** is the difference between that target and the critic's own value at the recorded command:

    e = y − q,    y = r + 0.98 · (1 − d) · min(Q̄1, Q̄2)(s', a'),    a' = clip(π̄(s') + ε, −1, 1),    q = min(Q1, Q2)(s, a).

Here (s, a, r, s', d) is one **recorded row** (state, command, reward, next state, episode-end flag; d is always 0 in
ThrusterChain, the project's simulated boat), 0.98 is the discount γ, and ε is Gaussian noise with standard deviation
0.2 clipped at ±0.5, drawn once per row and per thruster. The residual is in return units, the same units as q.

BCA uses the residual in two places. During training, at every update, BCA computes it on the host's 256-row training
batch and feeds it to the error-size network and the running unit (module M2). At a **refresh**, BCA computes it once
on every **held-out row** (recorded rows kept out of training for calibration; 805 to 832 rows per band in the near-80
run) and turns it into the scores (M3) that give the threshold (M5) and the band (M6). The residual is never stored; the
scores computed from it carry a fingerprint (`bank_scores_sha256`) in every band record.

**Frozen-critic variant.** In Stage 2 and in the near-80 run's "version 6" arm, BCA's band is fitted after training on a
frozen critic that was trained with a fixed scripted controller (the **stall-aware controller**). There the "target
actor" is that controller, and the saved critic serves as its own target copy (`band.py:214-222`). Everything else is
the same formula.

## 2. What "works as intended" means (checkable statements)

- **C1, the formula.** For any networks and any rows, y equals the written formula, to float32 rounding; rows with d = 1
  give y = r exactly; noise 0 gives the noise-free formula; y reads the target copies and q reads the online critic.
- **C2, the host's own target.** Under the same random key, BCA's y is the very target the host's critic update uses:
  the host's critic loss (`td3_bc.py:106-121`) equals the loss built from BCA's y, and differs under another key.
- **C3, reproduction from records.** Recomputing e at the recorded held-out rows from the saved critic, with the
  refresh's own key, and carrying it downstream reproduces the stored scores byte for byte and the stored threshold R,
  in every recorded band.
- **C4, meaning, where the truth is known.** On a world whose exact value is known, an exact critic gives e = 0; a
  critic too high by b at every state and command gives e = −(1 − γ)·b = −0.02·b; a critic too high by b only at the
  recorded rows gives e = −b; too high by b only at the next step's rows gives e = +0.98·b. (This is arithmetic, and it
  is the part the advisor should hear: the residual sees an error only where it changes from a row to its next step.)

## 4. Synthetic test: random networks and a world with a known value

`M1_test.py` Part S calls the repository's own `native_target` and `td3_bc_update` (the real functions, not a copy) on
the host's own network classes at random initialization and on 512 random rows in ThrusterChain's shapes. The online
networks come from seed 0 and the target copies from seed 1, so that reading the wrong copy would show. Printed output
quoted; Python 3.14.2, NumPy 2.5.4, JAX 0.12.0 on Windows (CPU).

**S1, the formula (C1).** Largest |y − formula| over 512 rows: 1.1e-7 (|y| up to 3.1; float32 rounding). The 53 rows
with d = 1 give y = r exactly (gap 0.0). With noise 0 the gap to the noise-free formula is 1.2e-7. Had y read the online
critic instead of the target copy, the gap would have been 0.268. q equals the smaller of the two heads in 512 of 512
rows (the heads differ by up to 0.357; online and target critics differ by up to 0.342 at the same rows). PASS.

**S2, the host's own target (C2).** The host's critic loss from `td3_bc_update`: 1.864258. The loss built from BCA's y
under the same key: 1.864258 (relative gap 1.8e-8). Under another noise key the host's loss is 1.865237 (relative gap
5.2e-4), so the equality is not trivial. PASS.

**S3, the noise law.** 100,000 draws: standard deviation of the clipped noise 0.1979 (0.2 before clipping, 0.197
expected after), largest |ε| 0.500, share clipped 1.26% (1.24% expected). A second key gives a second draw of the same
law: SD(y − y with noise 0) = 0.0095, SD(y at key A − y at key B) = 0.0140, about √2 times the first, as for
independent draws. PASS.

**S4, a world with a known value (C4).** 4,096 one-number states s in [−1, 1], self-loop transitions (s' = s), reward
r(s) = s, target actor 0, recorded commands in [0.8, 1]. The exact value of any policy is s / (1 − 0.98) at every
command. Pushed through the real `native_target` with that value as the critic:

| Critic | Residual y − q, mean over rows (expected) | b = −1 | b = −0.5 | b = +0.5 | b = +1 | b = +2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| exact | largest \|e\| 3.8e-6 (0) | | | | | |
| too high by b at every state and command | −0.02 b | +0.0200 | +0.0100 | −0.0100 | −0.0200 | −0.0400 |
| too high by b only at the recorded rows | −b | +1.0000 | +0.5000 | −0.5000 | −1.0000 | −2.0000 |
| too high by b only at the next step's rows | +0.98 b | −0.9800 | −0.4900 | +0.4900 | +0.9800 | +1.9600 |

Within each case the residual's spread across rows is at most 1.3e-6. PASS on all four. Reading: a critic that is too
high by 1.0 return units everywhere shows a residual of only −0.02; the residual reports an error only where it changes
between a row and its next step. This is the known limit of one-step residuals, not a defect of the code; it matters for
how far the band (M6) can be read as "the critic's error".

## 8. What to say to the advisor

1. Module 1 is the residual: the critic's own one-step training error at a recorded row, y − q, with y the exact target
   TD3+BC trains its critic toward. We checked it three ways: against the written formula on random networks (gap
   1e-7), against the host's own loss under the same random key (equal to every digit, synthetic and real), and by
   recomputing it from the saved critics of all ten near-80 bands, where it reproduces BCA's stored scores byte for
   byte and the stored threshold.
2. On the real held-out rows the residual is small and well behaved: median 0.10 return units against values of −8 to
   −54, 92% of rows inside the band in every seed and world, the critic slightly below its own target on average.
   Nothing in the ten bands is exceptional; the recomputation was identical in 10 of 10.
3. The one thing to keep in mind downstream: the residual measures the critic's consistency with itself, not its error
   against the truth. An error shared by a row and its next step is shrunk by 1 − γ = 0.02. The records that measured
   the truth separately found the critic 2.3 times as wrong where data are missing; M1 cannot see that part, and
   modules M2-M6 inherit the limit. The consistent pattern M1 does see, in every band, is that large residuals are
   twice as common at high water current as at low current, which is what the weighting (M4) builds on.
4. During training BCA's residual and the host's update use different noise draws at the same step (same formula, same
   networks, keys differing by design); under one key they are identical. The map's sentence "y equals the target the
   host trains on" is exact in that sense.

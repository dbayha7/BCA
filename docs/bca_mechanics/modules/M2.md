# M2. The error-size network η(s, a) and the unit u

**Verdict: works with a caveat** (mechanics: works, on synthetic rows and on all 20 recorded ThrusterChain bands;
the caveat is in what the network's output means: on ThrusterChain it is nearly one constant, and its link to the
critic's true error is weak).

**The one sentence for the advisor.** *The error-size network runs cleanly (every one of 400,000 recorded fit steps
accepted, always positive, the unit within 5% of the residual's spread) and it does exactly what its loss asks, about
88% of its own training rows inside u·η, but on ThrusterChain it is almost a constant: swapping it for its average
changes the band's coverage by less than half a point, and it rises only 7% where the critic's error nearly doubles
(strong current), so the row-by-row shape of BCA's band comes from the threshold and the unit, not from η.*

## 1. What the module does, in plain words

BCA puts an error band around a **critic**, the learned estimate q of how much return a command earns from a state.
At a recorded row the critic's one-step error is the **residual** e = y − q (module M1), in critic value units. Module
M2 produces two things that turn a residual into a dimensionless **score** (module M3):

- the **unit** u: one number, a running average of the residual's spread (its standard deviation over training
  batches), in critic value units;
- the **error-size network** η(s, a): a small network (two layers of 64 units, one output) that says, for each row,
  how large |e| should be there, as a multiple of u. It is always positive (a softplus plus a floor of 0.01).

The band at a row is q ± R·u·η(s, a), where R is the threshold of module M5. The job of η and u is to make the
product u·η already about right, so that 90% of residuals satisfy |e| ≤ u·η and R comes out near 1. η is fitted at
every update (co-trained hosts) or for 20,000 steps on a frozen critic (the weighting test's band, Stage 2, the
near-80 run's version-6 bands examined here). The fit minimizes

    L = (weighted share of rows with |e| ≤ u·η, smoothed  −  0.9)²  +  0.005 × weighted mean of η²

over batches of 256 training rows, where the weights are one Bayesian-bootstrap draw per batch (Dirichlet masses, a
random reweighting that makes the fit resemble a posterior sample), the share is smoothed with a steep sigmoid (slope
20) so that it has a gradient, and the second term is a **width penalty** that prefers a narrow band. After each step
the unit moves: u ← 0.99·u + 0.01·SD(e over the batch).

**The records used.** The near-80 run (`outputs/shift/beyond-the-data/near80/`, pre-registration 51e677ab) fitted
one such band on a frozen fixed-controller critic for each of 2 settings (near80: the ball and the 70-90% throttle
band removed; random_rows: the same number of rows removed at random) × 2 worlds (stalling, no-stall) × seeds 0-4:
**20 bands**. Each band record (`v6_band.json`) holds the accepted share of the 20,000 fit steps, the unit, the
threshold and the hashes of its inputs; `v6_band.msgpack` holds η's parameters; `v6_bank_scores.npz` holds η and the
score at every held-out calibration row (805-832 rows per band). Files: branch `claude/near80-run-files`,
`runs/shift_explore/near80/learners/<setting>/<world>/seed<k>/`. The weighting test (`outputs/shift_iw/v5/`, branch
`main`) recorded its pre-registered mark M2, the only measurement on record of η's link to the critic's *exact* error.

## 2. What "works as intended" means (checkable statements)

- **C1, mechanics.** Every fit step is accepted (nothing non-finite ever enters); η is finite and at or above its
  0.01 floor at every row; the unit equals the residual's spread on the rows it was averaged over (within a few
  percent).
- **C2, the fit's own target.** On the rows the fit saw (the kept training rows), the share with |e| ≤ u·η is at the
  level the loss aims at: 0.9, less what the width penalty trades away (section 4 derives the equilibrium, 0.87).
- **C3, carried to new rows.** On the held-out calibration rows the same share is near 0.9, so that module M5's
  threshold R comes out near 1 or a little above (the map's wording).
- **C4, purpose.** η is larger where the critic's error is larger: when |e| grows (with the current, at full
  throttle), η grows with it, so the band holds its coverage row by row and not only on average.

C1 and C2 are the mechanics. C3 and C4 are the purpose; they are where the caveat lies.

## 4. Synthetic test: the project's own fit on rows whose error size is known

`M2_test.py` part A drives the real `fit_scale` (no copy of it) with a synthetic host whose critic pair and target
actor return 0, so that y − q equals the reward column, which is filled with a known error e = σ(row)·Z, Z standard
normal. The state is 13 standard normal numbers (already normalized, as the pipeline hands them over), the command 4
uniform numbers in [−1, 1]. 40,000 training rows, 4,000 held-out rows from the same law, 20,000 fit steps of 256 rows,
the study's settings. Three worlds: the error size **visible** to the network (log σ = log 0.1 + 0.6·s₀ + 0.4·a₀),
**constant** (σ = 0.1), and **hidden** (log σ = log 0.1 + 0.6·h with h a number the network never sees). Numbers from
the committed run (Windows venv, jax 0.12.0 CPU; the fit is deterministic given its keys).

**A0, the pieces** (PASS). η at states ±1000 and commands ±1: 0.0100, 200.4, 0.703, 0.718, all finite and positive;
at state +1000 the float32 softplus underflows and η *equals* the floor (hence the correction to M2.2). Soft coverage
is 1.0000 at the band's center, 0.500 at its edge, 4·10⁻¹¹ at twice the band. Bootstrap masses: sum 1.000000, all
≥ 0, largest 0.027. **A0b, the acceptance rule** (PASS): a batch with every residual 0 is accepted and u becomes
0.99·1 + 0.01·10⁻⁶ (positive, the floor at work); a batch with one NaN residual is rejected and η's parameters and u
stay exactly as they were.

What the synthetic test establishes:

- **A1 (C1) PASS.** Every step accepted in all three worlds; η finite and above the floor at every row; the unit
  within 3% of SD(e) on the training rows (0.985, 0.997, 0.993).
- **The loss's own equilibrium is 0.87, not 0.90.** With a constant error size the loss (C(η) − 0.9)² + 0.005·η²,
  C(η) = P(|Z| ≤ η), is stationary at η = 1.515·σ/u and C = 0.870 (`M2_test.py`, `penalty_equilibrium`): the width
  penalty 0.005 buys a band 8% narrower than the 90% band (1.515 against 1.645) for 3 points of coverage. The fitted
  constant-world η is 1.432·σ/u on held-out rows and the training-row coverage 0.884; the soft coverage's smoothing
  and the bootstrap masses account for the small difference.
- **A2a (C2) PASS.** On the rows the fit saw, the share inside u·η is 0.886, 0.884, 0.878, within 0.02 of 0.870 in
  all three worlds.
- **A2b (C3) FAIL as a strict statement.** On held-out rows from the *same* law the share is 0.859, 0.848, 0.833
  and R is 1.208, 1.195, 1.391: the network overfits the training rows' particular errors (3.6 points on average
  with 40,000 rows and 128 passes over them), most where there is nothing to learn (the hidden world: η's spread is
  15% of its mean and all of it is noise; R 1.39). R absorbs it, so the band of module M6 still covers 0.908. On
  ThrusterChain η is far flatter and this cost is under half a point (section 5c).
- **A3 (C4 where it can hold) PASS.** Where the error size can be read off (s, a), η follows it (Spearman 0.880),
  but not fully: the quadratic penalty flattens η at large errors (the visible world's largest-error tenth is
  covered 0.55, its smallest 0.99; figure, panel a). With a constant size η is nearly constant (spread/mean 0.12).
  Where the size is hidden, η is flat and the band is wrong row by row (1.00 on the smallest-error tenth, 0.43 on
  the largest) while the average stays 0.833: **the fit enforces coverage on average, never row by row**.
- **A4, the real data's situation, PASS.** The same fitted η and u (visible world), held-out errors scaled up by a
  factor:

| Factor on held-out errors | Share \|e\| ≤ u·η | Expected P(\|Z\| ≤ 1.555/factor) | R (module M5) | Share inside R·u·η |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | 0.859 | 0.880 | 1.208 | 0.908 |
| 1.2 | 0.797 | 0.805 | 1.450 | 0.908 |
| 1.3 | 0.766 | 0.768 | 1.571 | 0.908 |
| 1.4 | 0.733 | 0.733 | 1.692 | 0.908 |

  If a critic's errors on rows it never trained on are 1.2-1.4 times its training errors, the held-out share falls
  to about 0.8 and R rises to 1.45-1.7 while the full band still covers 0.908. This is the recorded pattern of
  section 5, and it says where the correction lives: in M5, not in M2.

### 5e. Consistent and exceptional cases

Consistent, 20 of 20 bands: every fit step accepted; η within the floor; the unit within 5% of the training-row
spread; training coverage 0.867-0.891; held-out coverage 0.762-0.827; the constant-η swap within 1.5 points; coverage
falling from the weakest to the strongest current tenth (0.92 to 0.77 training, 0.81 to 0.69 held-out); η's
rank correlation with |e| 0.16-0.33.

Exceptional: in the random_rows bands of the stalling world, a few training rows get a very small η: 120 rows with
η < 0.5 on seed 0 (of 41,056; 24 on seed 3, 15 on seed 2; 0-3 in the near80 bands). They are high-current (0.62-0.64
against 0.37), higher-throttle (0.65-0.68 against 0.47) rows whose |e| averages 0.94-1.50, nine to fourteen times the
band's mean error: **the network gives its smallest η to the rows with the largest errors.** The loss explains it:
for a row whose score is far above any plausible η the sigmoid of M2.4 is saturated, the coverage term's gradient is
zero, and only the width penalty acts, pulling η down. Among the held-out rows 0 to 2 such rows occur per band (η min
0.138 on random_rows/stall/seed 0), so the recorded thresholds are not affected; it matters for any use of η at
off-data commands (modules M8 and M9), where the band could narrow exactly where the critic is worst.

## 8. What to say to the advisor

1. The error-size network is mechanically sound: 400,000 recorded fit steps, all accepted; η always finite and at or
   above its floor; the unit within 5% of the residual's spread; the stored η and scores reproduced byte for byte
   from the saved networks in the pinned environment.
2. It does what its loss asks. The loss aims at 90% coverage but its width penalty settles it at about 87-88%, by
   design (derived and measured: 0.870 in theory, 0.878-0.886 on synthetic rows, 0.867-0.891 on the real training
   rows).
3. On the held-out rows the band at threshold 1 covers only 76-83%, because the critic's errors on rows it never
   trained on are 1.2-1.5 times larger. That is the critic's generalization gap, not a fault of η; the threshold R
   (module M5), computed on held-out rows, absorbs it: 1.35-1.71 instead of about 1.05.
4. The caveat: on ThrusterChain η is nearly a constant. Replacing it by its average changes coverage by less than
   half a point. Where the critic's error nearly doubles (strong current) η rises 7%, so the band's held-out coverage
   falls from 93-95% to 84% across the current range even after the threshold. The pre-registered mark that links the
   width to the exact critic error passes, but at 0.10-0.33 (0.06-0.18 after removing the boat's drift), while the
   width follows the noisiness of recorded outcomes at 0.52-0.76.
5. Consistent in 20 of 20 bands. The one exceptional pattern: a handful of training rows with the largest errors get
   the smallest η (the coverage gradient vanishes far outside the band and only the width penalty acts). It does not
   touch the recorded thresholds; it would matter for uses of η at off-data commands.

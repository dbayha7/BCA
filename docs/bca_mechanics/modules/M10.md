## 1. What the module does, in plain words

BCA's main pipeline puts an error band around a critic. The restructured BCA (step 1 of
`outputs/shift/beyond-the-data/restructure/`, then the near-80 run) uses the same Bayesian posterior for a different
job: to decide, per world, whether the critic is credibly *too optimistic about full-throttle commands*, which in
ThrusterChain (the project's simulated boat) is exactly where one world hides a stall. This decision is the **harm
switch**; the number it hands on is the **height**, which module M11 turns into a training penalty (the ramp).

The words used below:

- **Throttle index** t(a): the largest of the four thruster magnitudes of a command a, a number in [0, 1].
- **Kept training rows**: the recorded rows the setting leaves in training after its removal (the main setting
  removes the densest 15% of commands; the near-80 setting also removes every command with a throttle from 70% to
  90%; the random-rows setting removes as many random rows instead). About 41,000-42,500 rows per seed-world.
- **The gap rule**: a rule that reads commands only. Among the kept training rows with a throttle index above the 99th
  percentile, sort the indices and find the largest empty stretch between neighbors. Its lower end is **t_lo**, its
  upper end **t_up**. The rule is **defined** when the stretch is at least 0.05 wide and at least 9 rows lie at or
  above t_up. Those rows are the **survivors**: full-throttle commands that stayed in training.
- **The second critic**: a critic pair trained exactly like the setting's own critic (100,000 updates, the fixed
  stall-aware controller) but on the kept rows *minus the survivors*. So the survivors are held out from it.
- **Held-out rows**: the setting's calibration rows (the code calls them "the bank"), about 800-880 rows per
  seed-world, never used in training.
- **The overestimate** s of a held-out row: the second critic's value for the recorded command, minus what one step
  actually delivered plus the critic's value of the controller's next command: s = min(Q1, Q2)(o, a) − [r + 0.98 ·
  (1 − d) · min(Q1, Q2)(o′, c(o′))]. Positive means the critic promised more than the step delivered. In value units.
- **The full-throttle group** ("tail"): the survivors plus the held-out rows with t > t_lo. **The rest**: the held-out
  rows with t ≤ t_lo. In the records the tail has 18-60 rows, almost all of them survivors; the rest has 803-878.
- **BCA's posterior** on a group of scores (module M5, weights 1): 1,000 Dirichlet draws over the scores, each giving a
  **crossing** (the draw's 90% quantile; +∞ when the draw puts too little mass on the scores, which happens with
  probability 0.9^n per draw for n scores). **λ̂** is the plain 90% quantile, **λ_HPD** the 95%-credible quantile of
  the crossings, and the **threshold** R = max(λ̂, λ_HPD). R is +∞ ("cannot certify") unless at least 950 of the 1,000
  crossings are finite.
- **q05**: the 5% quantile of (tail crossing − rest crossing) over all 1,000 × 1,000 pairs of draws: a 95%-credible
  lower bound on how much larger the tail's 90% quantile is than the rest's.
- **The margin**: 0.10 value units. **The switch** is on if and only if q05 > 0.10.
- **The height** h = R_tail − R_rest. It is +∞ when the tail cannot be certified.
- **Plain gap**: λ̂_tail − λ̂_rest, the gap between the two plain 90% quantiles (a point estimate of what q05 bounds).

Every number of the certificate is recorded per seed and world in `switch.json` (near-80 run, 30 files) and in
`restructure/step1/results/results.json` (step 1, 10 seed-worlds).

## 2. What "works as intended" means (checkable statements)

- **S1, the decision.** The switch is on in every stalling world and off in every no-stall world, with q05 well away
  from the margin on both sides; and the decision does not depend on the posterior's random stream.
- **S2, it reads outcomes, not labels.** When the pooled scores are dealt back to the two groups at random, the switch
  turns off (step 1's pre-set condition C3; reproduced here on the near-80 scores).
- **S3, mechanics.** The recorded certificate is reproduced, field for field, from its recorded inputs (the saved
  second critic applied to the recorded rows, the gap rule rerun on the kept commands, the recorded streams) in the
  pinned environment; and from the per-row scores on this machine bit for bit.
- **S4, statistical meaning (synthetic, the truth known).** With two groups whose 90% quantiles differ by a known gap,
  q05 lies below the true gap in about 95% of data sets (a valid lower bound), the switch turns on once the gap
  clears the margin, and with no gap it never turns on at the recorded spread of the scores.
- **S5, the height.** It is finite exactly when module M5's group-size rule says a threshold can be certified (never
  at 24 or fewer rows, by chance at 25-35, always at 36 or more); where finite it equals the true gap plus a
  small-sample margin (synthetic); and in the records q05 ≤ plain gap ≤ height.

S1-S3 are the owner's own checks (step 1's C1 and C3, the near-80 reproduction check), S4-S5 are new.

One statement of MODULES.md is sharpened here. It says the height "is finite whenever the tail holds at least 36 rows
and may be infinite below that". The exact rule (from the binomial arithmetic of M5 and confirmed on synthetic data
below) is: never finite at 24 or fewer rows (probability below 0.001), finite with probability 0.003 at 25, 0.03 at
26, 0.15 at 27, 0.40 at 28, 0.70 at 29, 0.90 at 30, 0.98 at 31, 0.996 at 32, and always at 33 or more (1.000 to
three decimals; `calibration/reference.certifiable` uses tolerance 1e-6 and says 36). The header of
`scripts/certificate_table.py` ("never finite with 28 or fewer") is the expected-share approximation and is too strict
by this rule; the 28-row record it describes is infinite by chance (probability 0.60).

## 4. Synthetic test: two groups with a known gap

`M10_test.py` Part A calls the project's own `step1lib.certificate` (which calls `calibration/wbcp.calibrate`) on
synthetic scores. The **spread** of the synthetic scores is 0.15 value units, the recorded spread of the rest group's
scores (standard deviation 0.120-0.179 in all 30 near-80 seed-worlds), so that the margin of 0.10 means the same
thing as in the records (about two thirds of a standard deviation). Group sizes 40 and 830 are typical recorded sizes.

**Table A1. A known gap** (tail ~ N(gap, 0.15) with 40 rows, rest ~ N(0, 0.15) with 830 rows; 200 data sets per gap;
the true gap between the two 90% quantiles is exactly `gap`).

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.00 | 0.000 | 0.940 | −0.052 | +0.116 (0.020 to 0.257) |
| 0.05 | 0.005 | 0.930 | −0.007 | +0.089 (−0.003 to 0.231) |
| 0.10 (= the margin) | 0.060 | 0.940 | 0.052 | +0.113 (0.026 to 0.234) |
| 0.15 | 0.480 | 0.950 | 0.098 | +0.097 (0.017 to 0.266) |
| 0.20 | 0.890 | 0.905 | 0.145 | +0.106 (0.009 to 0.235) |
| 0.30 | 1.000 | 0.915 | 0.246 | +0.107 (0.010 to 0.252) |
| 0.50 | 1.000 | 0.955 | 0.444 | +0.102 (0.011 to 0.227) |

What this shows. q05 is a lower bound on the true gap in 90-96% of data sets (nominal 95%): **S4 holds**. The switch
never fires with no gap, fires in 6% of data sets when the true gap equals the margin, and always once the gap is 0.30
(two standard deviations). The height overstates the true gap by about 0.10 value units with 40 tail rows: that is the
small-sample margin of the tail's credible threshold (R = max(λ̂, λ_HPD), and λ_HPD sits above λ̂ when the group is
small), while the rest's 830-row threshold carries almost none. So in a harmless world the height is not zero; it is a
positive number of the order of the margin, which is why the records' no-stall heights are 0.04-0.78 although their
q05 is at or below zero.

**S5 holds**: the switch is on at every size, even with 15 rows, while the height is finite exactly as often as the
binomial rule predicts. The band "on, but height +∞" runs from 100% at 24 rows to 0% at 32. This is the mechanism of
the exceptional case in section 7.

**A4, the shuffle control** (one data set with gap 2.0, spread 1.0 and 50 tail rows; the pooled scores dealt back at random
200 times with step 1's streams, the certificate recomputed with the same posterior streams). Real: on, q05 1.841,
height 2.704. Shuffled: switched on in 5 of the 200 permutations (2.5%); q05 median −0.494, largest 0.349. At a
spread of 1.0 the margin 0.10 is only a tenth of a standard deviation, so a 95%-credible lower bound clears it in a
few percent of random splits; at the recorded spread (Table A2) it never did. **S2 holds** on synthetic data, with
the margin's protection again relative to the scores' spread.

**A5, the random stream.** The same tags give the same certificate (bit for bit, three group sizes). Over 20 other
streams on one fixed data set (gap 1.0, spread 1.0): with 30 tail rows q05 ranged from −0.007 to 0.171, so the
decision at this gap-to-spread ratio *can* depend on the stream when q05 is near the margin; with 50 rows 0.483-0.522;
with 300 rows 0.768-0.783. The height moved by 0.8% (30 rows), 2.4% (50 rows) and 12% (300 rows) of its median: with
few scores the credible threshold is pinned to the top score, with many it falls between order statistics that the
draws pick differently. Section 5 (B4) asks the same question of the real scores.

**A6, the gap rule on synthetic throttles.** 40,000 throttle indices spread over [0, 0.78] plus 50 at 1.0: defined,
t_lo = the largest bulk throttle (0.77998), t_up = 1.0, 50 survivors. With only 8 rows at 1.0: undefined (minimum 9).
With 50 rows at 0.81 (gap 0.030 < 0.05): undefined.

### 5.2 Recomputation from the recorded inputs (B2; the pinned environment)

`M10_recompute_pinned.py`, run through the WSL bridge in the pinned environment (Python 3.10.20, numpy 2.2.6, jax
0.6.2 on CPU), reran for each of the 30 near-80 seed-worlds: the gap rule on the kept training commands, the saved
second critic (`critic_second.msgpack`, SHA-256 checked against its record) applied to the survivors and the held-out
rows with the stall-aware controller, and `step1lib.certificate` with the recorded streams. Nothing was trained.

- **S3 holds.** All 30 certificates equal their `switch.json` record in every numeric field (relative tolerance 1e-9;
  q05, q50, height, R_tail, R_rest, λ̂ of both groups, finite shares, means, plain gap, the design's e11 form), the gap
  rule's t_lo and t_up equal the records in all 30, and the survivors' SHA-256 equals the second critic's record in all
  30. The per-row scores were saved (`data/M10/overestimates.npz`, 30 tail and 30 rest arrays); on this machine
  (numpy 2.5.4) the certificate recomputed from them with the recorded streams equals the record with a largest
  absolute difference of 0.0.
- **The gap rule in the records.** t_lo is 0.6999-0.7000 in the near-80 setting (the removed band starts at 70%) and
  0.782-0.791 in the main and random-rows settings; t_up is 0.977-1.000; the rule sorts 410-425 of 40,969-42,500
  kept rows. The held-out rows contributed 0-3 rows to the tail: the full-throttle group is the survivors.
- **Which score the thresholds are.** Where finite, R_tail is the tail group's largest score in 13 of 23 seed-worlds
  and its second largest in 10; the plain 90% quantile λ̂_tail is the 2nd to 7th largest; R_rest is the 66th to 74th
  largest of 803-878 rest scores (near its 90% quantile, as the large-sample threshold should be). So the height is,
  in every record, the gap between one or two extreme overestimates of the full-throttle group and the rest's
  ordinary 90% level.

### 5.3 The shuffle control on the near-80 scores (B3; new, exploratory)

1 of 200 and one (main setting, stall, seed 11) had 4 of 200; the largest shuffled q05 was 0.254 against real values
of 0.56-1.26 in the stalling world; the median shuffled q05 was −0.04 to −0.06 everywhere. Step 1's own record (200
permutations per seed-world, seeds 0-4) had on-rates of 0.0 in 9 of 10 seed-worlds and 0.005 in stall/seed 4. **S2
holds** on the real scores.

## 7. Consistent and exceptional cases (for debugging and for the advisor)

**Consistent (every record).**
1. The switch is right in all 40 seed-worlds, with q05 at least 0.46 above the margin where full throttle stalls and at
   least 0.047 below it where it does not; the decision survives shuffling and 20 alternative random streams.
2. The certificate reproduces exactly from the saved second critic and the recorded rows (30 of 30), and from the
   per-row scores bit for bit.
3. R_tail is finite or infinite exactly as the group size predicts (0.9^n arithmetic), independent of the critic.
4. The height is a conservative number: height ≥ plain gap ≥ q05 in every record; in the harmless world it is
   0.04-0.78, not zero.
5. The full-throttle group is the survivors: 0-3 held-out rows join it. Its size, 18-60 rows, is decided by how many
   full-throttle commands the data generator left in the kept training rows of that seed, not by anything BCA does.

**Exceptional (the cases to show).**
6. **Switch on, height +∞**: main setting, fresh seeds 10, 12, 13 in the stalling world (19, 22 and 18 rows). The
   switch reads q05, which stays finite when a few of the tail's crossings are infinite (85-90% of its draws were
   finite); the height reads R_tail, which is infinite as soon as fewer than 950 of 1,000 crossings are finite. The
   approved step-2 code refuses to build a switched-on arm with an infinite height (`step2lib.arm_table`, line 356),
   so those three policy phases stopped ("a built-in check failed", `files/stops/policy.main.stall.s1{0,2,3}.json`)
   and claim 4 could not be tested on them (`near80_results_check/REPORT.md`). No rule for this case was
   pre-registered; the data guard was set at the gap rule's 9 rows, not the certificate's about 29. The
   near-80 and random-rows settings never met it on seeds 0-4 (tails of 28-60; the two infinite heights there came with
   the switch off, so nothing was refused). The results check's own estimate is that about one fresh seed in four would
   stop this way.
7. **The height moves with the random stream by up to a factor of two** (near-80 setting, stall, seed 1: 2.56
   recorded, 2.56-4.87 over 20 streams) because, with 29-60 scores, the credible threshold is the group's largest or
   second-largest overestimate and the draws decide which. The switch never moved. This is M11's input; a ramp height
   that depends on the stream to that degree is a caveat for M11's dose readouts, not for the switch.
8. **A tail at the chance boundary**: random rows, stall, seed 0 (29 rows) was certified (probability 0.70) with a
   height of 5.61 set by its largest score; the same size in the no-stall world of seed 3 was not. With 25-32 rows
   whether the ramp gets a height at all is a coin with known odds.

## 9. What to say to the advisor

1. The harm switch is the one part of BCA on ThrusterChain that did what it was built for: it read held-out outcomes
   and turned caution on in every world where full throttle stalls (20 of 20) and off in every world where it does
   not (20 of 20), with a clear margin on both sides, and it turned off when the outcomes were shuffled. These numbers
   reproduce exactly from the saved critic and rows.
2. The statistic is sound on synthetic data: q05 is a valid 95% lower bound on the gap between the two groups' 90%
   quantiles, the margin 0.10 (two thirds of the recorded spread) gives zero false alarms with no gap, and the switch is
   certain once the gap reaches two standard deviations.
3. The caveat is the height. It is +∞ whenever the full-throttle group has fewer than about 29 rows, which is pure
   arithmetic of the 1,000-draw posterior (0.9^n) and has nothing to do with the critic; three of the five fresh
   stalling seeds had 18-22 such rows, the switch said "on", the height said "cannot certify", and the approved code
   stopped. Where finite, the height is the group's largest or second-largest overestimate minus the rest's 90% level,
   so it carries a positive offset even in a harmless world (0.04-0.78) and can change by a factor of two with the
   random stream.
4. The consistent pattern: the switch is a robust yes/no; the height is a coarse, conservative size. The exceptional
   cases are all one thing, a small full-throttle group (18-29 rows), and they are predictable from the group size
   before any critic is trained. A rule for "on but uncertified" (for example, a fixed height, which step 2 already ran
   as the `fixed_gated` arm) would have to be pre-registered before the next fresh seeds.

  `--figure-only` redraws the figure from the saved JSON; `--png <path>` also writes a PNG preview.

  `M10_recompute_job.sh` (the header of that file gives the exact procedure; Python 3.10.20, numpy 2.2.6, jax 0.6.2,
  `JAX_PLATFORMS=cpu`, `OMP_NUM_THREADS=1`, the gate's flags). It reads the owner's run folder
  `runs/shift_explore/near80` read-only and takes about 90 seconds per setting. Its outputs are committed as

Widths in training: the toys' negative result matches the record

  • The capped pull cost return on the three lower data qualities and gained at most about half a point. The capped pull is BCA's own use of its band in training, a stronger pull toward the recorded command where the band is wide: the role the toys shelved. In the weighting test (exploratory result B, seeds 0–4, strong test), unweighted, it scored below plain TD3+BC on random (57.2 against 66.0), medium-replay (71.2 against 77.0) and medium data (78.8 against 84.1). On medium-expert it scored 0.55 points above it (91.6 against 91.1; one-sided lower bound +0.21 over the five seeds, which the page treats as a pointer, not evidence, since the run made 63 comparisons per quality with no correction), and on expert it was level (90.2 against 90.3). Weighted, it scored lower still or within 0.1 points of that on every quality; weighting did not raise return on any quality.
  • The same in the main test's four situations. An exploratory evaluation made on 16 Oct of the seed-0 rerun's actors, the ones the live panel replays, in the main test's four situations (one seed; 1,000 fixed starts per situation; stalling world; points on the weighting test's scale, 0 = uniform random commands, 100 = stall-aware controller; its records are not yet in the repository): unweighted or weighted, the capped pull scored below plain TD3+BC in all four situations on random (5.7 to 12.6 points lower), medium-replay (2.2 to 6.1) and medium data (2.3 to 6.7). On medium-expert and expert it scored between 2.3 points lower and 0.2 points higher. On this one seed, result B's small medium-expert lead did not appear. The full table, why the capped pull fails and potential fixes are in BCA's main channel.
  • Even perfectly placed, the pull won back too little. In the main test, the harder pull placed by the critic's exact error beat three controls by only −1.9 to +1.1 points, which stopped that test on 12 Oct (Routes and their status).
  • The lower edge gained only where something else did the work. The lower edge is the other width the toys shelved. As built, BCA's own width barely reacts to the command: in a read-only diagnosis on seeds 0–4 it was 0.31 to 0.36 value units both at a harmful command past 80% and at the 80% cap, while the critic's false preference for that command was 0.63 to 0.89. As in experiment 2's test 1, the width did not react where the decision is made (Restructured BCA). Stage 2's weighted lower edge did win back points in strong current (24.9 against −14.3 for no caution), but 86% of its extra width at harmful commands came from a classifier term that reads where the data lie; it cost 14.8 points in the no-stall world and missed two of its five marks, M4 and M5 (Stage 2). In the weighting test the lower edge, unweighted or weighted, moved return by −0.1 to +0.3 points against the same actor with no extra caution (result B).

Reading held-out outcomes: the toys' positive result matches the record

  • Step 1 is, in toy terms, a gate. The restructured BCA reads the critic's over-predictions on the 29 to 60 full-throttle steps per seed and world left after the removal (step 1's run record, step1/results/results.json), scored by critics that never trained on them: held-out tries of the risky action. Its step 1 passed: caution on in 5 of 5 stalling seeds, off in 5 of 5 no-stall seeds, 106.1 points net across the two worlds against a bar of 90 (designed after seeing seeds 0–4; Restructured BCA). In route search round 1, every gain came from reading outcomes against the fixed 0.10 margin (Routes and their status).
  • Step 2 put the same switch inside the actor's training and met all three of its pass marks (GO, 15 Oct; exploratory): net 102.9 across the two worlds, against 77.7 for the same penalty left always on. That whole lead comes from switching off in the no-stall world. The penalty it switches on rises past the gap in the recorded throttles, so where it acts is set by the recorded range; what told the worlds apart was the switch that reads outcomes.
  • BCA's Bayesian part got no credit, as in the toys. In step 1 itself, a plain rank test gave the same yes or no in 9 of the 10 seed-and-world pairs. A later route search credited step 1's fixed 0.10 margin, not the posterior: with 12 to 45 rows, BCA beat plain split conformal by only 1.78 points and a plain rank test by only 4.29, both against a 5-point bar. In step 2, plain split conformal's switch made the same on/off decision as BCA's in all 10 runs (step 2's run record, step2_results/results.md), and nothing was credited to calibration or to BCA's Bayesian part. That matches experiment 2's shelved test 4a: with equal weights, BCA is the exact binomial test.

Where the toys do not carry over

  • A quiet comparison group. ThrusterChain's comparison group was quiet (its 90% point stayed between −0.049 and −0.031 in step 1's run record), so the toy's step-1 weakness, a noisy comparison group, did not arise.
  • One-step targets. ThrusterChain's scores use one-step targets, so the critic's value of the next state would stay in a harm test. The toy's last stage has no next state, so there the critic drops out.
  • Thin evidence. A tail gate needs 29 rows. In step 1, no-stall seed 3 had 29, and BCA's random draws gave it no finite caution size (947 of BCA's 1,000 random draws cleared the bar; 950 were needed; step 1's run record). In the toys, computed exactly as they recommend, 29 equally weighted rows are just enough for a finite answer. Under a shift they need more: experiment 4's tail gate needs about 39 held-out outcomes per decision under its moderate shift and about 83 under its strong one. If step 1's 29 to 60 full-throttle steps per seed and world had to be weighted for a shift like those, they would be enough for some seeds and worlds under the moderate shift and for none under the strong one.
  • The shape of the harm. The stall is shortfall-shaped (past 80% every command pushes less, rather than failing rarely), the shape in which the toy's plain average served return best: with 30 survivors it ventured in 99% to 100% of seeds where venturing pays and in at most 1% where it loses.
  • A given region. In the toys the risky action was given and its held-out tries were real outcomes. Step 1 was given the full-throttle region, and its outcomes are one-step scores from critics.

Result F: what the toys predict, as a hypothesis

exploratory hypothesis Predictions from toy mechanisms, not ThrusterChain results. The control that would test them has not been run.

Result F is the weighting test's one case where BCA's band, used in training, clearly raised return. The band was weighted at the actor's own commands, and the command part of the weight was given the recording controller's exact command likelihood. That likelihood is zero wherever the controller never gives a command; on expert data, that includes every command past 80%. Where it is zero, the band refuses the command, and result F's rule for refused commands gives it no value credit: in the actor's training it earns no more value than the recorded command at that row. (The page calls this rule the fixed fallback; it is not this section's fallback action.)

On the strong test, against the band penalty as first planned (version 5's unweighted lower edge), return rose by 2.0 points on medium-expert and 5.8 on expert, every seed positive. An untargeted control, the same amount of no value credit at randomly chosen rows, gained about 1 unit of return, against 25.0 and 74.2 units for the rule. Only the actor was trained, against a frozen critic, and the rule was designed after a first version had lost 9.8 points on medium-expert. Two things are open: the control without the band, the same no-value-credit rule alone with no band, was not run; and with estimated weights the gain held only on expert (2.3 points).

  • Most of the gain would come from the no-value-credit rule, not from the band's calibration. In experiment 1, refusing the never-tried action did all the safety work, by construction for every method; BCA's calibrated width only added caution on a recorded action with noisy rewards, and cost return (−0.162 per episode). On ThrusterChain, the weighting test's band penalty as first planned (its version 5), on its own, gained nothing (90.6 against 90.7 points on expert). So the toys predict that the untested control would recover most of result F's gain.
  • Past 80%, a gate could not do better than the rule. The expert recording holds no commands past 80%, so there are no held-out tries there, and every valid toy gate falls back without evidence (the tail gate cannot say yes below 29 tries). There, a gate and the no-value-credit rule would decide alike.
  • The rule would cost return where the unseen commands help. A rule that reads only where the data lie cannot tell a harmful unseen action from a harmless one. In experiment 1, refusal was right in the harmful world and cost 0.65 per episode in the harmless one, and no data could tell them apart. Stage 2's data-only rivals showed the same pattern (Stage 2): the range hinge, a penalty that grows as a command leaves the range of the recorded commands, won back 84.6 points in strong current in the stalling world and lost 20.4 there in the no-stall world. The weighting test does not report result F in a world where pushing past 80% helps.
  • Estimated weights lie outside what the toys tested. Experiment 4's weights were known exactly; estimating them is the notebooks' first next step, designed but not run. So the toys do not explain why the estimated weights kept a gain only on expert.

No plan changes here

The toys propose BCA as a gate that decides whether to venture at the gap, rather than as a width in training, and the notebook proposes a ThrusterChain test of the mean gate at deviations; it would change step 2 of the restructured BCA and needs a new pre-registration, approved by the project owner at their own terminal, and it has not been approved or run. Other learners that host BCA were not tested in the toys. Stage 2's version 7, which was to test the width on fresh seeds 5–9, has ended and will not run. Step 2 of the restructured BCA, its training pilot, was fixed in writing before any training and ran unchanged; any change to a pre-registered test needs the project owner's approval at their own terminal.

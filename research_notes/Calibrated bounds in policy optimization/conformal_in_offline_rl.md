# Conformal and calibrated uncertainty inside offline RL, bandits and off-policy evaluation

Compiled 14 Oct 2026 for the BCA project. arXiv ids, titles, authors and venues were checked against the arXiv API or the proceedings page unless flagged. "Inference" means my own reasoning from the cited material, not a claim made by a source.

## 1. Conformal off-policy evaluation and prediction: what is bounded, how importance weights enter, and whether intervals are used to improve the policy

### Takeaway
Every core conformal OPE paper bounds an individual outcome or return under the target policy, not the policy's mean value. That covers COPP (NeurIPS 2022), Zhang–Shi–Luo (AISTATS 2023) and Foffano et al. (CDC 2023), plus all the 2024–2026 follow-ups found here. They handle policy shift with weighted conformal prediction, where the weight is a ratio of outcome-given-context densities (π/μ integrated against an outcome model), or with pseudo-policy subsampling. None of them uses its intervals to train or improve a policy, and COPP explicitly lists "apply COPP to policy training" as future work.

### Cited Findings
**COPP: Taufiq, Ton, Cornish, Teh, Doucet (NeurIPS 2022; arXiv:2206.04405)**
- COPP outputs "reliable predictive intervals for the outcome under a new target policy" with "finite-sample guarantees without making any additional assumptions beyond the standard contextual bandit setup". The stated motivation is that "the expectation may not be the best measure of performance as it does not capture the variability of the outcome". It targets the outcome Y, not the mean. — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.04405)
- It frames policy shift as a shift in the conditional P(Y|X) rather than in P(X). The weights are w(x,y) = dP^{π*}(y|x)/dP^{πb}(y|x), obtained by integrating an estimated P̂(y|x,a) against π*(a|x) and πb(a|x). Because the weights depend on y, intervals require a grid of candidate y values per x (100 in the experiments). — [NeurIPS paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/cc84bfabe6389d8883fc2071c848f62a-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- With estimated weights, coverage is ≥ 1 − α − Δ_w, where Δ_w = ½·E_{πb}|ŵ(X,Y) − w(X,Y)| (Prop. 4.2). Conditional coverage holds only asymptotically, under "somewhat strong assumptions" (Prop. 4.3). COPP attributes the estimated-weight bound to its ref. [14], which, from context, appears to be Lei & Candès; I have not verified that from the reference list. — [NeurIPS paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/cc84bfabe6389d8883fc2071c848f62a-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- Experiments (Table 2): COPP attains the required 90% coverage across levels of policy shift ε. The WIS and SBA baselines are over-conservative (coverage above 90%), and unweighted CP "becomes progressively worse with increasing policy shift". — [NeurIPS paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/cc84bfabe6389d8883fc2071c848f62a-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- Stated limitations: "reliable estimation of our weights ŵ(x, y) requires sufficient overlap between behaviour and target policies" (the failure is shown in App. D.1.2), and modeling P(y|x,a) "can be challenging" (a direct weight estimator is given in App. B.5.2). — [NeurIPS paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/cc84bfabe6389d8883fc2071c848f62a-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- No policy improvement. The conclusion reads: "For future work, it would be interesting to apply COPP to policy training. This could be a step towards robust policy learning by optimising the worst case outcome." — [NeurIPS paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/cc84bfabe6389d8883fc2071c848f62a-Paper-Conference.pdf)

**Zhang, Shi & Luo, "Conformal Off-Policy Prediction" (AISTATS 2023, PMLR 206; arXiv:2206.06711)**
- The paper gives interval estimators "for a target policy's return starting from any initial state". It "accounts for the variability of the return around its expectation, focuses on the individual effect". — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v206/zhang23c.html)
- Mechanism: a "pseudo policy that generates subsamples as if they were sampled from the target policy". The authors sample a subset based on a pseudo variable that depends on both the target and behavior policies, then apply weighted CP to the selected subsamples. Importance-sampling-based and multi-sampling-based variants improve efficiency. Coverage is "valid ... asymptotically", and exact in finite samples "when the behavior policy is known (e.g., as in randomized studies)". The data are synthetic plus short-video-platform data. — [arXiv/ar5iv text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06711)

**Foffano, Russo & Proutiere, "Conformal Off-Policy Evaluation in MDPs" (IEEE CDC 2023; arXiv:2304.02574)**
- The method outputs "an interval containing the true reward of the target policy with a prescribed level of certainty". It uses weighted CP with w(x,y) = dP^π_{Y|X}/dP^{πb}_{Y|X} on the return Y given the initial state x. Several ways of handling the shift are proposed, and "some of these methods yield conformalized intervals with reduced length". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02574)
- On trajectory likelihood ratios: "For large horizons, it becomes quite difficult to learn the ratio of probabilities ... it is likely that the ratio of action probabilities is 0 most of the time, with very few values different from 0 that tend to be extremely [large]" (the fetched text was cut at "extremely"). — [ar5iv text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02574)

**2024–2026 follow-ups (all evaluation-only)**
- **MA-COPP** (Kuipers, Tumu, Yang, Kazemi et al.; CDC 2024; arXiv:2403.16871) is the first conformal off-policy prediction method for multi-agent systems. It gives joint prediction regions for all agents' trajectories when ego agents change policy, and notes that earlier COPP methods are "limited to single-agent systems and scalar outcomes". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16871)
- **PAC Off-Policy Prediction of Contextual Bandits** (Wan, Li & Wu; arXiv:2507.16236, Jul 2025) aims "to further achieve coverage conditional on a given offline data set". It builds probably-approximately-correct (training-conditional) intervals on a PAC-valid conformal framework, with simulations only. This is the OPE analog of BCA's training-conditional guarantee. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.16236)
- **Conformal Prediction Beyond the Horizon** (Gan, Lu, Zhang & Liu; arXiv:2510.26026, Oct 2025) gives infinite-horizon prediction intervals "for returns", on- and off-policy. It combines distributional RL with conformal calibration, pseudo-returns from truncated rollouts, and "time-aware calibration ... using experience replay and weighted subsampling". Its coverage guarantees account for "model misspecification and importance weight estimation". Experiments include Mountain Car. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26026)
- **Taufiq's PhD thesis** (arXiv:2502.06011, Feb 2025) contains three works. The Marginal Ratio OPE estimator reduces variance "by focusing on the marginal distribution of outcomes rather than direct policy shifts". The others are COPP and confounding-robust bounds. The abstract describes no policy-optimization use of COPP. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06011)
- **Lei & Candès** (JRSS-B; arXiv:2006.06138) is the propensity-weighted conformal template for counterfactuals and individual treatment effects that COPP extends. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.06138)

### Inferences
- These intervals measure the spread of individual returns: aleatoric reward and transition noise plus model error. They say nothing directly about how wrong the estimated mean value is. Used as a pessimism signal, they would penalize intrinsic noise, which matches BCA's observation that the width channel penalizes good-but-noisy actions.
- COPP-style weights on (x,y), marginalized over actions, are a variance-reduction device that avoids action-level π/μ. BCA's per-transition residual calibration would need action-level weights at (s,a), which are far more degenerate, and undefined for deterministic TD3+BC/ReBRAC actors against a continuous behavior density.
- As of Oct 2026, using conformal OPE intervals for policy improvement is still an open slot in this literature. The nearest exception, Conformal Policy Control (see Question 2), works in a different setup: risk control relative to a safe reference policy, not interval-based OPE.

### Gaps
- Google Scholar "cited by" lists were not accessible. Follow-ups were found by arXiv full-text API searches ("conformal" combined with off-policy, offline RL, bandit, pessimism or conservatism, and Q-function/critic), so papers that appear only at venues without arXiv versions may be missing.
- I did not extract COPP's interval-length-versus-shift numbers, Foffano et al.'s specific variance-reduction methods, or Zhang–Shi–Luo's exact pseudo-policy construction beyond their introductions.
- OpenReview reviews were not consulted.

## 2. Conformal or calibrated uncertainty used to set pessimism, penalties or trust regions in offline RL and contextual bandits: effects and failure modes

### Takeaway
Direct evidence that conformal calibration improves offline-RL policies is thin and recent:
- **UNIQ** (a single-author ICML 2026 workshop paper) reports D4RL gains over IQL (85.2 vs 81.6 average). It uses a split-conformal quantile of held-out Bellman residuals only as a global scale for ensemble disagreement, which then sets a state-adaptive expectile.
- **Conformal Policy Control** (ICML 2026) uses conformal calibration on safe-policy data to decide how far an optimized policy may deviate from it, with finite-sample risk guarantees and reported performance gains.

The other "conformal RL" papers found are applications (action masks, abstention, CP-based lower confidence bounds) without benchmark evidence. Stronger evidence that uncertainty improves policies comes from non-conformal calibrated mechanisms:
- epistemic ensembles that respond at unlogged actions (PBRL, MSG);
- uncertainty-gated deviation from the behavior policy (SPIBB, deep-SPIBB, HCPI);
- optimizing high-confidence lower bounds on the mean policy value in bandits (CRM, PAC-Bayes, PPL, PUNC).

### Cited Findings
**UNIQ (Upadhyay; arXiv:2606.07592, May 2026; ICML 2026 Workshop on Decision-Making from Offline Datasets to Online Adaptation)**
- Setup: an IQL backbone with an ensemble of value heads at expectiles {0.5, 0.7, 0.9}. The nonconformity score on a held-out calibration split is α_i = |r_i + γ·V̄^{(0.7)}(s'_i) − V̄^{(0.7)}(s_i)|, a Bellman residual like BCA's, and q̂ is its (1−δ)-quantile. Normalized uncertainty is u(s) = σ_ens(s)/(q̂+ε), and τ(s) is a sigmoid of −β_τ(u(s)−1). The value target is V_pess = V̄^{(0.7)} − κ·σ_ens, and the policy is extracted by advantage-weighted regression (AWR). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07592); venue and author from [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07592)
- The author's own caveats: q̂ "serves as an environment-adaptive scale factor rather than a per-state conformal guarantee", and "coverage guarantees hold at calibration time, not throughout training". q̂ stabilizes after about 300K steps, which produces a "late recovery" on hopper-medium-replay at about 700K steps. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07592)
- The model separates epistemic σ_ens (used for pessimism) from aleatoric expectile spread Δ_τ, which is "used as a diagnostic signal" only. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07592)
- Results (Table 1): the D4RL MuJoCo 9-task average is 85.2 vs IQL 81.6, TD3+BC 76.4, CQL 78.3, ReBRAC 89.7 and EDAC 92.9. Baselines are copied from published reports (CORL) rather than re-run, and UNIQ uses seeds 0–2. Per-task gains over IQL range from +0.1 to +8.1, for example hopper-medium 75.6 vs 67.5, walker2d-medium-replay 89.4 vs 82.2 and hopper-medium-replay 101.6 vs 97.4. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07592)
- Ablation (Table 3, seed 0 only, 4 tasks): full UNIQ averages 59.4. Without conformal normalization (raw σ) it averages 45.2, with hopper-medium-replay collapsing to 16.1 vs 59.3. Fixed τ gives 49.1, κ = 0 gives 56.1, and an ensemble of 5 gives 47.2 because it "over-penalizes replay states". "No single fixed κ is globally optimal", so κ is set per task (0 for hopper-medium-replay, 0.5 elsewhere), "selected per-task offline using held-out dataset statistics". — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07592)
- Credibility flag (inference from the reported numbers): the seed-0 ablation score for full UNIQ on hopper-medium-replay (59.3) cannot be reconciled with a 3-seed mean of 101.6. The other two seeds would need to average about 122.8, above typical Hopper normalized scores. The Table 1 caption also reads "UNIQ scores on best ;", which suggests best-run or best-configuration reporting. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07592)
- UNIQ cites "S. Park and Y. Sung (2023) Confidence-aware offline reinforcement learning via conformal prediction". I could not find this paper through arXiv full-text API search or web search, so treat it as unverified. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07592)

**Conformal Policy Control (Prinster, Fannjiang, Park, Cho, Liu; ICML 2026; arXiv:2603.02196)**
- The method "use[s] any safe reference policy as a probabilistic regulator for any optimized but untested policy. Conformal calibration on data from the safe policy determines how aggressively the new policy can act, while provably enforcing the user's declared risk tolerance."
- It claims finite-sample guarantees "even for non-monotonic bounded loss functions" and does not assume a correct model class or tuned hyperparameters.
- The v4 comment refers to a "likelihood-ratio control parameter" and a constrained policy π_t^(β).
- Experiments range from question answering to biomolecular engineering and show that safe exploration "can also improve performance". The setting is exploration and deployment, not D4RL. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02196)

**Other conformal-in-decision papers**
- **PlanCP** (Sun, Jiang, Qiu, Nobel, Kochenderfer, Schwager; NeurIPS 2023) applies CP to a diffusion dynamics model and adds a quantile term to its training loss. Maze2D average is 125.3 vs Diffuser 119.5 (IQL 47.0, CQL 7.7, BCQ 9.1 as listed). It claims lower uncertainty "without compromising performance". — [NeurIPS paper](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/fe318a2b6c699808019a456b706cd845-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- **HACO** (Basu, Patel, Sheth, Muralidharan; arXiv:2509.09772, 2025) uses 2.77M decisions from 168,126 Medicaid patients. It "(i) trains a lightweight risk model for adverse events, (ii) derives a conformal threshold to mask unsafe actions at a target risk level, and (iii) learns a preference policy on the resulting safe subset", and is evaluated only with fitted-Q evaluation (FQE). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09772)
- The follow-up **FG-FARL** (arXiv:2509.09655) compares against BC and HACO and "achieves comparable value to baselines while improving fairness metrics". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09655)
- **ConformalDQN** (Eghbali, Alhanai, Ghassemi; arXiv:2412.12597, Dec 2024) adds a conformal predictor and a composite loss to Double DQN on MIMIC-IV data, enabling "uncertainty-aware action selection ... avoid potentially harmful actions in unfamiliar states". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12597)
- **NeuroSep-CP-LCB** (Zhou, Beyah, Kamaleswaran; arXiv:2503.16708, 2025) uses conformal intervals as lower confidence bounds in a neural contextual bandit for sepsis. The abstract reports no regret numbers. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.16708)
- **Conformal Bandits** (Cuonzo & Deliu; arXiv:2512.09850, Dec 2025) is an online method. It reports a regret advantage under "weak arm separability" in portfolio allocation, and nominal coverage "where classical UCB policies may fail". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.09850)
- **ACP-UCB1** (Du & Xu; arXiv:2605.07115, May 2026) targets each arm's upper conformal endpoint F^{-1}(1−α/2). The authors note this "can rank arms differently from their means". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07115)
- **Decision-calibrated conformal** (Shekhar & Howard; arXiv:2606.10187, Jun 2026) measures forecast error "by its largest impact on the policies that could actually be deployed". They prove it is "the smallest valid uncertainty measure that uniformly protects all deployable pacing policies", and that "traditional residual calibration can be arbitrarily more conservative by paying for nuisance ... dimensions".
  - Uncertainty radii fall from 7236.7 to 18.4 on Criteo and from 4629.4 to 278.6 on KuaiRand.
  - On Criteo, the held-out any-violation rate falls from 16.7% to 3.3%. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10187)
- **Inverse conformal risk control** (Zhou & Zhu; arXiv:2510.07750, 2025) gives distribution-free finite-sample guarantees "on both miscoverage and regret" for families of robust policies. It traces the miscoverage–regret Pareto frontier so the robustness level can be calibrated rather than fixed a priori. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07750)

**Calibrated or ensemble pessimism in offline RL (non-conformal)**
- **PBRL** (Bai, Wang, Yang, Deng, Garg, Liu, Wang; ICLR 2022; arXiv:2202.11566) penalizes the value function by the disagreement of a bootstrapped Q-ensemble, plus a "novel OOD sampling method".
  - Theory: "such OOD sampling and pessimistic bootstrapping yields a provable uncertainty quantifier in linear MDPs". Theorem 1 needs the OOD target y = TV, which "is impossible to obtain in practice".
  - D4RL Gym 15-task averages: PBRL 74.37 and PBRL-Prior 76.66, vs TD3-BC 67.55, CQL 67.35, MOPO 53.3, UWAC 50.41 and BEAR 38.78.
  - Unseen actions near the data get a small penalty, so they can still be chosen. — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.11566)
- **MSG** (Ghasemipour, Gu, Nachum; NeurIPS 2022; arXiv:2205.13703) shows that ensembles sharing pessimistic targets can produce "effectively optimistic" lower-confidence-bound values. Its fix is independent targets with Q_LCB = mean − β·std.
  - Antmaze-large-play: 72.6 ± 7.0 vs IQL 39.6 and CQL 15.8. Antmaze-large-diverse: 71.4 ± 12.2 vs 47.5 and 14.9. The CQL antmaze numbers could not be reproduced by the authors.
  - In Gym with 4 members, shared-target LCB underperforms MSG and even a single network beats it. Efficient ensemble approximations do not match separate networks. — [NeurIPS paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/7423902b5534e2b267438c85444a54b1-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- **CCVL** (Hong, Kumar, Levine; ICLR 2023; arXiv:2212.04607) learns Q-values conditioned on a confidence level δ, giving "conservative estimates of the true value at any desired confidence". The level is chosen adaptively online, and the method "outperforms existing conservative offline RL algorithms on multiple discrete control domains" (Atari). — [OpenReview PDF](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=mdYvaTs4gr); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04607)
- **Cal-QL** (Nakamoto, Zhai, Singh, Sobol Mark et al.; NeurIPS 2023; arXiv:2303.05479) defines "calibration" as Q-values that lower-bound the learned policy's true value and upper-bound "some other (suboptimal) reference policy, which may simply be the behavior policy". It is a one-line change to CQL aimed at online fine-tuning; the abstract claims it "outperforms state-of-the-art methods on 9/…" tasks, and the denominator was cut off in the fetched text. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05479)
- **Deep-SPIBB** (Brandfonbrener, Tachet des Combes, Laroche; arXiv:2206.01085, 2022) uses uncertainty as a constraint in the policy-improvement step, falling back to the behavior policy where uncertain, rather than as a value penalty.
  - "deep-SPIBB consistently outperforms pessimism when given access to the same imperfect uncertainty estimators" (cartpole, catch, Atari).
  - "By defaulting to the behavior policy, deep-SPIBB can at least recover the performance of the behavior simply by tuning ε, no matter the quality of the uncertainty estimates."
  - Every method compared except pessimism reduces to a behavior-cloning variant at one extreme of its hyperparameter. — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.01085)
- **SPIBB** (Laroche, Trichelair, Tachet des Combes; ICML 2019 long oral; arXiv:1712.06924) "bootstraps the trained policy with the baseline when the uncertainty is high". Its Π_b-SPIBB variant has safe-policy-improvement guarantees. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.06924)
- **HCPI**, "High Confidence Policy Improvement" (ICML 2015, PMLR 37; authors not captured from the page; I believe they are Thomas, Theocharous & Ghavamzadeh): the user picks a performance lower bound and confidence level, and the algorithm returns a policy below the bound with probability at most that level, with "no hyper-parameter that requires expert tuning". — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v37/thomas15.html)
- **SCORE** (Deng, Fu, Wang, Yang et al.; arXiv:2110.12468; venue not verified) states that pessimism "fails in practice ... due to the inability to obtain high-quality estimations of uncertainty". It adds an annealed BC regularizer so uncertainty estimates are good early in training, and claims state-of-the-art D4RL results with 3.1× speed-up. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.12468v2); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.12468)
- **Lu, Ball, Parker-Holder, Osborne et al.** (ICLR 2022 spotlight; arXiv:2110.04135) describe a "breakdown between theory and practice": pessimism should be bounded by the model's total-variation error but is implemented with uncertainty-heuristic penalties. Bayesian-optimized hyperparameters are "vastly different" from hand-tuned ones and give "drastically stronger performance". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04135)

**Contextual bandits: optimizing calibrated lower bounds on the mean value**
- **CRM/POEM** (Swaminathan & Joachims; arXiv:1502.02362; ICML 2015 from memory): generalization bounds "that account for the variance of the propensity-weighted empirical risk estimator" yield the CRM principle and the POEM algorithm. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.02362)
- **Bayesian CRM** (London & Sandler; arXiv:1806.11500) gives a PAC-Bayes bound for truncated IPS. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.11500)
- **Sakhi, Alquier, Chopin** (ICML 2023): tractable PAC-Bayes bounds, "tighter than their competitors", that "can be optimized directly to confidently improve upon the logging policy offline", with no tuning on held-out sets. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/sakhi23a.html)
- **Kuzborskij, Vernade, György, Szepesvári** (AISTATS 2021) build a self-normalized importance-weighting lower bound for off-policy selection. It is "superior to its main competitors, both in terms of tightness of the confidence intervals and the quality of the policies chosen". — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v130/kuzborskij21a.html)
- **PPL** (Jin, Ren, Yang, Wang; to appear in Annals of Statistics; arXiv:2212.09900) "optimizes lower confidence bounds (LCBs) — instead of point estimates — of the policy values". Its suboptimality "only depends on (i) the overlap for the optimal policy, and (ii) the complexity of the policy class". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09900)
- **PUNC** (Li, Ma, Srebro; NeurIPS 2022; arXiv:2205.10671) uses ℓ∞ confidence-set pessimism and is "adaptively minimax optimal" across ℓq-constrained classes up to log d. — [NeurIPS paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/8443219a991f068c34d9491ad68ffa94-Paper-Conference.pdf)

### Inferences
Mechanisms ranked by strength of evidence that calibrated uncertainty positively improves the policy:
1. **Calibrate the deviation budget, not a per-action width.** Use held-out data to set how far the learned policy may depart from the behavior or a safe reference: Conformal Policy Control's β, SPIBB/deep-SPIBB's ε, and HCPI's accept/reject. This has the best guarantees, reports improvements, and is robust to poor uncertainty estimates (deep-SPIBB). It is also the published analog of BCA's working "deviation gate".
2. **Optimize a calibrated lower bound on the mean policy value** (CRM, PAC-Bayes, PPL, PUNC, the self-normalized lower bound). The evidence is theoretical and empirical in bandits. The bound is on the mean value, not on individual outcomes.
3. **Epistemic penalties that are defined at unlogged actions**: PBRL with OOD sampling, MSG with independent ensembles. D4RL evidence is strong, but they are not conformal. Conformal calibration could be layered on, for example by setting β in mean − β·std on held-out data.
4. **A conformal quantile as a scale normalizer for an epistemic signal** (UNIQ's q̂ dividing σ_ens, which drives state-adaptive conservatism). There is one workshop paper, 3 seeds, a single-seed ablation and an internal inconsistency, so the evidence is weak but the result is directionally positive.
5. **Decision-focused nonconformity scores** (Shekhar & Howard): score the error in what matters for the deploy decision, such as advantage or ranking against candidate actions, instead of the raw residual. Strong evidence outside RL; untested in offline RL.

Failure modes reported:
- over-penalization: UNIQ without conformal normalization, or with a 5-member ensemble;
- shared-target ensembles becoming optimistic (MSG);
- uncertainty heuristics that do not track the quantity the theory needs (Lu et al.);
- pessimism that collapses with poor uncertainty estimates (SCORE, deep-SPIBB);
- only marginal value gains from conformal action masks (FG-FARL vs HACO).

### Gaps
- I found no D4RL-scale paper that uses conformal widths directly as a critic penalty or a BC multiplier (BCA's configuration) and reports gains.
- Conformal Policy Control's quantitative results and exact β mechanism (only the abstract and v4 comment were read), CCVL's Atari numbers and Cal-QL's full numbers were not extracted.
- The Park & Sung (2023) paper could not be found.
- HACO and ConformalDQN report only offline (FQE) evaluation; there is no online ground truth.

## 3. Pessimism theory: what an uncertainty quantifier must satisfy, whether conformal outcome intervals qualify, and epistemic versus aleatoric calibration

### Takeaway
Pessimism theory requires a high-probability bound on the error of the estimated Bellman backup, which is a conditional mean. The bound must hold uniformly over all (s,a), including actions the data never took, and the resulting suboptimality bound pays 2·Σ E_{π*}[Γ] along the optimal policy's path. A split-conformal interval covers individual noisy outcomes, marginally over the logged distribution. That fails the "for all (s,a)" requirement at unlogged actions. Where noise is large, the interval is much wider than the mean error and does not shrink with n. Distribution-free methods cannot deliver shrinking pointwise mean-error bounds (Barber 2020), so the epistemic part must come from structure such as ensembles, features or counts. Several papers separate epistemic from aleatoric uncertainty: UNIQ, UACQR, 1R2R and Valdettaro et al.

### Cited Findings
- **Jin, Yang & Wang** (ICML 2021; arXiv:2012.15085), Definition 4.1 (ξ-uncertainty quantifier): {Γ_h} qualifies if the event E = {|(B̂_h V̂_{h+1})(x,a) − (B_h V̂_{h+1})(x,a)| ≤ Γ_h(x,a) for all (x,a) ∈ S×A, h ∈ [H]} has P_D(E) ≥ 1 − ξ. — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2012.15085)
- Jin et al., Theorem 4.2: if Γ is a ξ-uncertainty quantifier, then SubOpt(Pess(D); x) ≤ 2·Σ_h E_{π*}[Γ_h(s_h,a_h) | s_1 = x], with the expectation taken over trajectories of π*. Pessimism "eliminate[s] the spurious correlation". In linear MDPs PEVI "matches the information-theoretic lower bound up to multiplicative factors of the dimension and horizon", and the "intrinsic uncertainty" term is "impossible" to eliminate. — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2012.15085)
- **Xie, Cheng, Jiang, Mineiro et al.** (NeurIPS 2021 oral; arXiv:2106.06926): "overly pessimistic reasoning can be equally damaging in precluding the discovery of good policies, which is an issue for the popular bonus-based pessimism". Instead of a pointwise lower bound, pessimism is applied at the initial state over the set of Bellman-consistent functions. Under linear MDPs this gives "a factor of O(d) improvement" over Jin et al. when the action set is small. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06926); [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.06926)
- **Rashidinejad, Zhu, Ma, Jiao et al.** (NeurIPS 2021 and IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory; arXiv:2103.12021) define a weak concentrability coefficient "that measures the deviation from the behavior policy to the expert policy alone". They study the finite-sample properties and information-theoretic limits of a lower-confidence-bound algorithm in multi-armed bandits, contextual bandits and MDPs, and it interpolates between imitation learning and offline RL. The specific count-based penalty form is from memory and not verified. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.12021)
- **SCORE** restates Jin et al.: suboptimality is bounded by Σ 2γ^t E_{π*}[U(s,a)|s_0], "so the remaining issue is finding a sufficiently small ξ-uncertainty quantifier". — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.12468v2)
- **PBRL**: bootstrapped-ensemble standard deviation scales like the lower-confidence-bound penalty in linear MDPs, but it is a valid ξ-quantifier only with OOD sampling and an idealized OOD target. — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.11566)
- **Barber** (arXiv:2004.09477; venue not verified) proves an "explicit lower bound on the length of any distribution-free confidence interval" for the label probability conditional on features. The bound "is independent of the sample size and holds for all distributions with no point masses", so no distribution-free procedure can "be adaptive with respect to any type of special structure". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.09477)
- **UACQR** (Rossellini, Barber, Willett; arXiv:2306.08693; venue not verified): conformalized quantile regression (CQR) intervals "do not distinguish between ... aleatoric uncertainty ... and ... epistemic uncertainty", so they can be "overly narrow in regions where epistemic uncertainty is high". UACQR separates the two sources. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08693)
- **Kiyani, Pappas, Roth, Hassani** (arXiv:2502.02561, Feb 2025; venue not verified): "prediction sets are optimal for decision makers who wish to optimize their value at risk", "a simple max-min decision policy is optimal for risk-averse decision makers", and they derive a Risk-Averse Calibration (RAC) algorithm. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02561)
- **1R2R** (Rigter, Lacerda, Hawes; NeurIPS 2023; arXiv:2212.00124) is risk-averse to both epistemic uncertainty (it avoids OOD regions) and aleatoric uncertainty, which "discourages actions that may result in poor outcomes due to environment stochasticity". It is competitive on deterministic D4RL and best on static CVaR_0.1 in stochastic Currency Exchange and HIV tasks. For D4RL it is tuned for expected return. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.00124); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.00124v2)
- **Valdettaro et al.** (PMLR 244, UAI 2024 from memory) disentangle aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty in finite offline MDPs via the law of total variance over posterior samples, then optimize the posterior expected value. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v244/valdettaro24a.html)
- Conformal targets rank actions by tail quantiles rather than means (ACP-UCB1), and residual calibration "pays for nuisance" dimensions (Shekhar & Howard). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07115); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10187)

### Inferences
- A split-conformal threshold q on |y − Q(s,a)|, with y = r + γV(s'), is at least the (1−α) quantile of |noise + bias|. Jin's Γ only needs to bound |B̂V − BV|, the error of the conditional mean. Where noise is present, q is therefore a valid but loose bound whose excess, roughly the noise quantile, does not vanish as n grows. Plugged into Theorem 4.2, it gives a suboptimality bound that does not go to zero and systematically disfavors high-variance actions. In other words, it turns pessimism into a value-at-risk objective, which matches Kiyani et al. This matches BCA's observation that the width penalizes good-but-noisy actions.
- Marginal coverage over logged (s,a) pairs is not the "for all (x,a)" event Jin et al. require. Unlogged actions, where the policy actually goes, are where Γ must be large, and conformal coverage on held-out logged transitions says nothing there. PBRL needed explicit OOD sampling to get a valid quantifier for exactly this reason. This is BCA's "width only at logged actions" finding.
- Two forms are closer to what the theory needs. One is Bellman-consistent or policy-level pessimism (Xie et al.; HCPI; PPL), which needs only a lower bound on the value of the candidate policy, not pointwise bounds. The other is calibrating an epistemic quantity's scale against mean-level held-out error.
- BCA's Dirichlet/Bayesian-bootstrap machinery naturally yields a posterior for a mean (Rubin's Bayesian bootstrap of a mean) as well as for a quantile. It could therefore be pointed at mean-level targets, such as the mean held-out advantage of rare deviation actions within a cell, rather than the (1−α) quantile of individual residuals. This is an untested design idea.

### Gaps
- I found no paper that formally proves conformal residual intervals are or are not ξ-uncertainty quantifiers. The looseness and non-vanishing-bias argument above is my inference from Jin et al.'s definition and theorem.
- I found no offline-RL paper that calibrates the error of the conditional mean (rather than individual outcomes) on held-out data and uses it for pessimism on D4RL.
- Venues for Barber (2020), UACQR and Kiyani et al. were not verified from source pages.

## 4. Policy-induced distribution shift: weighted conformal prediction with policy ratios, and when weighting helps or hurts

### Takeaway
Weighted CP restores marginal coverage under known or accurately estimated shift, but it has four costs:
- its effective sample size shrinks with weight dispersion;
- the test point's own weight mass can push the threshold to infinity;
- with estimated weights, coverage degrades by ½·E|ŵ − w| and needs overlap;
- long horizons and near-deterministic target policies make action-level ratios degenerate.

The literature responds with outcome-marginal weights (COPP), pseudo-policy subsampling (Zhang–Shi–Luo, Gan et al.), feedback-shift corrections (Fannjiang et al.), and calibrating a likelihood-ratio-controlled interpolation toward a safe policy (Conformal Policy Control).

### Cited Findings
- **Tibshirani, Barber, Candès, Ramdas** (NeurIPS 2019; arXiv:1904.06019): weighted CP works when the likelihood ratio is "known — or, in practice, can be estimated accurately with access to a large set of unlabeled data". Scores are weighted with p_i^w(x) = w(X_i)/(Σ_j w(X_j) + w(x)), plus a mass p_{n+1}^w(x) = w(x)/(Σ_j w(X_j) + w(x)) placed at +∞. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06019); [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.06019)
- In their experiment, weighting restored average coverage to 90.8%, but the coverage histogram is "more dispersed ... because ... we are relying on a reduced 'effective sample size'", n̂ = (Σ|w(X_i)|)²/Σ|w(X_i)|². — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.06019)
- **COPP**: the coverage gap from estimated weights is Δ_w = ½·E|ŵ − w|, overlap is required, and failure without overlap is shown in App. D.1.2. Unweighted CP under-covers increasingly as policy shift grows. Outcome-marginal weights w(x,y) also let COPP handle stochastic target policies, whereas COPP notes that [14] (apparently Lei & Candès) "assume a deterministic target policy". — [NeurIPS paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/cc84bfabe6389d8883fc2071c848f62a-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- **Foffano et al.**: per-step ratio products are mostly 0 with rare extreme values at long horizons, and "this method still suffers from a large variance". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02574)
- **Zhang–Shi–Luo**: pseudo-policy subsampling plus weighted CP gives asymptotic validity, and exact coverage when the behavior policy is known. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v206/zhang23c.html)
- **Gan et al. 2025**: "weighted subsampling" and experience replay "restore approximate exchangeability", with coverage guarantees that account for importance-weight estimation. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26026)
- **Taufiq's Marginal Ratio estimator**: reweighting on the outcome marginal rather than the policy ratio reduces variance in contextual bandits. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06011)
- **Barber, Candès, Ramdas, Tibshirani**, "Conformal prediction beyond exchangeability" (arXiv:2202.13415; Annals of Statistics from memory) extend CP to data-drift settings and to nonsymmetric algorithms. I saw only the abstract; the form of their coverage-gap bound is not verified here. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13415)
- **Yang, Kuchibhotla, Tchetgen Tchetgen** (arXiv:2203.01761): doubly robust, semiparametrically efficient calibration of prediction sets under covariate shift. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.01761)
- **Qiu, Dobriban, Tchetgen Tchetgen** (arXiv:2203.06126): prediction sets adaptive to unknown covariate shift. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.06126)
- **Fannjiang, Bates, Angelopoulos et al.** (arXiv:2202.03613; PNAS 2022 from memory) treat "feedback covariate shift", where test data are "chosen based on" the training data and are therefore statistically dependent on it, and give CP that is valid in this setting. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.03613)
- **Conformal Policy Control** calibrates on data from the safe reference policy, uses a "likelihood-ratio control parameter", and guarantees the user's risk tolerance for the constrained new policy. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02196)

### Inferences
- From the formula in Tibshirani et al.: the weighted (1−α) quantile is +∞ whenever p_{n+1}^w(x) > α, that is, whenever the test point's own weight exceeds α/(1−α) of the calibration weight total. In WBCP terms, with uniform Dirichlet masses replaced by weights, the extra atom at the worst-case loss gets a large expected share. With β = 0.95, the threshold blows up even sooner. Weighting therefore hurts most exactly where the learned policy departs furthest from the data, which is where a decision signal is needed.
- For deterministic actors (TD3+BC, ReBRAC), π/μ at the action level is singular against a continuous μ, and smoothing changes the target. Workable options from the literature are outcome-marginal or state-marginal weights (COPP, Marginal Ratio) or an explicit mixture or likelihood-ratio-constrained policy (Conformal Policy Control's β). In the last case the policy's departure from the reference is the controlled quantity, so the weights stay bounded by construction. That last point is my reading of the abstract and v4 comment, not verified in their theorems.
- If held-out residuals shape the policy (a penalty or BC multiplier), the test distribution comes to depend on the calibration data. That is a feedback-type violation in the spirit of Fannjiang et al., which concerns dependence on training data, so a fresh calibration split is needed for any coverage claim after policy updates.
- Reported evidence on when weighting helps: under correct weights and good overlap, it fixes the under-coverage of unweighted CP (COPP Table 2; Tibshirani et al.). It hurts through the lower effective sample size, weight-estimation error (Δ_w), lack of overlap, and long horizons. None of the papers reports an effect of weighting on policy quality, as opposed to coverage.

### Gaps
- I found no empirical study comparing weighted and unweighted conformal calibration as an input to policy improvement in offline RL.
- I did not extract quantitative effective-sample-size or interval-length results from COPP App. D.1.2 or from Foffano et al.
- Conformal Policy Control's exact weighting and guarantee conditions were not read beyond the abstract and comments.

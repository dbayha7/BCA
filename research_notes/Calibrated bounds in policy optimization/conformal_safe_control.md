# Conformal prediction and distribution-free quantile bounds in safe / risk-sensitive control and RL

Scope note: compiled 14 Oct 2026 from primary sources read in full text (arXiv HTML/ar5iv/PMLR/NeurIPS PDFs) unless flagged. "Marginal" = probability over the joint draw of calibration data and test point; "PAC / training-conditional / dataset-conditional" = holds with probability 1-beta over the calibration draw. Numbers are as reported by the authors.

## Q1. Conformal prediction (CP) for safe planning, control, shields and filters: what guarantee, and what effect on task performance vs. uncalibrated baselines?

### Takeaway
Almost all CP planners/filters give a *marginal* probability-of-safety guarantee that holds only under exchangeability between calibration and deployment (often requiring that the robot not change the other agents' behavior) plus recursive feasibility; adaptive-CP (ACI-style) variants give only long-run average coverage. Against unsafe learned policies they cut violations at a modest performance cost (e.g., GA3C collisions 49 to 10 per 1,000 runs at +10% time-to-goal); against Gaussian, bootstrap or worst-case uncertainty they usually *reduce* conservatism, sometimes at a cost in MPC feasibility.

### Cited Findings
**Lindemann et al., "Safe Planning in Dynamic Environments using Conformal Prediction" (arXiv:2210.10254; IEEE RA-L 2023)**
- Guarantee: split CP on trajectory-predictor errors yields regions with P(||Y_tau - Yhat_tau|t|| <= C_tau|t) >= 1 - delta_bar; an MPC that avoids these regions satisfies P(c(x_t, Y_t) >= 0 for all t) >= 1 - delta *assuming recursive feasibility*; the multi-step guarantee uses a union bound (delta_bar = delta/T) — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.10254v2)
- Assumption: the agent-trajectory distribution D does not depend on the robot (no interaction); authors suggest robust CP for f-divergence shifts — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.10254v2)
- ORCA pedestrians: |D_cal| = 2,000, delta = 0.05, T = 20; 498/500 test trajectories inside regions; closed-loop (receding-horizon) MPC average cost 367.1 vs. 414 for open-loop over 500 test trajectories — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.10254v2)
- CARLA intersection: |D_cal| = 1,500; because of limited calibration data they used T = 30 (delta_bar = 0.05/30); 1/100 MPC runs violated the constraint; one-step regions covered 99.985% vs. the required 99.83%, i.e., over-coverage that the authors attribute to union-bound conservatism. They also added slack variables (for feasibility) and used the smallest kappa-step region across t — practical modifications outside the theorem — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.10254v2)
- No uncalibrated-predictor baseline was reported; the only performance comparison is closed-loop vs. open-loop *within* CP — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.10254v2)

**Dixit et al., "Adaptive Conformal Prediction for Motion Planning among Dynamic Agents" (L4DC 2023, PMLR 211)**
- Guarantee: adaptive/fully-adaptive CP (Gibbs & Candès) gives "on-average coverage guarantees, albeit weaker than the point-wise guarantees" of standard CP; safety theorem again assumes recursive MPC feasibility — [Source](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v211/dixit23a/dixit23a.pdf)
- Drone dodging a Frisbee, 1,000 Monte Carlo runs per delta. At delta = 0.025: ACP feasibility 83.8%, success 99.2%, mean min-distance 2.91 vs. bootstrap-variance method (Wei et al. 2022) 87.4% / 100% / 14.2 and EKF-Gaussian 97.1% / 100% / 5.27. At delta = 0.05: ACP 80.9% / 100% / 2.74 vs. 90.3% / 100% / 4.97 and 97.6% / 100% / 4.25 — [Source](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v211/dixit23a/dixit23a.pdf)
- Authors: ACP "avoids the conservatism of other approaches" but MPC feasibility is worse when uncertainty sets jump (large learning rates); small learning rates gave empirical miscoverage of only 0.94-2.36% vs. a 10% target (over-coverage) — [Source](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v211/dixit23a/dixit23a.pdf)

**Strawn, Ayanian, Lindemann, "Conformal Predictive Safety Filter for RL Controllers in Dynamic Environments" (arXiv:2306.02551, 2023; RA-L per my knowledge, not verified in text)**
- Learned safety filter that imitates a pre-trained RL policy while avoiding CP regions (delta = 0.01, 1,000 calibration trajectories, 20,000 training trajectories) — [Source](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.02551)
- N = 4 agents, 1,000 runs: GA3C (RL) 49 collisions / 14 timeouts / 7.503 s to goal; CPSF-GA3C 10 / 8 / 8.278 s; ORCA 3 / 27 / 8.678 s; CPSF-ORCA 2 / 15 / 8.412 s. Counts are raw numbers; 10 collisions is consistent with 1% per run at delta = 0.01 — [Source](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.02551)
- Headline claims: 80% fewer collisions with GA3C, 67% fewer failures with ORCA (the N = 4 table shows 27 to 15, i.e., about -44%; the 67% appears to be an average over agent counts), and 18% shorter time than a Gaussian-uncertainty filter (CPSF 8.278 s with 99% of positions inside intervals vs. GASF 10.05 s with 100%) — [Source](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.02551)

**Lekeufack, Angelopoulos, Bajcsy, Jordan, Malik, "Conformal Decision Theory" (arXiv:2310.05921, 2023; ICRA 2024 per my knowledge)**
- Calibrates the *decision* parameter lambda directly (lambda_{t+1} = lambda_t + eta(epsilon - loss_t)); guarantee: long-run average realized loss <= epsilon + O(1/T) for arbitrary (even adversarial) sequences, under an "eventually safe" assumption; a batch version (conformal risk control) gives E[loss] <= epsilon on exchangeable held-out decision problems and is explicitly proposed for offline RL, but requires counterfactual evaluation of the loss for each lambda — [Source](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.05921)
- Results summarized under Q4 (faster than prediction-set ACI planning and safe).

**Muthali et al., "Multi-Agent Reachability Calibration with Conformal Prediction" (arXiv:2304.00432, 2023; CDC 2023 per my knowledge)**
- Online RollingRC calibration of quantile-regression control-interval predictions, propagated with HJ reachability; target total miscoverage gamma = 0.05 over the N = 3 nearest agents — [Source](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.00432)
- Without CP (Appendix C), quantile-regression coverage falls from 0.968 to 0.800 over 0.5-3 s horizons vs. 0.997-0.965 with CP, with calibrated sets about 1.5-1.7x larger (e.g., 3,885 vs. 2,300 m^2 at 3 s) — [Source](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.00432)

**Tabbara, Yang, Sibai, "Statistically Assuring Safety of Control Systems using Ensembles of Safety Filters and Conformal Prediction" (arXiv:2511.07899, 2025)**
- Phase 1: one-sided CP lower bound on the learned HJ value decides when to switch from a nominal (reward-seeking) controller to a learned safe policy; Phase 2: trajectory-level CP certificate on the deployment distribution, P(J^pi(x0) > 0) ~ Beta(N_cert - k, k + 1) — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07899v2)
- Prior HJ filters switch at a hand-picked threshold epsilon "without formal guidance on how to choose it" — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07899v2)
- Highway overtaking, |D_cal| = 10,000, alpha = 0.06, 50 trials: nominal only 46% violations / 54% success; CP-switched single models 2-22% violations / 78-98% success; ensemble 4% / 96% — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07899v2)
- Caveat: a more conservative switch (largest qhat = 2.37) produced *more* violations (22%) because switching earlier put the learned safe policy in out-of-distribution states — calibration-induced deployment shift — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07899v2)

**Other shields/filters (2024-2026)**
- Perceive with Confidence (Dixit et al., CoRL 2024): CP-calibrated perception plus any safe planner gives an end-to-end statistical safety assurance; "up to 40% improvements in empirical safety compared to baselines" (abstract-level claim) — [Source](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v270/dixit25a.html)
- Conformal Safety Shielding (Scarbro et al., arXiv:2506.17275, 2025): an action is allowed only if safe for every state in the CP set. At 95% coverage the conformal shield beat the uncalibrated baseline on success with lower failure probability; at 99% / 99.5% coverage, larger sets created "stuck" states (empty shield) and success fell below the baseline — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.17275v2)
- FORCE-OPT (Chakraborty et al., arXiv:2507.22389, 2025): CP-calibrated GMM reachable sets as a plan-safety monitor (numbers under Q4) — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.22389v1)

**Conformal predictive programming (CPP; Zhao, Yu, Sesia, Deshmukh, Lindemann; arXiv:2402.07407, 2024)**
- Replaces a chance constraint by an empirical-quantile constraint on an optimization dataset (K samples), then certifies on an independent calibration set (L samples): a-posteriori *marginal* guarantee, or *conditional (PAC)* guarantee through a quantile shift delta* = 1 - S/(L+1) + sqrt(ln(1/beta)/(2L)); for some problem classes it inherits a-priori guarantees from sample-average approximation (SAA) — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.07407)
- Versus scenario approach (SA): CPP certificates do not depend on the number of decision variables or on support-constraint analysis, at the cost of a second dataset — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.07407)
- Non-convex case (K = L = 200, delta = 0.1): empirical coverage 0.90 / 0.89; solving with the conditional level is "more costly" than with the marginal level (histograms only). Computing SA's a-posteriori level took 10.26-10.29 s vs. 0.18-6.21 s CPP solve times. Under distribution shift, plain CPP covered 0.80 vs. 0.96 for robust CPP (target 0.90) — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.07407)
- I found no head-to-head optimal-cost comparison of CPP vs. SA or SAA at matched violation in the text.

### Inferences
- The safety guarantees are only as good as (a) exchangeability between calibration and closed-loop deployment and (b) recursive feasibility. Both are routinely patched in practice (slack variables, clipped ACP, minimum regions), so empirical violation rates, not theorems, are the evidence.
- The recurring architecture "nominal/learned policy + calibrated gate + fallback" (Tabbara 2025; Strawn 2023; Park 2021 under Q3) is the closest analogue to BCA's deviation gate. Its main documented failure mode is that the gate itself changes the state distribution, which argues for weighting or re-certifying on the gated deployment distribution (Tabbara's Phase 2).
- Over-coverage is common (CARLA 99.985% vs. 99.83%; ACP 1-2% vs. 10%), i.e., practical CP pipelines tend to be conservative relative to target, chiefly because of union bounds over time and agents.

### Gaps
- No ORCA/CARLA comparison against an *uncalibrated* LSTM planner is reported by Lindemann et al., so the safety benefit of calibration there is not quantified.
- Venues for Strawn (RA-L), Muthali (CDC 2023) and CDT (ICRA 2024) are from my knowledge, not verified in fetched text; the Lindemann RA-L 2023 venue is as given in the task.
- Not fetched (leads): Lindemann et al. tutorial "Formal verification and control with conformal prediction" (2024, cited by Tabbara 2025); Tonkens et al. (cited by PlanCP); Cleaveland et al. LCP time-series regions (AAAI 2024); Stamouli et al. shrinking-horizon CP-MPC (L4DC 2024); Yu et al. STL control with CP. Titles and venues are from memory and unverified.

## Q2. Chance-constrained, quantile or CVaR objectives in RL where conformal or distribution-free quantile bounds optimize or constrain the policy: reported return vs. violation trade-offs

### Takeaway
Direct use of distribution-free quantile bounds *inside* RL policy optimization is rare. The clearest case is chance-constrained policy optimization, which tunes constraint backoffs to the least conservative value whose Clopper-Pearson (PAC) lower bound meets the target. Most "conformal RL" work instead applies CP at execution time (shields, filters, CBF constraints) or produces off-policy evaluation intervals, and reports return/cost mostly in figures without isolating the calibration step.

### Cited Findings
- **Chance Constrained Policy Optimization (CCPO; Petsagkourakis, Sandoval, Bradford, Galvanin, Zhang, del Rio-Chanona; arXiv:2008.00030)**: backoffs b tighten the constraints inside a policy-gradient objective. The joint-satisfaction probability uses a Clopper-Pearson lower bound F_lb on the empirical CDF (confidence 1-epsilon), and Bayesian optimization finds the smallest backoffs with F_lb >= 1-alpha. Authors: "the best trade-off is realized if F_lb is as close as possible to 1-alpha"; larger backoffs make the policy "over-conservative and mitigate performance" — [Source](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10143173/1/2008.00030v2.pdf)
- CCPO results (1,000 Monte Carlo closed-loop trajectories): case 1 (alpha = 0.01, confidence 99%), without backoffs "almost 50% of the constraints are violated" vs. all satisfied with backoffs; case 2 (F_lb target 0.95), 24% satisfaction without tightening vs. 97% with it. The objective cost of tightening is not quantified in the text I read — [Source](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10143173/1/2008.00030v2.pdf)
- **Safe Flow Q-Learning (SafeFQL; arXiv:2603.15136, 2026), offline safe RL**: an HJ-style safety value with a one-step flow policy, plus a CP calibration step that selects the safety level delta* with a PAC beta-binomial condition (sum_{i<l} C(N_s, i) eps^i (1-eps)^(N_s-i) <= beta_s, giving P(V_c < 0) >= 1-eps_s with confidence 1-beta_s) — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15136v1)
- SafeFQL results: zero violations with higher reward than all baselines on boat navigation; on Safety-Gymnasium velocity tasks (DSRL datasets) the "lowest costs" and "highest reward among frameworks with comparable costs" (figure only); 2.5x faster inference than diffusion baselines. The calibrated delta* equals 0 on most tasks and is non-zero only on Hopper and Walker2D, so in most tasks calibration certified rather than changed the policy — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15136v1)
- **Zhou, Zhang, Luo, "Computationally and Sample Efficient Safe RL Using Adaptive Conformal Prediction" (arXiv:2503.17678, 2025)**: ACP-quantified discrete-time CBF constraint (rank ceil((K+1)(1-alpha))) filters an optimistic MPPI explorer. They report no collisions over 10 learning runs x 50 episodes for GP, RFF and QFF surrogates, whereas QFF-CBF without ACP was not safe; rewards appear in figures only, with no matched-safety return comparison — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.17678)
- **Adaptive Shielding under hidden-parameter shifts (arXiv:2506.11033, 2025)**: an ACP-bounded (2% failure probability) shield over a function-encoder dynamics model, plus safety-regularized training. Claims "consistently lower cost rates while maintaining competitive returns" on Safe-Gym, including out-of-distribution dynamics, while zero-violation baselines (CPO, Saute) "sacrifice return". Numbers are in figures only — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11033)
- **HACO (arXiv preprint, 11 Sept 2025; read via an aggregator page, so secondary)**: offline RL for Medicaid outreach (2.77M decisions, 168,126 patients). A conformal threshold (tau of about 0.038 at alpha = 0.10; risk-model AUC of about 0.81) masks unsafe actions before preference learning; evaluated only by fitted Q evaluation, with no return-vs-violation comparison to an uncalibrated threshold — [Source](https://exa.ai/library/publication/ggh32n6cd59)
- **PlanCP (Sun et al., NeurIPS 2023)**: adds a CP-inspired quantile term to Diffuser training and calibrates at test time (alpha = 0.1, 20% calibration split). Maze2D average 125.3 vs. 119.5 for Diffuser (U-Maze 116.4 ± 3.2 vs. 113.9 ± 3.1; Medium 128.5 vs. 121.5; Large 130.9 vs. 123.0); D4RL locomotion average 68.55 vs. 67.58, mostly within reported standard deviations. The authors call the performance gain "a by-product ... not necessarily guaranteed"; no safety constraint is involved — [Source](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/fe318a2b6c699808019a456b706cd845-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- **Conformal off-policy evaluation intervals (not yet used for optimization)**: PACOPP (Wan, Li, Wu, arXiv:2507.16236, 2025) gives PAC intervals for contextual-bandit OPE. Weighted-CP COPP (Taufiq et al. 2022) "generally failed to achieve the nominal coverage" because its density model was misspecified, while the rejection-sampling methods (COPP-RS, PACOPP) "attained the desired coverage in most cases" (nominal 80%) and PACOPP at delta = 0.01 was "not overly conservative" — [Source](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2507.16236). "Conformal Prediction Beyond the Horizon" (arXiv:2510.26026, 2025) builds return intervals for infinite-horizon on- and off-policy evaluation with distributional RL and weighted subsampling, with only asymptotic coverage bounds — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.26026)
- **CVaR/percentile RL without distribution-free calibration**: CVaR-CPO constrains the CVaR of cumulative cost using "quantile-based estimation" — [Source](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10444044); Chow et al. give Lagrangian policy-gradient/actor-critic methods for chance- and CVaR-constrained MDPs (JMLR 2018) — [Source](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume18/15-636/15-636.pdf). "Conformal Prediction Intervals for MDP Trajectories" notes that distributional-RL quantiles "only provide approximate quantiles" and so "cannot provide finite-sample guarantees on the CVaR", proposing CP calibration of those quantiles as future work — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.04860)

### Inferences
- The CCPO recipe (choose the least conservative tightening whose PAC lower bound just meets 1-alpha) is a direct template for using BCA as a *constraint calibrator* rather than a gate. The "positive" effect comes from removing unnecessary tightening, not from beating an unconstrained policy.
- In the offline-RL papers found (SafeFQL, HACO, PlanCP), the return gains come from the policy class or training loss; the conformal step mainly certifies, or barely moves the threshold (delta* = 0 on most SafeFQL tasks). No paper isolates "calibrated threshold vs. fixed threshold at equal violation" in offline RL.
- Weighted-CP OPE can under-cover when weights or densities are estimated (COPP vs. PACOPP). This is directly relevant to WBCP's importance weights.

### Gaps
- I found no RL paper that optimizes a CVaR or chance objective whose quantile is calibrated by conformal or Wilks bounds *and* reports return at matched violation against a fixed-margin baseline.
- The CCPO journal venue and its objective-cost numbers were not verified; Adaptive Shielding's venue and numeric return/cost values are figure-only; HACO was read only through an aggregator.
- Not fetched (leads): Taufiq et al. 2022 (COPP), Foffano et al. 2023 (conformal OPE in MDPs) and Zhang et al. 2023 (conformal off-policy prediction), all cited in PACOPP. Policy-optimization uses of these are unknown.

## Q3. Training-conditional (PAC) vs. marginal guarantees: per-deployment failures of marginal CP, performance of PAC/tolerance versions, and calibration sample sizes

### Takeaway
Most control papers use marginal split CP, but several explicitly note that coverage *for a fixed calibration set* is Beta-distributed and switch to PAC versions: KnowNo (N = 400, delta = 0.01), Lin & Bansal (CP is equivalent to robust scenario optimization), SafeFQL, Tabbara, CCPO and Park et al. PAC versions cost only a few extra order statistics at moderate n, but are very conservative at small n or very high confidence. I found no control study that empirically measured the across-calibration-draw distribution of per-deployment violation rates for marginal vs. PAC CP.

### Cited Findings
- For a realized calibration set, split-CP coverage follows Beta(n+1-l, l) with l = floor((n+1)alpha) and mean (n+1-l)/(n+2); the marginal guarantee is "not conditional on any specific calibration set" — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07899v2)
- KnowNo (Ren et al., arXiv:2307.01928, CoRL 2023): "to ensure the desired probability of coverage for each new z_test, one needs a fresh calibration set" under the marginal guarantee. They instead use a dataset-conditional guarantee via Beta^-1(delta), with N = 400 and delta = 0.01, adjusting the calibration level so that 1-epsilon coverage holds with probability 0.99 over calibration sampling. Labeling 400 calibration examples took about 4 hours (multi-step) — [Source](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2307.01928)
- Lin & Bansal (L4DC 2024): prove "split conformal prediction reduces to a robust scenario-optimization problem"; the safe fraction of the set is Beta-distributed; its marginal value (N-k)/(N+1) is CP's usual coverage, and a PAC lower bound follows from the Beta CDF at confidence beta. Example: k = 731 outliers in N = 3,684,118 samples gives at least 99.979% safe with 90% confidence — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.08604); venue confirmed in CPP's reference list — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.07407)
- Luo et al., "Sample-Efficient Safety Assurances using Conformal Prediction" (WAFR 2022; extended arXiv v5): a marginal warning system is epsilon*-safe if epsilon + 1/(1+|A|) <= epsilon*, so it needs more than 1/epsilon* - 1 unsafe examples to be non-trivial; they recommend |A| of about 1.5/epsilon* - 1, i.e., "about 30 unsafe examples" for a 5% level (their sentence says "5% false positive rate", but the formula concerns the FNR/safety target). They contrast Theta(1/epsilon) for conformal with Theta(1/epsilon^2) for their standard PAC-learning analysis — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2109.14082v5)
- Luo et al., PAC variant: with a 99.9%-confidence bound on the (1-epsilon) quantile (Lyft data), "the FPR is very high until epsilon is large, since there are too few samples." Marginal CP on grasping gave FNR 0.05 / FPR 0.11 at epsilon = 0.05 and FNR 0.10 / FPR 0.04 at epsilon = 0.10. They note the marginal guarantee bounds each event, but failures can be correlated, which matters more to a fleet operator than to an individual driver — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2109.14082v5)
- Park, Li, Lee, Bastani, "PAC Confidence Predictions for DNN Classifiers" (arXiv:2011.00716): Clopper-Pearson histogram binning. Safe planning in AI Habitat: use the learned policy only while a PAC-calibrated recoverability predictor allows it, otherwise a backup policy (n = 20,000 calibration rollouts, xi = 0.1, delta = 0.01). The PAC method met the desired safety rate; naive gamma = 0.5 did not; uncalibrated-mean histogram binning "performs similarly" without the guarantee. Success rate was sacrificed because the learned policy was safe in only about 30% of rollouts — [Source](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.00716v3)
- Park, Bastani, Matni, Lee (ICLR 2020): PAC confidence sets for DNNs, including a half-cheetah dynamics model for RL; no control-performance results were verified — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00106)
- CPP: the conditional (PAC) quantile is strictly more restrictive than the marginal one (C_c > C_m), and optimizing with it is "more costly". Its PAC shift reduces to a Chernoff bound for large L; Clopper-Pearson is cited as less conservative than Chernoff — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.07407)
- SafeFQL (N_s i.i.d. states sampled from the certified level set, rolled out under the policy) and Tabbara (N_cert i.i.d. initial states from the deployment distribution) both certify with beta-binomial (PAC) conditions — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15136v1); [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07899v2)
- PACOPP: the PAC version's interval length "grew only moderately" as confidence increased to 99% (nominal coverage 80%) — [Source](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2507.16236)
- Calibration sample sizes used: Lindemann 2,000 (ORCA) and 1,500 (CARLA) trajectories; Strawn 1,000 at delta = 0.01; FORCE-OPT 35,220 at 95%; Tabbara 10,000; Park 2021 20,000 rollouts; KnowNo 400 (PAC); CPP L = 200; Luo about 30 unsafe examples (marginal) — [Lindemann](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.10254v2); [Strawn](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.02551); [FORCE-OPT](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.22389v1); [Tabbara](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07899v2); [Park](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.00716v3); [KnowNo](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2307.01928); [CPP](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.07407); [Luo](https://arxiv.org/html/2109.14082v5)
- Lindemann et al. reduced the union-bound horizon from T = 60 to T = 30 because of limited calibration data, i.e., the time-uniform guarantee is sample-hungry — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.10254v2)

### Inferences
- My calculation from Beta(n+1-l, l), alpha = 0.10: for marginal 90% split CP, the probability that a *given* calibration set yields realized coverage below 90% is 0.435 (n = 29), 0.451 (n = 100) and 0.485 (n = 1,000), approaching 0.5 rather than 0. The probability of realized coverage below 85% is 0.168 (n = 29), 0.055 (n = 100), 0.015 (n = 200) and 0.001 (n = 400). So per-deployment shortfalls are inherent at small n, and "near-coin-flip" small shortfalls persist at any n.
- My calculation of the PAC (90% content, 95% confidence) threshold rank: impossible below n = 29 (at n = 29 it is the maximum, matching Wilks); n = 50 gives the 49th smallest score (marginal: 46th); n = 100 the 96th (91st); n = 200 the 188th (181st); n = 400 the 371st (361st); n = 1,000 the 916th (901st). The PAC premium is large at n < 100 and small (about 1.5 quantile points) by n = 1,000.
- The BCA toy finding (marginal split CP at 32-38% unsafe deviations in its worst world) is far larger than the Beta fluctuation above. It is more consistent with additional effects the literature flags: distribution shift between calibration and deployment (Tabbara; COPP under-coverage), correlated failures (Luo), and possibly selection after an argmax over candidate deviations. The selection effect was not examined in any fetched control paper.

### Gaps
- No fetched control/RL paper reports, across repeated calibration draws, the distribution of realized violation rates for marginal vs. PAC CP.
- Selection-conditional validity (the gate accepting the "best" candidate) was not covered; conformal selection/FDR literature was not fetched.
- KnowNo's CoRL 2023 venue and Park 2021's ICLR 2021 venue are from my knowledge (the arXiv v3 text did not state the venue).

## Q4. Evidence that calibrated uncertainty allows LESS conservative behavior (higher performance) at the same safety level than fixed-margin, worst-case or uncalibrated methods

### Takeaway
Yes, but mostly against *worst-case, Gaussian, bootstrap or prediction-set* baselines and for *decision-level* calibration. Examples: CDT is 29% faster than ACI-set planning while safe; KnowNo needs 10-24% less human help at equal success; Muthali's progress is 0.544 vs. 0.305 for an earlier CP method with no more collisions; Strawn's filter is 18% faster than a Gaussian filter; FORCE-OPT flags 8.6% vs. 43.7% of safe plans compared with worst-case reachability; Lin & Bansal certify a safe volume of 0.81 vs. 0.56. Against over-optimistic *uncalibrated* baselines, calibration buys safety at some performance cost. Most experiments are small and none concern offline-RL return under a matched violation constraint.

### Cited Findings
- **Conformal Decision Theory vs. prediction sets** (Stanford Drone Dataset nexus_4, single start/goal, epsilon = 2 m): conformal controller (eta = 100-1,000) reached the goal in 16.2-17.4 s and stayed safe (min distance 1.12-1.27 m), though at eta = 50 it was unsafe (20.0 s, 0.61 m); ACI prediction-set planning (alpha = 0.01, eta in {0, 0.01, 0.1}) took 24.7-27.2 s and was unsafe in every setting (min distance 0.08-0.80 m); aggressive took 8.57 s but was unsafe (0.16 m); conservative never reached the goal. The authors attribute this to the controller's "ability to ignore irrelevant uncertainty ... much less conservative than the prediction-set strategy" — [Source](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.05921)
- **CDT manufacturing** (1,000 runs, T = 2,000, epsilon = 0.05): the conformal controller "performs as well as the Oracle-Loss" (risk never above 5%, high throughput), while UCB and LCB bandits violated the risk threshold — [Source](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.05921)
- **KnowNo**: statistically guaranteed task success "while reducing the amount of help required by 10-24%" vs. baselines. On hardware, at matched plan success it cut human help by 14% step-wise and 8% trial-wise (50 trials); the uncalibrated Simple Set needed epsilon = 0.42 to match KnowNo's error at epsilon = 0.25 — [Source](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2307.01928)
- **Muthali et al. vs. Luo et al. CP and confidence-based (uncalibrated) sets**, Waymo/Nocturne, 200 scenes: progress to goal 0.544 vs. 0.305 vs. 0.494; collision rate 0.0 vs. 0.005 vs. 0.0; conservatism 1.507 vs. 1.626 vs. 1.504; first-step set size 61 vs. 448 vs. 64 m^2. Both CP methods met 95% coverage, while the confidence-based method under-covered on nuScenes (0.816-0.926) — [Source](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.00432)
- **Strawn et al.**: CP filter time-to-goal 8.278 s vs. 10.05 s for a Gaussian filter (-18%), with 99% vs. 100% of positions inside intervals; relative to raw GA3C, collisions fell 49 to 10 but time rose 7.503 to 8.278 s — [Source](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.02551)
- **FORCE-OPT vs. worst-case and uncalibrated sets** (nuScenes Singapore, in-distribution; CP at 95% with 35,220 calibration samples): false-positive rate (unnecessary flags) / false-negative rate: FORCE-OPT 8.62% / 3.03%; worst-case reachable set 43.73% / 0%; single-mode CP (Lindemann-style) 31.12% / 0%; uncalibrated 99% GMM sets 1.19% / 33.33%. Out-of-distribution (Boston): uncalibrated FNR rises to 51.85%, FORCE-OPT 12.96% (FPR 5.22%), and FORCE-OPT with a worst-case fallback 0% FNR at 21.15% FPR vs. 45.42% for worst-case alone. More GMM modes required smaller CP inflation and lowered FPR — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.22389v1)
- **Lin & Bansal**: allowing calibrated non-zero outliers (robust scenario / CP) instead of the zero-outlier iterative scenario method raised the certified safe volume from 0.56 to 0.81 for a safety-level change from 99.999% to 99.974% (beta = 1e-16); the iterative method "can result in very conservative safe sets" — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.08604)
- **Dixit et al. ACP vs. bootstrap/EKF**: mean minimum distance 2.74-2.91 vs. 4.25-14.2 at 99.2-100% success, but 3.6-16.7 points lower MPC feasibility (80.9-83.8% vs. 87.4-97.6%) — [Source](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v211/dixit23a/dixit23a.pdf)
- **Tabbara et al.**: replacing nominal-only control with CP-calibrated switching raised success from 54% to 92-98% and cut violations from 46% to 2-8% (best models, alpha = 0.06, 50 trials). This is a safer-and-better case because success requires no violation, not a matched-safety comparison — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07899v2)
- **Counter-evidence: calibration costing performance or adding conservatism**
  - Muthali's calibrated sets were 1.5-1.7x larger than uncalibrated ones — [Source](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.00432)
  - Park 2021's PAC shield sacrificed success and did not beat uncalibrated histogram binning, only adding the guarantee — [Source](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.00716v3)
  - Conformal shielding at 99-99.5% coverage got stuck and fell below the baseline's success — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.17275v2)
  - Luo's 99.9%-confidence PAC alarms had very high FPR at small n — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2109.14082v5)
  - Under sub-Gaussian noise assumptions, a moment-generating-function bound is claimed to be sharper than CP-style worst-case bounds — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09334)

### Inferences
- **Mechanisms with evidence for positive (less conservative) effects:**
  1. Replace worst-case or hand-tuned margins by the smallest margin that meets the target: CCPO backoffs; Tabbara's switching threshold; Lin & Bansal level selection; FORCE-OPT vs. worst-case reachability.
  2. Calibrate the decision or its risk directly rather than covering every outcome: CDT vs. ACI sets; KnowNo vs. cumulative-threshold sets.
  3. Admit a calibrated number of empirical violations instead of requiring zero (Lin & Bansal).
  4. Improve the score function, since CP only rescales it: multimodal GMM scores (FORCE-OPT), informative uncertainty features (Muthali), heteroskedastic scores (cited by Scarbro). For BCA, a better scale network sigma(x) reduces the width that calibration must add.
  5. Optimize against the empirical quantile, then certify on independent data (CPP's K/L split; SafeFQL; Tabbara Phase 2).
- **Negative mechanisms to watch:** union bounds over time or agents; high confidence at small n; empty-shield "stuck" states; and calibration-induced distribution shift, where a more conservative gate puts the fallback or learned policy off-distribution.
- **Strength of evidence:** almost no comparison is at exactly matched violation. The closest matched cases are KnowNo (matched success), CDT (dominates on both axes, but a single scenario), Muthali (both CP methods meet 95% coverage) and FORCE-OPT vs. worst-case (FNR 3.0% vs. 0%). Sample sizes are often small: Tabbara's 50 trials make 2/50 compatible with violation rates above 10%.
- For BCA's gate, the literature suggests return can be recovered without losing the PAC guarantee by (i) a decision-level loss (the gate already does this), (ii) more calibration data, since the PAC premium shrinks quickly beyond about 100-200 points, (iii) a better score/scale model, and (iv) choosing the least conservative gate parameter subject to the PAC bound (CCPO/CDT-batch style) rather than a fixed 90%/95% setting.

### Gaps
- I found no offline-RL study showing higher return at matched violation from conformal/PAC calibration vs. a fixed-margin or uncalibrated rule; the closest RL items (SafeFQL, Adaptive Shielding, Zhou et al.) report figure-level trade-offs and do not isolate the calibration step.
- CDT's navigation result is a single scenario; Tabbara's uses 50 trials; Strawn's uses one simulator; uncertainty intervals for these comparisons are not reported.
- Not verified: Huang et al. "interaction-aware CP for crowd navigation" (2025), Hashemi et al. CP reachability, and Lindemann's 2024 tutorial (possible additional matched-safety numbers).

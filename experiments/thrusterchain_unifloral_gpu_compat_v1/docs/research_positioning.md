# What this benchmark contributes

ThrusterChain is a small deterministic control task with an inspectable model, an actuator stall region, and controlled shifts. Its proposed contribution is a reproducible diagnostic setting in which state support, action support, and evaluation shifts can be varied explicitly, with frozen-policy returns and narrowly scoped calibration checks reported separately. This is a claim about experimental controllability and auditability. It does not establish a new offline RL algorithm, a new uncertainty guarantee, or broad robotics performance.

The comparisons below are our interpretation of the benchmark's scope relative to primary sources, checked on 2026-10-22.

**OPCC.** Anurag Koul, Mariano Phielipp, and Alan Fern, *Offline Policy Comparison with Confidence: Benchmarks and Baselines* (2022, arXiv:2205.10739), already evaluate confidence in offline policy comparisons. Queries specify two policies, their starting states, and a horizon; predictions concern their relative expected discounted returns. Its risk-coverage evaluation uses coverage to mean the fraction of queries retained at a confidence threshold, which is distinct from prediction-interval coverage. ThrusterChain's controlled support and shift diagnostics complement that question, but its present interval diagnostics are not OPCC scores or evidence of improved policy-comparison confidence. [Primary paper, Sections 3.1-3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.10739v1).

**Clean Slate and Unifloral.** Matthew T. Jackson, Uljad Berdica, Jarek Liesen, Shimon Whiteson, and Jakob Foerster, *A Clean Slate for Offline Reinforcement Learning* (NeurIPS 2025), formalize offline RL settings, expose online tuning budgets, and provide streamlined implementations. The paper also explicitly introduces Unifloral as a unified algorithm with a common configurable hyperparameter space, and develops TD3-AWR and MoBRAC. Its methodological relevance here is to declare the data, model access, policy-selection rule, and simulator-tuning budget for each comparison. ThrusterChain's frozen offline selection protocol does not reproduce the paper's budgeted tuning experiments. [Primary proceedings paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/ebd3fdd63c57df59a8ba523655e6c86f-Abstract-Conference.html).

Unifloral also serves as an implementation framework: its repository provides standalone TD3+BC, IQL, CQL, and ReBRAC hosts and a unified implementation for a subset of methods. In the associated Unifloral-host study, that is its practical role; each host algorithm and intervention must still be named separately. This study-specific description does not erase the paper's unified-algorithm contribution. Nor does it establish Unifloral provenance for every later experiment: the separately audited native 45-run study and the unavailable older four-host exporter evidence have distinct records. [Authors' implementation repository](https://github.com/EmptyJackson/unifloral), [local historical actor audit](historical_actor_audit.md).

**OGBench.** Seohong Park, Kevin Frans, Benjamin Eysenbach, and Sergey Levine, *OGBench: Benchmarking Offline Goal-Conditioned RL* (ICLR 2025; initial preprint 2024), provide a broader capability benchmark. The paper reports eight environment types, 85 datasets, and six reference methods, probing stitching, long-horizon reasoning, high-dimensional observations, and stochasticity. Its published evaluation emphasizes goal-reaching success. ThrusterChain's fixed-objective deterministic task and support diagnostics do not establish those capabilities or reproduce that evaluation. [Primary paper, Sections 5-8](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.20092v2). OGBench also supports standard single-task offline RL; it should not be described as exclusively goal-conditioned. [Authors' repository](https://github.com/seohongpark/ogbench#usage-for-standard-non-goal-conditioned-offline-rl).

These comparisons motivate future experiments; this release claims no tested OPCC or OGBench integration, no reproduction of their results, and no performance advantage over their baselines.

Regular mode follows the offline-data/live-evaluation workflow: freeze an episode dataset, train and select using declared offline splits, freeze parameters and preprocessing, then evaluate in the simulator. Dataset creation is a separate budget. The three shifted start families are held out of dataset generation and offline selection. Simulator-assisted hyperparameter tuning must be labeled separately, use development seeds and families, and receive a new frozen receipt before final evaluation.

Modern interoperability should retain episode boundaries and final observations. Minari describes episodes with L+1 observations for L actions and rewards; our native arrays use that layout. This version supplies a documented flat transition export, but does not claim a tested Minari package integration or a published D4RL dataset. [Minari EpisodeData specification](https://minari.farama.org/api/minari_dataset/episode_data/).

Finite horizon and time-limit truncation answer different questions. The finite task exposes remaining time and terminates at its objective horizon. The legacy replay mode preserves original truncation semantics. [Gymnasium handling time limits](https://gymnasium.farama.org/tutorials/gymnasium_basics/handling_time_limits/).

The controlled query diagnostic uses explicit sampling laws. Weighted conformal claims require a common conditional response law, support inclusion, fresh independent calibration groups, and the specified density ratio including test-point mass at infinity. Finite ESS is a precision diagnostic; it does not repair zero support or establish a general value-function guarantee. [Tibshirani et al., Conformal Prediction Under Covariate Shift](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06019).

The immediate evidence is a reproducible integration profile and independent source audits. Research-scale seeds, predeclared algorithm budgets, held-out selection rules, uncertainty across independent replicates, contemporary offline baselines, Minari packaging, installation on a fresh host, and external review remain the next publication gates. Historical 45-run results remain development evidence for this redesign. Institutional documentation, backup, licensing review, and credential-invalidation gaps are not closed by a traceability manifest.

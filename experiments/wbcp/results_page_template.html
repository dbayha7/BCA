What the semi-synthetic tests show

TD3+BC with BCA's learned scale is trained on half of a D4RL dataset, frozen, and used to score every transition in the other half. That pool is resampled under shifts we impose, so the true risk of any threshold is known exactly. A calibration bank "fails" when the threshold it certifies misses more than 10% of test rows; a valid rule fails at most 5% of the time. Every experiment after the first had its expectations written down before it ran.

Setup

Target miscoverage α = 0.1 at credibility β = 0.95. Each failure rate is the share of 2,000-4,000 simulated banks whose certified threshold misses more than 10% of test rows, shown with its exact 95% interval.

Shifts reweight the pool by exp(γ z), z a standardized feature of (s, a): closeness of the logged action to the policy's (policy), sparseness of the region (density) or the size of the state (state). Resampling never changes a row's score, so the shift is purely covariate and the true threshold λ* is exact.

Terms

Bank
The calibration set a threshold is computed from. BCA's hopper bank is about 1,100 rows.
Uniform BCA
WBCP with equal weights (BQ-CP), today's BCA calibration.
WBCP
Weighted Bayesian conformal prediction (Lou and Luo), with weights from a classifier or, as a control, the exact ones.
RCPS
A conservative frequentist baseline.
K
Rows taken from each withheld episode. Random puts them anywhere; spaced puts one in each K-th of the episode; BCA sampler is BCA's own reservation.
ρ, design effect
ρ is the correlation between misses in the same episode. The design effect 1 + (K − 1) ρ is how much that inflates a bank's variance.
Normalized / raw
The score with and without BCA's learned scale σ(s, a). The toggle in the header switches every chart that has both.
Question
Does BCA's original bank, whole held-out episodes, give the guarantee WBCP promises?
Answer
No. About 28% of banks fail with no shift at all, because rows in one episode are correlated and the posterior treats them as independent. A bigger bank of whole episodes only makes the threshold tighter.

Question
Does taking only K rows from each of many episodes restore validity, and does spacing them apart help?
Answer
Yes for small K. K ≤ 10 stays near 5% without shift, the threshold does not widen, and spacing helps at larger K. The cost is training data: every contributing episode leaves training.

Question
Does the WBCP-versus-uniform picture survive thinned banks, and which design holds under shift?
Answer
Uniform BCA fails 25-98% under density and state shift while WBCP stays at 4-9%. K = 5 holds; K = 10 fails under the density tilt, spaced or not, because it draws from too few episodes.

Does BCA's own implementation of the thinned bank behave like the validated design, and what K does each config get?
Answer
Yes on hopper: 5.0% without shift and every shift range met. Under the rule "K = 5, raised until it fits the withholding cap", most TD3+BC / ReBRAC / CQL banks stay validated and most IQL banks do not.

Question
Does hopper's answer (K = 5) carry over to walker2d-medium-replay and pen-cloned?
Answer
No. ρ is about seven times hopper's, K = 5 fails 6-8%, and the configured walker2d banks fail 18-30%. Estimated weights also hurt WBCP there even without shift, unless the weight model gets far more data.

Question
Why does WBCP fail 7-9% under strong shift on hopper, even with independent rows and exact weights?
Answer
It is finite-sample behaviour the paper allows and also reports. It is driven by heavy weights that sit on the misses, it shrinks as banks grow, and failing banks exceed α by under a point.

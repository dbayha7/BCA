ThrusterChain

Purpose

Test, against exact answers, whether BCA does what it claims. BCA (Bayesian Conformal Aggregation, explained in Methods) is an add-on to TD3+BC (twin delayed deep deterministic policy gradient plus behaviour cloning), a standard method that learns a controller only from recorded data. The learned controller is called the actor. It relies on a critic: a learned estimate of how good each command is. Where BCA judges that the critic may be wrong, it adds caution: a harder pull toward the recorded command. The test asks three things. Does BCA find where the critic's error costs return (the total reward over an episode, which is one run of 100 steps from one starting state)? Does its caution then help in situations beyond the recorded data? And does the actor still do better than simply copying the recorded commands?

Task

Hold five floats at rest in a channel against a current and waves, with four thrusters, for 100 steps. Each step: read 13 numbers, set 4 throttles between −1 and 1, and pay the floats' squared distance from rest plus 0.3 times the squared throttles. Past 80% throttle a propeller stalls: it pushes less but still costs more. The whole task is explained in The task, just below.

The shift

A distribution shift is a difference between the training data and the situations met at test time. Here it is built on purpose and controlled. The data come from a fixed recording controller, whose most common single command puts thruster 1 at full throttle ({{dataset/full_throttle_share_train#p2}} of rows, where a row is one recorded step; a bad command, because the propeller stalls). The densest-15% removal, the rule this project used on D4RL (Datasets for Deep Data-Driven Reinforcement Learning, the standard benchmark of recorded datasets), deletes the 15% of recorded commands that sit closest together, and with them almost every full-throttle command. The learned actor is then tested in the recorded conditions and in three shifted situations whose current, waves or starting pushes go beyond anything recorded. Out of distribution, on this page, means outside the recorded conditions.

Measured

Exactly, state by state: the physics has no randomness, so every true value is one simulation away. A true value is the exact discounted sum of the rewards that follow a command in a state while a given controller drives afterwards; discounted means that later rewards count less: a reward \(t\) steps ahead counts \(0.98^t\) as much. Scores are points: 0 is the recording controller, 100 the stall-aware controller, which never pushes past 80%.

stopped before BCA The gate has run on this environment: four harm checks that must pass before BCA is run at all. They were pre-registered: every rule and pass mark was written down and hashed before any result existed. A hash is a short fingerprint computed from a file; changing any character of the file changes it, so publishing the hash proves the file was not edited later. The shift causes real, large harm: the check that the critic is wrong where data are missing and the check that acting on that error costs return both pass. But caution of the kind BCA applies cannot reach that harm: the check that caution placed at the harm could help (the room check) found no shifted situation where caution placed at the harm beats even caution of the same strength by the required margin. So the gate stopped before any BCA run, and the power check, which sets how many seeds a fair test of BCA needs, did not run. See Results; the checks are described in The harm checks. What has happened since, with no BCA run, is in After the stop.

next The stop was not accepted. The next experiment on this environment is The weighting test: it checks that BCA's mechanics work on data where every value is known, and whether importance weighting toward a known test distribution improves on unweighted BCA.

The task

A controller must keep five floats at rest in a channel of moving water for 100 steps, using four thrusters, while spending as little throttle as it can. At every step it reads 13 numbers (where each float is, how strong the current is, and where the wave is in its cycle) and sets four throttles. Nothing about the state is hidden, and once the start is drawn nothing is random: the next state follows exactly from this state and the throttles. What makes the task hard is the water. It never stops pushing, its push keeps rising and falling, it keeps the floats swinging, and where the current is strong, or the wave adds its hardest push to the current, holding the floats needs more push than a thruster can give without stalling.

A controller is tested in four situations, which differ only in how a start is drawn: the recorded conditions of the training data (current {{environment/start_laws/recorded/current/0#f1}} to {{environment/start_laws/recorded/current/1#f1}}, wave amplitude {{environment/start_laws/recorded/wave_amplitude/0#f1}} to {{environment/start_laws/recorded/wave_amplitude/1#f1}}), and three shifted ones beyond them: strong current (current {{environment/start_laws/strong_current/current/0#f1}} to {{environment/start_laws/strong_current/current/1#f1}}), big waves (amplitude {{environment/start_laws/big_waves/wave_amplitude/0#f1}} to {{environment/start_laws/big_waves/wave_amplitude/1#f1}}) and big starting pushes (the floats start three times as far from rest). The wave's amplitude is the largest push it adds or takes away.

In this section a share of states counts every state met in the 100 steps from each of a situation's 1,000 fixed evaluation starts (its evaluation bank, Starting State). A float's distance from rest is the length of its pair of numbers, \(\sqrt{p^2 + v^2}\); the float cost adds up the squared distances of the five floats.

What the water does to the floats

  • The current pushes every float downstream, all episode. Its strength \(c\) is drawn at the start and then never changes (from {{environment/start_laws/recorded/current/0#f1}} to {{environment/start_laws/recorded/current/1#f1}} in the recorded conditions), and it pushes all five floats equally. With no throttle, a steady current of strength 1 would leave float 1, once settled (its two numbers no longer changing), {{overview/water_alone/steady_offset_per_unit_current/0/distance#f2}} from rest but float 5 only {{overview/water_alone/steady_offset_per_unit_current/4/distance#f2}} (the distances grow in proportion to the current). Float 1's own swing turns and fades the slowest, so a steady push builds up furthest there.
  • The wave makes the push rise and fall. The wave adds \(w_1\) to the current's push, and \(w_1\) runs from plus the amplitude down to minus the amplitude and back, once every {{environment/dynamics/wave_period_steps#f2}} steps, about {{overview/waves/cycles_per_episode#f1}} times per episode. So the water's push on every float, \(c + w_1\), ranges between \(c - \text{amplitude}\) and \(c + \text{amplitude}\): from {{overview/waves/situations/recorded/push_lowest_possible#f1}} to {{overview/waves/situations/recorded/push_highest_possible#f1}} in the recorded conditions. Where the wave is bigger than the current, the push turns upstream for part of each cycle; that happens on {{overview/waves/situations/recorded/negative_push_share_of_states#p1}} of states in the recorded conditions. No fixed counter-push can cancel a push that keeps changing: a counter-push that holds the floats while the wave pushes hardest is too much when the wave eases off.
  • The wave's crests demand the most push. The page calls the states where the wave adds at least {{overview/controllers/crest_share_of_amplitude#p0}} of its amplitude to the current's push its crests, and the states where it takes at least {{overview/controllers/crest_share_of_amplitude#p0}} away its troughs. Holding the floats needs the most push near a crest and the least near a trough. Near the crests the stall-aware controller (below) has thruster 1 at its 80% limit on {{overview/controllers/stall_aware/recorded/crest/at_cap_thruster1#p0}} of states in the recorded conditions, {{overview/controllers/stall_aware/big_waves/crest/at_cap_thruster1#p0}} in big waves and {{overview/controllers/stall_aware/strong_current/crest/at_cap_thruster1#p0}} in strong current; near the troughs on {{overview/controllers/stall_aware/recorded/trough/at_cap_thruster1#p0}}, {{overview/controllers/stall_aware/big_waves/trough/at_cap_thruster1#p0}} and {{overview/controllers/stall_aware/strong_current/trough/at_cap_thruster1#p0}}. The crests are also where the recorded data go wrong. In {{overview/waves/situations/recorded/starts_whose_crest_passes_the_mark#p1}} of recorded starts the crests lift the push above 0.565, into the full-throttle region, where the recording controller flips a coin in each state and on half of them puts thruster 1 at full throttle (the mark 0.565 was chosen so that about {{overview/waves/trigger/share_target#p0}} of recorded commands are full-throttle commands). That region holds {{reference_returns/returns/stall/recorded/stall_aware/full_throttle_region_steps#p1}} of recorded states and {{reference_returns/returns/stall/big_waves/stall_aware/full_throttle_region_steps#p1}} in big waves.
  • The floats swing, and the wave keeps them swinging. Each float swings back and forth around rest, a little less each time, with its own period, the number of steps one swing takes: from {{overview/swings/list/4/period_steps#f1}} steps for float 5 to {{overview/swings/list/0/period_steps#f1}} for float 1. Left alone, a swing fades to half its size in {{overview/swings/list/4/half_life_steps#f0}} to {{overview/swings/list/0/half_life_steps#f0}} steps, so every change in the push starts a swing that carries on after the push has changed again. The wave's cycle of {{environment/dynamics/wave_period_steps#f2}} steps lies between the periods of floats 2 and 3 ({{overview/swings/list/1/period_steps#f1}} and {{overview/swings/list/2/period_steps#f1}} steps), the two closest to it. So the wave pushes those floats nearly in step with their own swing and adds to it cycle after cycle. With no throttle, the largest recorded wave (amplitude {{environment/start_laws/recorded/wave_amplitude/1#f1}}) keeps float 2 swinging out to {{overview/water_alone/wave_swing_float2_at_amplitude/0.4#f2}} from rest. The swings grow in proportion to the amplitude: per unit of amplitude, float 2 reaches {{overview/water_alone/wave_swing_per_unit_amplitude/1/peak_distance#f2}}, float 3 {{overview/water_alone/wave_swing_per_unit_amplitude/2/peak_distance#f2}} and float 5 only {{overview/water_alone/wave_swing_per_unit_amplitude/4/peak_distance#f2}}.
  • The floats barely affect each other. A weak, fixed coupling links them (Transition Dynamics), so each float answers almost only to the water and to the thrusters that reach it.
  • Each episode starts with the floats already out of place. Each of the ten float numbers starts with a spread (standard deviation) of {{environment/start_laws/recorded/float_start_sd#f1}} around rest, so a starting state's float cost averages {{overview/starts/situations/recorded/mean_start_float_cost#f1}} in the recorded conditions. Left alone, float 1 still keeps {{overview/swings/list/0/left_after_100_steps#p0}} of its starting swing after 100 steps.

None of this can surprise a controller. No thruster changes the water, the current never changes, and the wave moves on by the same share of its cycle every step (one cycle takes {{environment/dynamics/wave_period_steps#f2}} steps), so the current and the two wave numbers fix the water's push at every later step; \(w_2\) even tells which way the push is heading (Observation Space). The difficulty is not predicting the water but answering it with four thrusters that stall past 80%. To see the water act alone, pick No throttle in the episode panel at the top of the page. With no throttle the average return in the recorded conditions is {{reference_returns/returns/stall/recorded/no_throttle/J#f1}}.

What a controller does to counteract

The stall-aware controller, one of the fixed reference controllers listed under Reference controllers, shows what good control does. It applies the textbook rule \(K\): each of the four throttles is a fixed weighted sum of the 13 observed numbers (a linear rule), with the weights that give the lowest cost over the long run if push always equalled throttle. Then it caps every throttle at 80%. In the recorded conditions it brings the floats' cost from {{overview/water_alone/cost_split/recorded/no_throttle/float_cost_per_state#f2}} down to {{overview/water_alone/cost_split/recorded/stall_aware/float_cost_per_state#f3}} per state, removing {{overview/water_alone/cost_split/recorded/stall_aware_float_cost_removed_share#p1}} of it, and its average return is {{reference_returns/returns/stall/recorded/stall_aware/J#f1}}. It does six things at once.

  • It pushes back upstream, hardest where the water carries the floats furthest. A negative throttle pushes upstream. Against a current \(c\) alone, with the floats at rest, \(K\) sets thruster 1 to \(-{{recording_controller/K_current_column/0#t2}}\,c\), thruster 2 to \(-{{recording_controller/K_current_column/1#t2}}\,c\), thruster 3 to \(-{{recording_controller/K_current_column/2#t2}}\,c\) and thruster 4 to \(-{{recording_controller/K_current_column/3#t2}}\,c\). Thruster 1 does the most because float 1 is the float a steady push carries furthest, and only thruster 1 reaches it. Once the floats have settled, \(K\) holds thruster 1 at \({{overview/who_pushes/textbook_steady/throttles_per_unit_current/0#t2}}\,c\).
  • It chooses where the push it cannot cancel lands. Each thruster gives the same push to both of its floats, and that makes it impossible to cancel a push that is equal on all five floats. Take the push on floats 1, 3 and 5 minus the push on floats 2 and 4. Each thruster pushes one float of each group, so its push drops out of that difference. The water pushes three floats in the first group and two in the second, so the difference always equals the water's push, whatever the throttles. Some push is always left over, and a controller can only choose which floats keep it, trading their distance from rest against the throttle it spends. Once the floats have settled in a steady current, \(K\) leaves {{overview/who_pushes/textbook_steady/leftover_push_per_float/0#p0}} of the water's push on float 1, which a steady push carries furthest, and {{overview/who_pushes/textbook_steady/leftover_push_per_float/4#p0}} on float 5, which it barely moves. The floats then cost \({{overview/who_pushes/textbook_steady/float_cost_per_unit_current_squared#t3}}\,c^2\) in every settled state, against \({{overview/water_alone/steady_float_cost_per_unit_current_squared#t2}}\,c^2\) with no throttle.
  • It raises and lowers its push with the wave, in step with it. \(K\) reads both wave numbers, so in every state it knows how hard the wave pushes and whether that push is rising or falling. The part of \(K\) that reads the wave asks thruster 1 for {{overview/controllers/hold_terms/0/wave_hold_size#f2}} per unit of amplitude, timed {{overview/controllers/hold_terms/0/wave_hold_lead_steps#f1}} steps ahead of the wave's push; the part that pushes against the floats' own movement brings the throttle back into step. So against a wave alone, once the floats have settled, thruster 1's throttle rises and falls by {{overview/controllers/wave_response/textbook_rule/throttle_swing_per_unit_amplitude/0#f2}} for each unit of amplitude, in step with the water's push (only {{derived/thr1_wave_lag_steps#f2}} steps behind it). State by state, the stall-aware controller's thruster 1 follows the water's push: the harder the water pushes downstream, the harder thruster 1 pushes upstream. Over all states of the recorded conditions their correlation is {{overview/controllers/stall_aware/recorded/thruster1_vs_push_correlation#f2}} (a correlation runs from −1 to 1, and −1 means one number moves in exact proportion to the other, in the opposite direction). Near the wave's crests, where the push averages {{overview/controllers/stall_aware/recorded/crest/push#f2}}, thruster 1 averages {{overview/controllers/stall_aware/recorded/crest/thruster1#f2}}; near its troughs, where the push averages {{overview/controllers/stall_aware/recorded/trough/push#f2}}, thruster 1 eases to {{overview/controllers/stall_aware/recorded/trough/thruster1#f2}}. In big waves, where the push near the troughs averages {{overview/controllers/stall_aware/big_waves/trough/push#f2}} (upstream), thruster 1 there on average even pushes slightly downstream (+{{overview/controllers/stall_aware/big_waves/trough/thruster1#f2}}).
  • It damps the swinging. Besides holding against the water, \(K\) pushes against each float's own swing, so a swing dies out far sooner than by fading alone. Under \(K\), what is left of a start's swinging fades to half within {{overview/starts/half_life/textbook_feedback_slowest#f1}} steps on every float; with no throttle, float 1's takes {{overview/starts/half_life/no_throttle_slowest#f0}}. Against a wave, once settled, \(K\) cuts float 2's swing from {{overview/water_alone/wave_swing_per_unit_amplitude/1/peak_distance#f2}} to {{overview/controllers/wave_response/textbook_rule/float_swing_per_unit_amplitude/1#f2}} per unit of amplitude, and the floats' cost per state from {{overview/water_alone/wave_float_cost_per_unit_amplitude_squared#f2}} to {{overview/controllers/wave_response/textbook_rule/float_cost_per_unit_amplitude_squared#f2}} times the amplitude squared.
  • It weighs throttle against distance. Throttle costs too: 0.3 times the sum of the squared throttles, 0.192 for one thruster at 80% and 0.3 at full throttle. So \(K\) stops short of cancelling every push. In the recorded conditions the stall-aware controller spends {{overview/water_alone/cost_split/recorded/stall_aware/throttle_cost_per_state#f3}} per state on throttle, {{derived/sa_recorded_throttle_share#p0}} of its total cost, and that total is {{overview/water_alone/cost_split/recorded/no_throttle_over_stall_aware#f0}} times smaller than with no throttle.
  • It never goes past 80%. Up to 80%, a thruster pushes exactly as hard as its throttle. Past 80% its propeller stalls: the push falls while the throttle cost keeps rising (Action Space), so the hardest push a thruster can give is 0.8, at 80%. In any state, thruster 1 at full throttle adds only {{overview/signs/thruster1_one_step_from_rest/stall_full/speed_change_float1#f2}} to the speed numbers of floats 1 and 2, against {{overview/signs/thruster1_one_step_from_rest/at_80/speed_change_float1#f2}} at 80%.

Why strong current is the hard case

In strong current the current alone, from {{environment/start_laws/strong_current/current/0#f1}} to {{environment/start_laws/strong_current/current/1#f1}}, asks for most of what thruster 1 can give. With the floats at rest, \(K\) asks thruster 1 for 80% against a current of {{recording_controller/current_where_textbook_hold_reaches_80pct_thruster1#f3}} and for full throttle against {{recording_controller/current_where_textbook_hold_reaches_full_throttle_thruster1#f3}}. Once the floats have settled under \(K\), it asks for these at currents of only {{overview/who_pushes/textbook_steady/current_where_thruster1_reaches_80pct#f3}} and {{overview/who_pushes/textbook_steady/current_where_thruster1_reaches_full_throttle#f3}}. On top of that the wave moves thruster 1's throttle up and down by about {{overview/controllers/wave_response/textbook_rule/throttle_swing_per_unit_amplitude/0#f2}} × its amplitude, and the water's push runs from {{overview/waves/situations/strong_current/push_lowest_possible#f1}} to {{overview/waves/situations/strong_current/push_highest_possible#f1}}.

So in most states of strong current the stall-aware controller is at its 80% limit: thruster 1 on {{overview/controllers/stall_aware/strong_current/at_cap_thruster1#p1}} of states (some thruster on {{overview/controllers/stall_aware/strong_current/at_cap_any_thruster#p1}}), and it accepts that float 1 drifts a little downstream. The rising-thrust controller gives the same textbook command but allows up to 100%, as if push kept rising with throttle. In strong current it goes past 80% on {{reference_returns/returns/stall/strong_current/rising_thrust/steps_past_80#p1}} of states (thruster 1 on {{overview/controllers/rising_thrust/strong_current/past_80_thruster1#p1}}, thruster 3 on {{overview/controllers/rising_thrust/strong_current/past_80_by_thruster/2#p1}}). A stalled thruster then pushes on average {{overview/controllers/rising_thrust/strong_current/push_lost_per_stalled_thruster_state#f2}} less than its throttle asks, and a stalled thruster 1 pushes only {{overview/controllers/rising_thrust/strong_current/thruster1_push_when_past#f2}} while costing more than at 80%. In a steady current of {{overview/controllers/steady_current/currents/4#f1}} with no wave, the stall-aware controller settles with thruster 1 at {{overview/controllers/steady_current/table/stall_aware/4/thruster1#f2}} and a float cost of {{overview/controllers/steady_current/table/stall_aware/4/float_cost#f3}} in each state; the rising-thrust controller settles at full throttle, where thruster 1 pushes only {{derived/rt_thruster1_push_size_at_09#f1}}, and leaves a float cost of {{overview/controllers/steady_current/table/rising_thrust/4/float_cost#f2}}, {{derived/steady_ratio_at_09#f0}} times as much. Over 100 steps it costs {{overview/controllers/rising_thrust/strong_current/extra_cost_against_stall_aware#f1}} more than the stall-aware controller, which puts it at {{reference_returns/returns/stall/strong_current/rising_thrust/points#f1}} points, below the recording controller's 0.

How each situation changes the challenge

The four situations share the water's rules and differ only in how a start is drawn (Starting State). Their evaluation banks use the same random draws, so each differs from the recorded conditions only in the setting it changes.

  • Recorded conditions (current {{environment/start_laws/recorded/current/0#f1}} to {{environment/start_laws/recorded/current/1#f1}}, wave amplitude {{environment/start_laws/recorded/wave_amplitude/0#f1}} to {{environment/start_laws/recorded/wave_amplitude/1#f1}}, starting spread {{environment/start_laws/recorded/float_start_sd#f1}}): the conditions of the recorded data. Holding seldom needs 80%: the stall-aware controller is at its 80% limit on {{overview/controllers/stall_aware/recorded/at_cap_any_thruster#p1}} of states, and the rising-thrust controller goes past 80% on {{reference_returns/returns/stall/recorded/rising_thrust/steps_past_80#p1}}.
  • Strong current (current {{environment/start_laws/strong_current/current/0#f1}} to {{environment/start_laws/strong_current/current/1#f1}}): the same waves and starts on top of a stronger current, so the controller must push hard in every state and thruster 1 is at its 80% limit on most states (above). This is the main test.
  • Big waves (wave amplitude {{environment/start_laws/big_waves/wave_amplitude/0#f1}} to {{environment/start_laws/big_waves/wave_amplitude/1#f1}}, every start's amplitude {{overview/starts/bank_relations/big_waves_amplitude_minus_recorded#f1}} larger than in the recorded bank): the current is as recorded, but the push ranges further, from {{overview/waves/situations/big_waves/push_lowest_possible#f1}} to {{overview/waves/situations/big_waves/push_highest_possible#f1}}, and points upstream on {{overview/waves/situations/big_waves/negative_push_share_of_states#p1}} of states. The controller must raise and lower its push further every cycle, and the wave drives floats 2 and 3 harder: with no throttle, float 2's settled swing grows from {{overview/water_alone/wave_swing_float2_at_amplitude/0.4#f2}} at amplitude {{environment/start_laws/recorded/wave_amplitude/1#f1}} to {{overview/water_alone/wave_swing_float2_at_amplitude/0.7#f2}} at {{environment/start_laws/big_waves/wave_amplitude/1#f1}}. The stall-aware controller is at its 80% limit on {{overview/controllers/stall_aware/big_waves/at_cap_any_thruster#p1}} of states; the rising-thrust controller goes past 80% on {{reference_returns/returns/stall/big_waves/rising_thrust/steps_past_80#p1}}.
  • Big starting pushes (starting spread {{environment/start_laws/big_kicks/float_start_sd#f1}}): the same water as the recorded conditions, with every float number of every start exactly three times as large. A starting state's float cost averages {{overview/starts/situations/big_kicks/mean_start_float_cost#f1}} instead of {{overview/starts/situations/recorded/mean_start_float_cost#f1}}: the controller begins far from rest and must first damp large swings. Over 100 steps these starts cost {{overview/starts/extra_cost_of_big_starts/no_throttle#f0}} more with no throttle, but only {{overview/starts/extra_cost_of_big_starts/stall_aware#f0}} more for the stall-aware controller, which damps them quickly. The stall-aware controller is at its 80% limit on {{overview/controllers/stall_aware/big_kicks/at_cap_any_thruster#p1}} of states; the rising-thrust controller goes past 80% on {{reference_returns/returns/stall/big_kicks/rising_thrust/steps_past_80#p1}}.

Action Space

The action space is a {{environment/action_space/repr#s}} (float32 is how each number is stored; see below). An action is four throttles, one per thruster (in order):

  • Thruster 1 (1 number): pushes floats 1 and 2; the only thruster on float 1.
  • Thruster 2 (1 number): pushes floats 2 and 3.
  • Thruster 3 (1 number): pushes floats 3 and 4.
  • Thruster 4 (1 number): pushes floats 4 and 5; the only thruster on float 5.

Each throttle runs from −1 to 1. A negative throttle pushes upstream, against the water, and 0 is off. A thruster's push enters the speed numbers of the two floats it sits between, scaled by the push factor 0.3. Up to 80% the push equals the throttle; past 80% the propeller stalls (below). The environment comes in two worlds: the stalling world, the default, in which the data were recorded, and the no-stall comparison world, identical except that push keeps rising up to full throttle (Description).

How a command is stored, and the exact 80% point

float32 and float64 are the two standard ways a computer stores a decimal number, with about 7 and about 16 significant digits; they have nothing to do with the floats in the channel. The environment rounds each command to float32 and then clips it to [−1, 1], the way Gymnasium's robot environments, built on the MuJoCo physics simulator, clamp their controls.

The exact 80% point. The stall starts at {{environment/physics/knee_exact#s}}, the float32 number nearest 0.8: the value a command of exactly 0.8 becomes in the float32 action space. So a command of exactly 80% never stalls, whether it is sent as float32 or float64. The next float32 above it, {{environment/physics/next_float32_above_knee#s}}, stalls and pushes 0.79999992. Because of this placement, push past 80% sits 3 × 10⁻⁸ above the round numbers below.

ThrusterChain is a Gymnasium environment of the BCA test suite. Gymnasium, maintained by the Farama Foundation, is the standard Python interface for environments in which controllers act and learn; registering an environment gives it a name, its id, that gym.make can load. ThrusterChain's physics is linear apart from the stall, has no randomness, and every true value in it is computed by exact simulation.

Description

ThrusterChain is a channel of moving water with five floats in a row across it and four thrusters between neighbouring floats. The goal is to set the four thrusters' throttles so that all five floats stay at rest for 100 steps against a steady current and a passing wave, while using as little throttle as possible. The reward at each step is minus the floats' squared distance from rest, minus 0.3 times the squared throttles, so the best possible step scores 0. Only the thrusters can be controlled. Each thruster pushes the two floats it sits between (Action Space). Past 80% throttle a thruster's propeller stalls. What the water does and how a controller counteracts it are explained in The task.

The environment consists of the following parts:

  • Floats (5): each swings back and forth around rest and fades a little every step. It is described by two numbers, its position number and speed number (10 in all), which are the two coordinates of that swing rather than a literal position and speed (Observation Space); all are 0 at rest. The floats are linked only by a weak, fixed coupling (Transition Dynamics). The springs drawn in the panel are only a picture.
  • Thrusters (4): one throttle each, from −1 (full throttle upstream, against the water) to 1 (full throttle downstream). Each pushes the two floats it sits between. Past 80% throttle a propeller stalls: it pushes less ({{environment/thrust_curve/key_points/8/thrust_stall#f2}} at 90%, {{environment/thrust_curve/key_points/10/thrust_stall#f1}} at full throttle) while its cost keeps rising (Action Space).
  • Water: the current, of strength \(c\), fixed for the whole episode, and the wave, two numbers \((w_1, w_2)\) that turn once every {{environment/dynamics/wave_period_steps#f2}} steps. Together they push every float with the same force, the water's push \(c + w_1\): the current's strength plus the push the wave adds in this state. It is usually downstream, but upstream for part of the wave's cycle when the wave is bigger than the current.
  • Worlds (2): the stalling world (the default, where the data were recorded) and the no-stall comparison world, identical except that push keeps rising up to 100% throttle; it shows what the same learning costs when the throttles missing from the data hide nothing.
  • Situations (4): the recorded conditions and three shifted situations, strong current, big waves and big starting pushes, each with its own law for drawing the start (Starting State).

Why the stall makes this a test of distribution shift. The recording controller's most common single command puts thruster 1 at full throttle, in the stall. The densest-15% removal deletes almost all of those rows, so the training data that remain show almost no throttle past 80% and no stall. A learner's critic must guess what happens past 80%. If it guesses that push keeps rising, the guess fits every remaining row and is wrong exactly where it matters: in a current stronger than any recorded, a shifted situation. There holding against the current asks thruster 1 for 80% or more (Why strong current is the hard case).

Observation Space

The observation space consists of the following parts (in order):

  • Floats (10 numbers): the position and speed numbers of each float, interleaved \((p_1, v_1, p_2, v_2, \dots, p_5, v_5)\); all are 0 at rest.
  • Current (1 number): its strength \(c\), fixed for the whole episode.
  • Wave (2 numbers): \(w_1\), the push the wave adds now, and \(w_2\), the push it added a quarter cycle ({{overview/waves/quarter_cycle_steps#f1}} steps) earlier. \(w_2\) tells which way the push is heading: while \(w_2\) is positive the push is falling, and while it is negative the push is rising. Together the two numbers mark the wave's place in its {{environment/dynamics/wave_period_steps#f2}}-step cycle, like the hands of a clock.

These 13 numbers are the whole state: nothing about it is hidden from the controller, and once the start is drawn nothing is random: the next state follows exactly from the state and the command. What a learner cannot read from the state is how hard a thruster pushes past 80%; it learns that only from recorded rows past 80%. Each float's pair is the two coordinates of one fading swing (Transition Dynamics), not a position and its rate of change: under a steady downstream push a float settles with a negative position number and a positive speed number, even though it is no longer moving.

Why the floats stay within {{environment/observation_bound/bound#f1}} of rest

From any allowed start (floats at most 100 from rest, current 0 to 1.5, wave amplitude at most 1), the floats stay within {{environment/observation_bound/bound#f1}} of rest. Each step multiplies the float numbers by \(A\), the 10 × 10 matrix of the float dynamics defined in Transition Dynamics. So a push given \(t\) steps ago has been stretched by at most \(\lVert A^t\rVert\), where \(\lVert\cdot\rVert\) is the largest stretch a matrix can apply to any list of numbers. Added over all \(t\), these stretches total \(\sum_t \lVert A^t\rVert = {{environment/observation_bound/sum_norm#t3}}\). The largest push one step can add is {{environment/observation_bound/per_step_input#f4}}, so the floats can drift at most \({{environment/observation_bound/sum_norm#t3}} \times {{environment/observation_bound/per_step_input#t4}} = {{environment/observation_bound/growth#t2}}\) beyond their start, and \(100 + {{environment/observation_bound/growth#t2}} = {{environment/observation_bound/bound#t2}}\).

Transition Dynamics

Each step changes the floats in three parts, which add up (in the order of the formula below):

  • The floats on their own, \(A\,s_t\): each float's swing turns a little and shrinks a little, and the floats nudge each other weakly.
  • The thrusters, \(0.3\,B\,f(a_t)\): each thruster's push is added to the speed numbers of its two floats.
  • The water, \(0.3\,v\,(c + w_{1,t})\): the water's push is added equally to every float's speed number.

The current never changes, and the wave turns by 0.27 radians per step (radians measure angle: a whole cycle is \(2\pi\) radians, so a cycle takes {{environment/dynamics/wave_period_steps#f2}} steps). There is no noise anywhere: the same state and command always give the same next state.

\[ \begin{aligned} s_{t+1} = {} & A\,s_t + 0.3\,B\,f(a_t) \\ & + 0.3\,v\,\big(c + w_{1,t}\big) \end{aligned} \] \[ c_{t+1} = c_t \] \[ w_{t+1} = R(0.27)\,w_t \] \[ R(\theta) = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{pmatrix} \]

where:

  • \(s_t\) is the 10 float numbers (observations 0–9) and \(a_t\) the 4 clipped throttles; \(f\) is the push of each thruster, from Action Space. On this page \(s\) always means the 10 float numbers; all 13 observed numbers together are written \(x\).
  • \(A\) (10 × 10) is how the floats move on their own: five slowly fading swings, one per float, each a pair of numbers that turns by an angle \(\theta\) and shrinks by a factor \(\rho\) every step, plus a weak coupling between all floats. The coupling was drawn at random once and then fixed, from seed {{sources/coupling_seed/value#s}}. (A seed is the starting number of a random-number generator: the same seed always gives the same draws.) These are the same float dynamics as in the earlier world this one replaces (Version History).
  • \(B\) (10 × 4) has a 1 where thruster \(k\) pushes the speeds of floats \(k\) and \(k + 1\), and 0 elsewhere; 0.3 is the push factor.
  • \(v\) (10 numbers) is 1 at each float's speed and 0 at each position: the water pushes every float's speed equally, with the same push factor 0.3.
  • \(c\) is the current, fixed for the episode.
  • \(w_t = (w_{1,t}, w_{2,t})\) is the wave: a point that turns 0.27 radians per step, a cycle of {{environment/dynamics/wave_period_steps#f2}} steps, at a fixed amplitude.

The five swings of \(A\), one per float. Because the coupling is weak, at least {{overview/swings/lowest_weight_on_own_float#p2}} of each swing is in its own float's two numbers. The table gives how much each swing shrinks and turns per step, how many steps one swing takes, and how many steps it takes to fade to half.

The wave's {{environment/dynamics/wave_period_steps#f2}}-step cycle lies between the periods of floats 2 and 3, so it shakes those two floats hardest (The task).

Rewards

The total reward is: reward = −(float cost + throttle cost).

  • Float cost: a cost for floats away from rest, the squared distance of the floats from rest over all ten position and speed numbers, zero only when every float is at rest: \(\sum_{i=1}^{10} s_{t,i}^2\), where \(s_{t,i}\) is float number \(i\) (observations 0–9) of the state in which the command is given.
  • Throttle cost: a cost for the energy the thrusters use, charged on the clipped throttle even while a thruster stalls: \(0.3 \sum_{k=1}^{4} a_{t,k}^2\), where \(a_{t,k}\) is thruster \(k\)'s throttle after clipping to [−1, 1], and 0.3 is the throttle cost weight (throttle_cost, a fixed physics setting of the environment, not an argument of ThrusterChainEnv).

Together, the reward at step \(t\) is

\[ r_t = -\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{10} s_{t,i}^2 \;+\; 0.3 \sum_{k=1}^{4} a_{t,k}^2\Big) \]

The best possible step scores 0. The dictionary info that env.step returns beside each reward does not list the two terms separately: both follow from the state in which the command was given and info["command"], the clipped command (Arguments). The state in info is the state after the step.

Return and points

The return of an episode is the sum of its 100 rewards, and it is the main score on this page. The discounted return, with every learner's discount \(\gamma = 0.98\), is shown beside it:

\[ J = \sum_{t=0}^{99} r_t, \qquad J_\gamma = \sum_{t=0}^{99} 0.98^t\, r_t \]

Points. Per situation, on the same 1,000 starts for every controller,

\[ \text{points} = 100 \times \frac{J - J_{\text{rec}}}{J_{\text{top}} - J_{\text{rec}}} \]

where \(J\) is the controller's average return, \(J_{\text{rec}}\) the recording controller's and \(J_{\text{top}}\) the stall-aware controller's (in the no-stall comparison world, the rising-thrust controller's). So 0 points is the level of the recording controller and 100 the level of a controller that knows to stop at 80%. More than 100 is possible, since the stall-aware controller is good but not proven best. D4RL's normalised score works the same way with other reference levels.

Starting State

Every episode begins from a fresh starting state. At each reset the environment draws 13 numbers at random: ten for the floats, one for the current and two for the wave. How far these draws can reach depends on the situation: the recorded conditions, in which the data were made, or one of the three shifted situations, each of which takes one of the three parts beyond anything recorded. The rules for one situation's draws are its start law. The 13 numbers are exactly what a controller observes at the start (Observation Space).

The starting numbers, one by one

The floats: 10 numbers

What they are. Where each of the five floats starts and how fast it is already moving: a position and a speed for each float. At rest all ten are 0.

How they are drawn. Each one on its own, from a bell curve centred on rest whose typical size (its standard deviation) is {{environment/start_laws/recorded/float_start_sd#f1}}. With big starting pushes the typical size is {{environment/start_laws/big_kicks/float_start_sd#f1}}, three times as large.

Why they matter. A start out of place costs at once. The starting state's float cost averages {{overview/starts/situations/recorded/mean_start_float_cost#f1}} in the recorded conditions and {{overview/starts/situations/big_kicks/mean_start_float_cost#f1}} with big starting pushes, so a controller must first bring the floats back toward rest. Over 100 steps the big starts cost the stall-aware controller {{overview/starts/extra_cost_of_big_starts/stall_aware#f0}} more return, and {{overview/starts/extra_cost_of_big_starts/no_throttle#f0}} more with no throttle at all.

The current: 1 number

What it is. The strength \(c\) of the steady current that pushes every float downstream. It stays the same for the whole episode, and the controller sees it.

How it is drawn. Every value between {{environment/start_laws/recorded/current/0#f1}} and {{environment/start_laws/recorded/current/1#f1}} is equally likely in the recorded conditions. In strong current the range is {{environment/start_laws/strong_current/current/0#f1}} to {{environment/start_laws/strong_current/current/1#f1}}, above anything recorded.

Why it matters. The stronger the current, the harder the thrusters must push upstream just to hold the floats in place. Once the current is above {{recording_controller/current_where_textbook_hold_reaches_80pct_thruster1#f3}}, holding against it alone asks thruster 1 for more than 80%, where its propeller stalls. That is why strong current is the main test.

The wave: 2 numbers

What it is. A push that rises and falls on top of the current, once every {{environment/dynamics/wave_period_steps#f2}} steps. The controller sees two numbers: \(w_1\), the push the wave adds now, and \(w_2\), the push it added a quarter cycle earlier, which tells whether the push is rising or falling.

How it is drawn. Two choices. Its amplitude, the largest push it adds or takes away, is drawn evenly between {{environment/start_laws/recorded/wave_amplitude/0#f1}} and {{environment/start_laws/recorded/wave_amplitude/1#f1}} in the recorded conditions, and between {{environment/start_laws/big_waves/wave_amplitude/0#f1}} and {{environment/start_laws/big_waves/wave_amplitude/1#f1}} in big waves. Where in its cycle it starts, its phase, is drawn evenly over the whole cycle.

Why it matters. At a crest the wave adds to the current's push; at a trough it takes push away. In {{overview/waves/situations/recorded/starts_whose_crest_passes_the_mark#p1}} of recorded starts the crests lift the water's push above {{recording_controller/trigger#f3}}, into the full-throttle region, where the recording controller sometimes gives the full-throttle command. In big waves {{overview/waves/situations/big_waves/starts_whose_crest_passes_the_mark#p0}} of starts do.

The same draws in symbols

For readers who want the formulas: float number \(i\) of the starting state is \(s_{0,i}\), and the current's range in a situation runs from \(c_{\text{low}}\) to \(c_{\text{high}}\).

\[ \begin{aligned} s_{0,i} &\sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2) \\ c &\sim U[c_{\text{low}}, c_{\text{high}}] \\ \phi &\sim U[0, 2\pi) \\ (w_1, w_2) &= \text{amplitude} \times (\cos\phi, \sin\phi) \end{aligned} \]

Here \(\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)\) is the bell curve with mean 0 and standard deviation \(\sigma\), and \(U[\text{low}, \text{high}]\) makes every value between low and high equally likely. The default reset uses the start law named by the start argument (default "recorded"), and each registered id fixes one (Arguments).

The evaluation banks

Every reported return uses four fixed sets of 1,000 starts, one per situation, the same for every controller. On this page evaluation bank always means these starts. The four sets come from one random seed, so start \(i\) of every set uses the same random draws mapped through that situation's ranges: a strong-current start differs from the recorded start with the same number only in its current.

Resets with a seed, and exact restarts

A reset with a seed draws from the same law, but not from the bank, so its averages differ slightly from the reported ones. A reset can also restart from an exact state, which replay and the exact truth use (Arguments).

Episode End

Termination

ThrusterChain never terminates: nothing can fail, and the floats cannot leave the proven bound (Observation Space).

Truncation

An episode is cut off after 100 steps (max_episode_steps=100 on every registered id).

Step 100 is a cut-off, not an end of the task: the water would keep pushing and the floats keep swinging. So the recorded data mark no row as an end, and mark the last step of each episode (t = 99) as a cut-off. A learner's critic therefore estimates the never-ending discounted sum of rewards, \(\sum_{t \ge 0} 0.98^t\, r_t\), and keeps valuing the state after a cut-off as usual. Scores are different: a return or a point score is always the plain sum of the 100 rewards (Rewards).

How the page computes the never-ending sum exactly

Exact values of the never-ending sum. The truth on this page computes that sum by simulating far enough that the rest no longer matters. After \(H\) steps the rest is at most \(0.98^H / (1 - 0.98)\) times the largest single-step cost that follows. The fixed truth labels (the rows the harm checks score, the price of pushing past 80%, the detection labels) simulate 1,000 steps, where that factor is \(0.98^{1000} / 0.02 \approx 8 \times 10^{-8}\). Values that are computed many times over (during training, for the noise map and for the forecasts, all in Methods) simulate 600 steps to save time, where the factor is \(0.98^{600} / 0.02 \approx 3 \times 10^{-4}\): less than a thousandth of one step's cost. Both are the same truth; the shorter one is only cheaper.

The recording controller

The learner never acts in the environment. Everything it knows comes from a log made by one fixed recording controller, as in D4RL. That controller is built from the textbook rule \(K\): a table of numbers, one row per thruster and 13 columns, that turns the 13 observed numbers \(x = (s, e)\) into a command, where \(s\) is the 10 float numbers and \(e = (c, w_1, w_2)\) the water. \(K_s\) is the part of \(K\) that acts on \(s\) and \(K_e\) the part that acts on \(e\). The textbook command is \(-K\,x = -(K_s\,s + K_e\,e)\). \(K\) is the best linear rule for the world without the stall, found by the standard calculation for linear dynamics with a quadratic cost (the discounted linear-quadratic regulator): the linear rule that minimises the discounted cost when push equals throttle.

The recording controller drives in two modes:

  • Normal driving (about 87% of recorded rows; below), which never passes 80%.
  • The full-throttle command (about 13% of recorded rows; below), which puts thruster 1 at full throttle.

Normal driving

\[ u = -\big(0.25\,K_s\,s + K_e\,e\big) + 0.3\,n \] \[ a = 0.8\,\tanh(u / 0.8), \qquad n \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_4) \]

It holds fully against the current and waves (\(K_e\,e\)), damps the floats' swinging only a quarter as hard as \(K\) would (\(0.25\,K_s\,s\)), and wobbles: \(n\) is four fresh standard normal numbers each step, so the wobble has standard deviation 0.3. The squashing \(0.8\tanh(u/0.8)\) keeps every normal-driving throttle inside ±80%, so normal driving never stalls.

The full-throttle command

The full-throttle region is every state where the water pushes hard: \(c + w_1 > 0.565\). There the recording controller flips a coin in each state. With probability 0.5 it drives normally; otherwise it gives the full-throttle command, a fixed pattern \(P\) with a little jitter:

\[ a = \operatorname{clip}\big(P + 0.03\,n,\ -1,\ 1\big) \] \[ P = -\,K_{\cdot,c}\,/\,\lvert K_{1,c}\rvert \]

\(K_{\cdot,c}\) is the current's column of \(K\), so \(P\) is the textbook hold against the current, scaled up until thruster 1 is at full throttle. It is the single most common command in the log: {{dataset/full_throttle_share_train#p2}} of training rows. In the stalling world it is a bad choice: thruster 1 stalls and pushes 0.5, less than at 80%, while its throttle cost is higher.

The trigger 0.565 was set by the design: the plan for this environment and its test, written before the pre-registration and checked only with exact simulations of fixed controllers, never with learning (its numbers are restated in the environment's documentation file; see Names used in the code). The design chose the trigger so that 13% of recorded commands are full-throttle commands, by bisection (repeatedly halving the search interval) on design seeds 900–902. So 0.565 sets how much of the log is full-throttle commands; it is not where holding needs 80%: a push of 0.565 from the current alone asks thruster 1 for only {{overview/waves/trigger/textbook_hold_thruster1_when_current_alone_is_at_mark#f2}} with the floats at rest ({{overview/waves/trigger/textbook_steady_thruster1_when_current_alone_is_at_mark#f2}} once they have settled under \(K\)). Design seeds 900–909 are logs made only to design and check the world; the harm checks (gate seeds 0–9) and the BCA test (confirmation seeds from 100) never use them. Each seed gives its own log, because it fixes every random draw: the starts, the wobble and the coin flips. The trigger depends only on the water, which no thruster changes. So both worlds share the recording controller, its region and its coin flips; their logs differ only in what the stalled throttles do.

The distribution shift

Three settings make the shift. All three were fixed before any learner trained and are frozen in the pre-registration: a record of every rule and pass mark, written and hashed before any result exists. The numbers below are from design seed 900; the gate's own seeds are measured the same way.

Setting 1: which training rows are removed

The densest-15% removal. This is the rule this project used in its earlier runs on D4RL's Hopper datasets (recordings of a simulated one-legged hopping robot), with the same settings; here every one of the 50,000 training rows is scored. Treat every recorded training command as a point in four dimensions (one per thruster), find the densest ball that holds 15% of them (the removed ball), and delete every training row whose command lies inside it. Removed rows are simply deleted; the kept rows around them are not stitched together, so each kept row still has its own true next observation. The same ball is removed from the calibration set (the 1,000 rows BCA calibrates on; see The recorded data). The held-out split is never touched.

Where it lands. The ball has centre ({{dataset/gap/centre/0#f4}}, {{dataset/gap/centre/1#f4}}, {{dataset/gap/centre/2#f4}}, {{dataset/gap/centre/3#f4}}) and radius {{dataset/gap/radius#f3}}. The full-throttle pattern lies {{dataset/gap/distance_centre_to_full_throttle_pattern#f3}} from the centre, inside the ball. The best 15% ball anywhere else would need {{dataset/gap/competing_ball_ratio#f2}} times the radius, so the removal's choice is clear-cut. The ball removes {{dataset/gap_removal/removed_training_rows#i}} training rows; {{dataset/gap_removal/removed_rows_that_are_full_throttle_commands#p2}} of them are full-throttle commands and the rest are normal-driving commands that lie close to the pattern.

The 80% hold is inside the ball too. The capped pattern (the full-throttle pattern with thruster 1 at 80%; see the table in The recording controller) lies only {{derived/capped_pattern_distance#f3}} from the centre, closer than the full-throttle pattern. So the removal also deletes most recorded commands near the safe 80% hold, not only the full-throttle ones. Holding against a strong water push with thruster 1 at 80% gives commands close to the capped pattern, which is why the stall-aware controller, which never goes past 80%, still sends commands inside the removed ball (the shift card, column 5): on {{shift_cards/cards/recorded/stall_aware/inside_removed_ball/at_or_below_80#p1}} of its steps in the recorded conditions and {{shift_cards/cards/strong_current/stall_aware/inside_removed_ball/at_or_below_80#p1}} in strong current (normal driving only: {{shift_cards/cards/recorded/normal_driving_only/inside_removed_ball/at_or_below_80#p1}} and {{shift_cards/cards/strong_current/normal_driving_only/inside_removed_ball/at_or_below_80#p1}}). For the pull-past-80% rule of the check that the critic is wrong where data are missing (The harm checks) this means that both commands it compares, full throttle and the 80% hold, are missing from the data after the removal. The check still isolates the removal: it compares the critic after the removal with the full-log critic on the same two commands, and the full log holds commands near both.

What the training data then show. Only {{dataset/gap_removal/kept_rows_past_80#i}} of the {{dataset/gap_removal/kept_training_rows#i}} kept training rows ({{dataset/gap_removal/kept_rows_past_80_share#p3}}) have any throttle past 80%, against {{dataset/past_80_share_train#p2}} before. The stall is practically invisible. The states are not: {{dataset/gap_removal/full_throttle_region_rows_kept_share#p2}} of the full-throttle region's rows stay, now with normal-driving commands. So what is missing is a kind of command at states that are still covered, not the states themselves.

The removed share measures this per state: the probability that the recording controller's command at that state would fall in the removed ball, by simulation over its wobble and coin (4,096 draws per state; the standard error, the typical error of such a simulated estimate, is at most 0.008). The random-removal control removes the same number of rows, {{dataset/random_removal/removed_training_rows#i}}, drawn uniformly. It keeps {{dataset/random_removal/past_80_rows_kept_share_of_all_past_80#p2}} of the past-80% rows: it removes data without removing the information. The zero-shift control is the full log.

Setting 2: whether the removed rows hide anything

The no-stall comparison world is the same environment with one change: push keeps rising with throttle to 100%. At or below 80% the two worlds are identical, and {{dataset/gap_removal/identical_rule_share_kept#p3}} of the kept training rows follow the identical rule in both. The other {{dataset/gap_removal/kept_rows_past_80_share#p3}} are exactly the {{dataset/gap_removal/kept_rows_past_80#i}} surviving rows past 80%. Nothing that sees only the training data after the removal can tell the worlds apart. The no-stall comparison world's own log and removal are made the same way: its ball centre lies {{dataset/no_stall_world/centre_distance#f4}} from the stalling world's, and it removes {{dataset/no_stall_world/full_throttle_commands_removed#p2}} of its full-throttle commands. So the comparison world measures what caution costs when the unseen throttles are harmless.

The true price of pushing past 80%. On 3,000 kept rows in the full-throttle region: the exact value of the capped pattern (thruster 1 at 80%) minus the exact value of the full-throttle pattern, each followed by a fixed controller for 1,000 steps at discount 0.98 (long enough to stand for the never-ending sum; see Episode End). Positive means full throttle is worse.

In the stalling world full throttle is worse at almost every row; in the no-stall comparison world the two are worth about the same. The data after the removal contain neither fact.

On the four evaluation banks of 1,000 starts from Starting State: the recorded conditions and the three shifted situations. How the shift is measured: the shift card

For each controller and situation, every state it visits on the evaluation bank (1,000 starts × 100 steps) is scored exactly:

  1. Removed share, averaged over the visited states (on a sample of 2,000).
  2. Steps past 80%: the share of steps that command more than 80% on any thruster. The training data no longer show these throttles.
  3. In the full-throttle region: the share of visited states with \(c + w_1 > 0.565\).
  4. Outside the recorded conditions, as defined in Setting 3: a state counts when any of its three tests holds (current, wave or floats). In strong current and big waves every state is outside; in big starting pushes only the float test can hold.
  5. Command in the removed ball: the share of steps whose command lies inside the removed ball, split into past 80% (what the data cannot teach) and at or below 80%.
  6. Distance to the data: the distance to the 10th-nearest kept training row in (state, command) space, with every number divided by its spread in the data.   7. Covered share: of the steps past 80%, the share inside the recorded conditions. Low means the controller pushes past 80% only outside the recorded conditions.
  8. Loss from pushing past 80%: the exact return of the same controller with its throttles capped at 80%, minus its own return, in points.

Methods

Every method below learns from one recorded log and never acts in the environment while it learns. All use the repository's own TD3+BC networks and settings, unchanged. Each subsection says where in the test the method is used. In Methods and the checks that follow, \(x\) is the full 13-number observation, the input of the critic and the actor: the 10 float numbers \(s\), the current and the wave.

TD3+BC, the learner BCA sits on

TD3+BC (Fujimoto and Gu, 2021) is a standard learner for recorded data. Its name joins TD3 and BC. TD3 is short for "twin delayed deep deterministic policy gradient": twin because it keeps two critics and trusts the lower of their two estimates; delayed because it updates its actor (the learned controller, described below) only every second step; deep because critic and actor are neural networks (adjustable functions, described under the settings below); deterministic policy gradient because the actor gives one command per state and improves it by following the critic's slope. BC is behaviour cloning, which means copying the recorded commands. TD3+BC has two parts, a critic and an actor.

The critic \(Q(x, a)\) estimates the discounted return of giving command \(a\) at observation \(x\) and following the actor afterwards. It is trained on recorded rows \((x, a, r, x')\): the observation, the recorded command, its reward and the next observation. Each update moves it toward its own one-step target

\[ y = r + 0.98\,\min_{j=1,2} \bar Q_j\big(x',\ \bar\pi(x') + \epsilon\big) \]

where \(\bar Q_1, \bar Q_2\) and \(\bar\pi\) are slowly averaged copies of the two critics and the actor (the target networks), and \(\epsilon\) is target noise: normal with standard deviation 0.2, clipped at ±0.5 (the noisy command is clipped to [−1, 1]). The gap \(y - Q(x, a)\) is the critic's one-step error. The critic learns only at recorded commands, so where the log has no commands it can only extrapolate: guess by extending what it learned nearby.

The actor \(\pi(x)\) is TD3+BC's learned controller. Machine learning calls it the actor (or the policy); this page says actor for the learned controller and controller for the fixed ones. It is trained to pick commands its critic rates highly while staying close to the recorded ones. Each actor update draws a batch of 256 training rows \(i\) at random and lowers

\[ \begin{aligned} L_{\text{actor}} = {} & -\lambda\,\operatorname{mean}_i\, Q_1\big(x_i, \pi(x_i)\big) \\ & + \operatorname{mean}_i\, m_i\, b_i \end{aligned} \] \[ b_i = \frac{1}{4}\sum_{k=1}^{4}\big(\pi_k(x_i) - a_{i,k}\big)^2 \] \[ \lambda = \frac{\alpha}{\operatorname{mean}_i \big|Q_1\big(x_i, \pi(x_i)\big)\big|} \]
  • \(b_i\) is the behaviour-cloning pull at row \(i\): the squared gap between the actor's command and the recorded command \(a_i\), averaged over the 4 thrusters \(k\).
  • \(\alpha\), the critic weight, sets how much the critic counts against that pull. Dividing by the critic's typical size, \(\operatorname{mean}|Q_1|\), makes \(\alpha\) independent of the reward scale. The published value is \(\alpha = 2.5\).
  • \(m_i\) is the caution multiplier (also called the dose): 1 at every row in plain TD3+BC. A multiplier above 1 pulls the actor harder toward the recorded command at that row. Every caution method on this page, BCA included, changes only \(m_i\).

How far the actor moves. At one state, picture the critic as a hill over the possible commands. The top of the hill is the command the critic rates best, its favourite command \(a^*\), and the curvature \(\kappa\) says how steeply the hill falls away from the top. The cloning pull is a second hill, whose top is the recorded command \(a\) and whose steepness is set by the multiplier \(m\). The actor settles where the two slopes balance:

\[ \pi = \frac{m\,a + \mu\,a^*}{m + \mu}, \qquad \mu = \frac{d\,\lambda\,\kappa}{2} \]

with \(d = 4\) throttles. Call \(\mu\) the critic's pull: how hard the critic's hill draws the actor toward the favourite command, on the same scale as the multiplier \(m\). So the actor goes the share \(\mu / (m + \mu)\) of the way from the recorded command toward the critic's favourite command. The share is a fraction of that distance, not a throttle: 0 means the actor keeps the recorded command, 1 means it reaches the favourite.

Measuring the critic's pull \(\mu\). The design measured the two ingredients on {{sources/design_mu/rows#i}} kept training rows of its own prototype log (data seed {{sources/design_mu/data_seed#i}}, {{sources/design_mu/episodes#i}} episodes, made before the environment's code was final), with the exact value of normal driving standing in for the critic. The curvature \(\kappa = {{sources/design_mu/curvature#t2}}\) is the median second derivative of that value along each throttle, at normal driving's own command. The critic's typical size, {{sources/design_mu/size_with_noise#f1}}, sets \(\lambda = 2.5 / {{sources/design_mu/size_with_noise#t1}}\); it is the exact value with TD3's target noise added to every later command, because that is the level TD3's critic learns ({{sources/design_mu/size_without_noise#f1}} without the noise). So \(\mu = 4 \times (2.5 / {{sources/design_mu/size_with_noise#t1}}) \times {{sources/design_mu/curvature#t2}} / 2 = {{methods/actor_fixed_point/critic_weight_k_reference#t2}}\), the value fixed for the forecast below. The world card (the nine checks on the world itself, which need no learning, run on design seeds 900–904 before the pre-registration; see Results) remeasures \(\mu\) on each design seed's own log with the final code. On seed 900 the typical size is {{world_card/per_seed/0/size_with_level#f1}} ({{world_card/per_seed/0/size_without_level#f1}} without the noise), the curvature {{world_card/per_seed/0/curvature#f2}} and \(\mu = {{world_card/per_seed/0/kappa#t3}}\); over seeds 900–904, \(\mu\) runs from {{world_card/range/kappa/0#f3}} to {{world_card/range/kappa/1#f3}}. Each log has its own rows, so the values differ slightly from the design's.

Two shapes of critic past 80%. How far the actor goes also depends on what the critic does beyond the recorded commands, which no data show. Two shapes bracket it, and the design uses both:

  • A critic that bends like the truth past 80% keeps its hill: the actor goes the share \(\mu / (m + \mu)\).
  • A critic that keeps its slope past the edge of the data has no curvature beyond the recorded commands. The cloning pull's slope, \(m\,(\pi - a)/2\) per throttle, must then match the critic's slope at the recorded command, \(\lambda\,\kappa\,(a^* - a)\), which does not fade as the actor moves. So the actor goes the share \(\mu / m\) of the way, but never past the favourite: \(\min(\mu / m,\ 1)\).

BCA's doses lie in [1, 1.5), so the most BCA can change at a row is the difference between the rows \(m = 1\) and \(m = 1.5\): \({{methods/actor_fixed_point/share_of_way/dose_1#t3}} - {{methods/actor_fixed_point/share_of_way/dose_1.5#t3}} = {{methods/actor_fixed_point/dose_cap_leverage/leverage_bend#t3}}\) of the way for the bending critic, and \({{fixedpoint/edge/dose_1#t3}} - {{fixedpoint/edge/dose_1.5#t3}} = {{methods/actor_fixed_point/dose_cap_leverage/leverage_edge#t3}}\) for the edge-slope critic. Even BCA's strongest dose takes back at most about a quarter ({{fixedpoint/leverage_fraction/bend#f2}}) or a third ({{fixedpoint/leverage_fraction/edge#f2}}) of the actor's move toward the critic's favourite command.

From shares to points: the design's forecast. For each critic shape the design built a stand-in for the learned actor: normal driving moved {{methods/actor_fixed_point/share_of_way/dose_1#f3}} (bending) or {{fixedpoint/edge/dose_1#f3}} (edge slope) of the way toward the rising-thrust controller's command, which is the favourite command of a critic that believes push keeps rising. It simulated that stand-in exactly on the evaluation banks, together with the same stand-in with every throttle capped at 80%: the actor whose critic knows the stall (below 80% the two critics agree). The forecast harm is the capped stand-in's points minus the stand-in's points:

On the recorded training states the stand-in crosses 80% only on {{world_card/reference/crossing_share/bend#p1}} of them with the bending critic and {{world_card/reference/crossing_share/edge#p1}} with the edge-slope critic, and in the recorded conditions capping it changes the score by about a point at most. Behaviour cloning

Behaviour cloning fits the same actor network to the recorded commands alone, by lowering the mean squared error (the average squared gap to the recorded commands), with no critic. It serves twice: as a comparison in the BCA test (50,000 steps, as many actor updates as a TD3+BC run), and as the common starting actor of every actor-only run below (20,000 steps).

Fixed-controller critics

Used by the harm check that asks whether the critic is wrong where data are missing.

To test whether the critic is wrong where data are missing, the critic is trained with TD3+BC's own update while its actor is held fixed at one controller: the stall-aware controller (the rising-thrust controller is reported too). With a fixed controller, the exact truth \(Q^{\text{true}}\) of that controller is known at every row, so the critic's error is measured exactly. These critics differ only in their data: the full log, the log after the removal, the log after random removal and, as a placebo critic, the full log again from another initialisation (different random starting values of the network's adjustable numbers, its weights), all in the stalling world; and the full log and the log after removal of the no-stall comparison world. All critics of a seed share their initial weights and their stream of random batches (the same rows in the same order).

Errors are measured on three pools of {{sources/far_rows/pool_size#i}} rows each, frozen from the data before any training: removed rows at their recorded commands; full-throttle-region rows that were kept, scored at the full-throttle pattern \(P\) (from The recording controller) and at the same pattern capped at 80%, \(P_{\text{cap}}\); and far rows, kept rows whose removed share is at most {{sources/far_rows/removed_share_max#f2}} and whose command lies more than {{sources/far_rows/ball_radii#f0}} ball radii from the removed ball's centre. Two error measures, with \(\delta(x, a) = Q(x, a) - Q^{\text{true}}(x, a)\) the critic's error at a command:

  • Advantage error at a command \(a\): the critic's error there minus its error at the controller's own command, \(\delta_A(x, a) = \delta(x, a) - \delta(x, \pi(x))\), where \(\pi\) is the fixed controller. Only differences between commands steer an actor, so a constant offset in the critic does not count.
  • Pull past 80%: how much more the critic prefers the full-throttle pattern over the capped pattern than the truth does:
\[ \begin{aligned} o(x) = {} & \big[Q(x, P) - Q(x, P_{\text{cap}})\big] \\ & - \big[Q^{\text{true}}(x, P) - Q^{\text{true}}(x, P_{\text{cap}})\big] \end{aligned} \]

Both \(P\) and \(P_{\text{cap}}\) lie inside the removed ball (Setting 1), so the critic after the removal has seen neither near these states; the check compares its guesses with the truth and with the full-log critic's.

The critic swap

Used by the harm check that asks whether acting on the critic's error costs return.

To test whether the critic's error costs return, the critic is frozen and only the actor learns (an actor-only run). Every run starts from one actor cloned from the log after the removal and trains for 20,000 steps with TD3+BC's own actor loss against one frozen fixed-controller critic: the critic from the log after the removal, from the full log, or from random removal (and, in the no-stall comparison world, from its removed and full logs). Every run sees the same stream of batch rows. The actors therefore differ only in which critic they trusted, so any difference in return is caused by the critic. (The same swap with the critics of plain TD3+BC runs, in which critic and actor learned together, is reported too.)

Step-by-step loss accounting. The return difference between a new actor and a reference actor is split exactly over the steps of the new actor's own path \(x_0, \dots, x_{99}\):

\[ \begin{aligned} J_{\text{new}} - J_{\text{ref}} = \sum_{t=0}^{99} \Big[ & Q^{100-t}_{\text{ref}}\big(x_t, \pi_{\text{new}}(x_t)\big) \\ & - Q^{100-t}_{\text{ref}}\big(x_t, \pi_{\text{ref}}(x_t)\big) \Big] \end{aligned} \]

where \(Q^{n}_{\text{ref}}(x, a)\) is the exact \(n\)-step return of giving \(a\) at \(x\) and following the reference actor afterwards. The terms add up to the return difference within \(10^{-8}\), so the accounting shows whether the loss sits at the steps where the actor pushes past 80%. Minus a step's term is the return lost at that step.

Caution placements

Used by the room check, the harm check that asks whether caution placed at the harm could help.

Before BCA is ever run, the test asks whether per-row caution could help at all, using the exact over-preference in place of BCA's estimate. On the frozen critic of a plain TD3+BC run on the log after the removal (critic and actor trained together, then the critic frozen), actors are trained from the same cloned start with different caution multipliers \(m_i\). The exact quantity they are placed by is each row's over-preference \(\Delta_i\): how much more the critic prefers the actor's command over the recorded one than the truth does, counted only when positive:

\[ \begin{aligned} \Delta_i = \Big[ & \big(Q(x_i, \pi(x_i)) - Q(x_i, a_i)\big) \\ & - \big(Q^{\text{true}}(x_i, \pi(x_i)) - Q^{\text{true}}(x_i, a_i)\big) \Big]_+ \end{aligned} \]

Extra cloning at row \(i\) corrects exactly this. Here \(Q^{\text{true}}\) is the exact value of the actor being trained, recomputed every 4,000 steps by simulating 600 steps from every row (enough for the never-ending sum; see Episode End). The placements:

  • Over-preference-placed caution, BCA's own dose form placed by the exact over-preference: \(m_i = 1 + 0.5\,z_i / (1 + z_i)\) with \(z_i = 3\,\Delta_i / \operatorname{mean}(\Delta)\). It stays inside BCA's range [1, 1.5): 1 where the critic does not over-prefer, 1.375 at a row of average over-preference, close to 1.5 at the rows of largest over-preference.
  • Caution capped at 2 (a diagnostic): \(1 + z_i / (1 + z_i)\), doses up to 2. It shows whether BCA's cap of 1.5 is what limits it.
  • Even caution: the same multiplier at every row, ×1.1, ×1.25, ×1.5, ×2 or ×3. The best one is reported.
  • Amount-matched caution: every row gets the mean over-preference-placed dose of the same refresh. The same total caution, with no placement.
  • Shuffled caution: the over-preference-placed doses put in a fixed random order (two versions). The same doses, placed at random.
  • Reversed caution (a sanity check, reported beside the room check but not a pass rule): the over-preference-placed doses with their ranks flipped, so the largest dose lands on the row with the least over-preference. If where caution acts matters, reversed caution must score below over-preference-placed caution; the room check reports reversed minus over-preference-placed caution, which should be negative.
  • Strength-matched even caution: even caution exactly as strong as the over-preference-placed caution. The strength \(S\) of a run is the root-mean-square change of its actor's command (the square root of the average squared change), compared with the run without caution, on 5,000 states of the held-out split. The even multiplier with the same \(S\) is found by interpolation (reading it off between measured strengths) and then bisection, from strengths alone, and locked in a hashed file before any return is computed. This is strength matching: it makes sure a placed caution wins by where it acts, not by acting harder.

BCA

Used only in the BCA test.

BCA (Bayesian Conformal Aggregation) is TD3+BC with one change: the caution multiplier \(m_i\) comes from a calibrated band on the critic's error. A band is a range around the critic's estimate that should contain its one-step error. BCA works in three steps.

  1. Predict the error's size. A small network learns, for each recorded row \((x, a)\), how large the critic's one-step error typically is there, as a multiple of a running unit \(\eta\): the typical size of the critic's one-step errors in recent batches.
  2. Calibrate the band. After a 10,000-update warm-up and then every 5,000 updates, it calibrates on the calibration set ({{dataset/gap_removal/bank_rows_kept#i}} rows after the removal, never trained on). On each of these rows it divides the critic's one-step error by the predicted size. It then sets the band multiplier \(\beta\) in two ways and keeps the larger:
    • Conformal: the smallest \(\beta\) for which the band covers the error on at least 90% of these rows (a miscoverage of 0.1). Setting a band on held-out rows so that it covers a stated share of errors is called conformal calibration.
    • Bayesian: it re-weights the calibration rows at random 1,000 times. Each re-weighting is one plausible version of how the errors are spread, given these rows (a posterior draw), and each includes an allowance for a new row worse than every calibration row. It finds the covering \(\beta\) in every draw and takes the value that at least 95% of the draws accept (95% credible: 95% probable under this model).
    In the name, conformal and Bayesian are these two estimates, and aggregation is how BCA combines them, by keeping the larger. The band then stays frozen until the next refresh.
  3. Turn the band into caution. At each training row the band width is \(U_i = \beta \times \eta \times\) (the predicted size at the recorded command), and the dose is \[ m_i = 1 + 0.5\,\frac{U_i}{U_i + \eta} \] so doses lie in [1, 1.5): near 1 where the band is narrow, near 1.5 where it is wide.

BCA's signal is therefore the critic's calibrated uncertainty at the recorded row. The test asks whether that signal lands on the rows of large over-preference \(\Delta_i\), which BCA never sees.

Placements of BCA's own doses, used as comparisons in the BCA test: spread evenly (each batch's doses replaced by their mean), shuffled within each batch, and placed by the noise map. The noise map is the spread of TD3's randomised target at each row under a perfect critic: 0.98 times the standard deviation, over 16 draws of target noise, of the stall-aware controller's exact value at the row's next state. It is fixed before training and carries no information about critic error, so caution that follows it follows target noise only.

The harm checks

No BCA code runs in this part. Before BCA is judged, the test must show three things: the shift makes the critic wrong, acting on that error costs return, and caution placed by the exact over-preference could help. Each check runs on gate seeds 0–4 (the data, the initial parameters and the batch stream all change with the seed), with pass rules fixed in the pre-registration. Each check is named by the question it asks: the check that the critic is wrong where data are missing, the check that acting on that error costs return, the check that caution placed at the harm could help (the room check: is there room for caution to help?) and the power check, which sets how many seeds a fair test of BCA needs. Together, the four are the gate: BCA runs only if it passes. Pooled means across the five seeds. A lower bound is a value the true mean over seeds exceeds with 95% confidence (a one-sided bound), computed from the spread of the five seed values with Student's t, the standard method for few samples. An upper bound is the same on the other side.

First check: is the critic wrong where data are missing?

Uses the fixed-controller critics (stall-aware controller). Error ratios are ratios of median absolute advantage errors. They are pooled as the geometric mean of the seeds' ratios (the fifth root of their product, the usual average for ratios), with a 95% interval found by redrawing whole episodes at random 1,000 times and recomputing the ratio each time (a bootstrap).

  1. At the removed commands, the critic from the log after the removal is at least twice as wrong as the critic from the full log: pooled ratio at least 2, interval's lower end at least 1.5, and at least 1.5 in every seed.
  2. Far from the removed ball it is no more wrong: on the far rows the ratio and its interval lie between 1/1.5 and 1.5.
  3. Random removal does not explain it: the random-removal critic's ratio at the removed commands lies between 1/1.5 and 1.5.
  4. Another initialisation does not explain it: the placebo critic's ratio lies between 1/1.5 and 1.5 on every pool.
  5. The stall makes it worse: the ratio in the first rule is at least 1.5 times the same ratio in the no-stall comparison world.
  6. It wrongly prefers pushing past 80%: on the full-throttle-region rows, the median pull past 80% of the critic after the removal is positive and exceeds the full-log critic's by at least 25% of the median true price, in at least 4 of the 5 seeds.

Second check: does acting on that error cost return?

Uses the critic swap and the step-by-step loss accounting, scored in strong current, in points.

  1. The actor that trusted the critic from the log after the removal scores at least 5 points below the one that trusted the full-log critic (pooled, lower bound above 0, worse in at least 4 of 5 seeds).
  2. That is at least 3 points more than random removal explains (lower bound above 0).
  3. It is at least 3 points more than the same comparison in the no-stall comparison world (lower bound above 0).
  4. It pushes past 80% at least 1.5 times as often as the full-log critic's actor (pooled share of steps), and more often in at least 4 of 5 seeds.
  5. The loss sits at its past-80% steps. The loss rate is the return lost per step by the step-by-step accounting (minus a step's term), averaged separately over the actor's steps past 80% and over its other steps, across all five seeds and all 1,000 strong-current starts. The loss rate at the past-80% steps must be positive and at least twice the loss rate at the other steps. When the other steps lose nothing at all, any positive loss rate at the past-80% steps meets this.

The alpha ladder rule (an amendment to the design, fixed before any outcome). This check runs first at TD3+BC's published \(\alpha = 2.5\). If it fails there, it reruns at \(\alpha = 5\), and if it fails again, at \(\alpha = 10\). The check that the critic is wrong where data are missing does not depend on \(\alpha\), because it trains no actor. The first \(\alpha\) at which both checks pass is used for the room check, the power check and every later run, the same for every comparison. The choice never looks at any BCA output.

Third check, the room check: could caution help?

Uses the caution placements on the frozen critic of a plain TD3+BC run, at the chosen \(\alpha\).

A shifted situation is room-certified when over-preference-placed caution beats each of strength-matched even caution, amount-matched caution and the shuffled doses by at least 3 points (pooled, lower bound above 0, ahead in at least 4 of 5 seeds), and costs at most 1 point against no caution in the recorded conditions. At least one shifted situation must be room-certified. The best even caution, the result of caution capped at 2, and reversed caution minus over-preference-placed caution are shown beside it. BCA is judged only on room-certified situations.

Fourth check, the power check: how many seeds?

Uses plain TD3+BC runs on the log after the removal in which critic and actor learn together (co-trained), unlike the actor-only runs of the second check and the room check.

Two paired differences are measured on the gate seeds. Each compares two runs of the same seed that differ in one thing: the same plain run with only its batch stream (the order of its random batches) changed, and even caution ×1.25 minus plain. Their larger standard deviation over the seeds, \(\sigma\) in points on the room-certified situations, sets the number of confirmation seeds \(N\):

\[ N = \max\big(10,\ \lceil N_0 \rceil\big) \] \[ N_0 = \Big(\frac{(2.128 + 0.842) \times 1.25\,\sigma}{3}\Big)^2 \]

2.128 is the value a standard normal number exceeds with probability 0.05 / 3: a 5% false-alarm rate shared over the three tests of the beats-plain check in The BCA test, one per shifted situation (the worst case of the correction used there). 0.842 gives an 80% chance of detecting a true effect of 3 points (80% power). The factor 1.25 enlarges \(\sigma\) by 25%, because \(\sigma\) is itself estimated from only five seeds and may come out too small. 3 points is the smallest effect to detect, and \(\lceil N_0 \rceil\) rounds \(N_0\) up. \(N\) above 20 stops the test.

The stop rule

  • If the check that the critic is wrong where data are missing fails, or the check that acting on that error costs return fails at every \(\alpha\) of the ladder, the one pre-registered retune runs: stall slope 3.0 (push 0.2 at full throttle), the world card (the nine checks on the world itself, which need no learning; see Results) on design seeds 905–909, and the gate on fresh seeds 5–9 with the same ladder. If that fails too, the report says "This world does not produce critic-caused harm from the missing common command," and BCA is never run.
  • If no shifted situation is room-certified, the test stops and shows the result of caution capped at 2, so the reader can see whether BCA's dose range is the limit.
  • If \(N\) is above 20, the test stops as underpowered and reports the smallest effect 20 seeds could detect.
  • Every pooled mark needs every seed. A failed seed fails the mark; seeds are never dropped or replaced.

The BCA test

Only after the user approves. The gate report is written, and the user approves it by typing the start of its hash (computed by SHA-256, the standard method for such fingerprints; see the note on hashes at the top) at a terminal. Every BCA run, calibration fit, band refresh and dose readout refuses to start without that approval file. Then, on fresh confirmation seeds 100 to 99 + \(N\), at the \(\alpha\) the harm checks chose, these runs are trained with the same data, initial weights and batch stream (checked by hash):

  • plain TD3+BC, and TD3+BC with BCA;
  • BCA's own doses spread evenly, shuffled, and placed by the noise map;
  • even caution ×1.25 and ×2, and even caution matched in strength to BCA;
  • behaviour cloning only;
  • plain TD3+BC and BCA on the full log (no shift; the shift-specificity check below uses this BCA run), and in the no-stall comparison world (seeds 100–104);
  • BCA's band on the frozen plain critic: BCA's error predictor and band fitted on the critic of the plain TD3+BC run, without changing that critic or its actor. It is reported beside the ranking check, not judged (below).

How this test can show that BCA helps

The claim is a chain of three links, and each link has its own measurement, decided before any BCA run:

  1. BCA finds the harm. Its doses are largest on the training rows where the critic over-prefers its own command (the three detection checks: the ranking check, the shift-specificity check and the beyond-noise check, defined below), more so in the full-throttle region after the removal than on the full log, and not merely where target noise is large. Over-preference is the critic error that draws the actor toward worse commands, so in the fixed wording below "finds the harm" means passing these checks.
  2. Its caution, placed there, helps out of distribution. TD3+BC with BCA beats plain TD3+BC in a room-certified shifted situation, such as strong current (the beats-plain check), and also beats the same doses placed blindly and even caution of the same strength (the placement check). Winning over blind placements of the same caution is what shows that the placement, and so the detection, made the difference.
  3. BCA's actor still beats copying the recording. Three actor checks: no loss in the recorded conditions (the no-loss check), at least 5 points better than behaviour cloning, which copies the recorded commands (the beats-copying check), and no harm when nothing is removed (the no-harm-without-shift check).

Only when all three links hold does the verdict say "BCA finds the harm, and its caution, placed where it finds it, improves returns out of distribution while still improving on the recording." That wording was fixed in advance; "improving on the recording" in it means passing the beats-copying check, beating a copy of the recorded commands.

Detection: does BCA find the rows of large over-preference?

Three tests judged together under Holm's correction, a correction for testing several things at once that keeps the family's false-alarm rate at 5% (one-sided). All three are scored on BCA's own run: its doses at the end of training, at the kept training rows, against the exact over-preference \(\Delta_i\) of its own critic and actor (exact values from 1,000-step simulations).

  • The ranking check. A row among the 10% most over-preferred gets a larger dose than another row at least 60% of the time on average (50% is chance), with the lower bound above 50%.
  • The shift-specificity check. BCA's mean dose in the full-throttle region, divided by its mean dose elsewhere, is at least 1.05 times as large on the log after the removal as in the BCA run on the full log.
  • The beyond-noise check. The dose still follows the over-preference after the noise map and region membership are accounted for: the partial rank correlation (the correlation of the two rank orders after removing what the noise map and the region explain) is above 0, with its lower bound above 0.

Reported beside the ranking check, not judged: the same ranking score for BCA's band on the frozen plain critic, against that critic's own over-preference. BCA's own run shapes the critic it is scored on; this second score shows whether BCA's signal also finds the over-preferred rows of a critic it never influenced, the same plain critic on which the room check measured whether caution could help.

Actor: does BCA's caution help out of distribution?

  • The beats-plain check. BCA minus plain TD3+BC is at least 2 points on a room-certified shifted situation, significant within the Holm family of those situations.
  • The placement check. On each situation that passed the beats-plain check, BCA also beats its own doses spread evenly, its doses shuffled, and even caution of the same strength (one Holm family).
  • The no-loss check (no loss in the recorded conditions): the lower bound of BCA minus plain is above −2 points.
  • The beats-copying check (beats copying the recording): in the recorded conditions, BCA minus behaviour cloning is at least 5 points, lower bound above 0.
  • The no-harm-without-shift check: on the full log, BCA minus plain has a lower bound above −2 points in the recorded conditions and in every room-certified situation.
  • The harm-present check: in every room-certified situation, averaged over the confirmation seeds, the plain TD3+BC actor still loses at least 3 points to pushing past 80%: the same actor with every throttle capped at 80% scores at least 3 points more (the shift card's loss from pushing past 80%).

Failed runs count against BCA. A failed BCA run scores 0 points, the recording controller's level. A failed comparison run is rerun once; if it fails again, its difference with BCA is set to 0.

Strong current is where the missing command matters most: at the states it visits, the recording controller would have given the removed full-throttle command 57% of the time, against 17% in the recorded conditions (measured on the shift cards). And every strong-current start lies outside the recorded range (current above 0.7). The pull toward the recorded commands acts only on the recorded rows. In strong current the actor is extrapolating. What it carries over from training is a critic that sees no cost in pushing past 80%: the gapped data never show the stall and cannot tell the stalling world from the no-stall comparison world. The cloned actor, with no critic at all, pushes past 80% on 9% of strong-current steps and scores 54.

Reasoning. The power check measures how much results vary from seed to seed on the room-certified situations, and turns that spread into a number of seeds (The harm checks). With no room-certified situation there is nothing to measure it on, and no BCA test to size.

Outcome. It did not run, as its rule requires.

After the stop: what BCA needs to help with harm outside the data

EXPLORATORY Everything in this subsection lies outside the pre-registered gate, and no BCA was run: no band, calibration, threshold, dose or BCA update. It changes no pre-registered rule, and no claim about BCA rests on it.

The gate is the four pre-registered harm checks of The harm checks. Here they are named by what they ask: the check that the critic is wrong where data are missing, the check that acting on that error costs return, the check that caution placed at the harm could help (the room check: is there room for caution to help?) and the power check, which sets how many seeds a fair test of BCA needs. The first two passed. The room check found no shifted situation where caution placed at the harm beats even caution of the same strength by the required margin, so the gate stopped and the power check never ran.

The decision

The user did not accept the stop and asked what has to change for BCA to help with harm that lies outside the recorded data. An exploratory analysis, with no BCA, answered with a written plan (PLAN.md, listed under Sources at the end of this subsection). Below, "the plan" always means this follow-up plan, not the environment's design. The user then asked for both claims at the end of this subsection to be tested, starting with the first: that BCA flags the actor's commands that the data do not support.

  • an exploratory BCA detection check (does BCA's band flag the rows where the critic is wrong?): folded into Stage 2, BCA's own pre-registered test, which is committed to the repository and approved by the user; no BCA run has started yet;

Why BCA as built cannot reach this harm

A reading of the gate's saved critics and actors (no training, no BCA; means over seeds {{beyond/design/seeds_lo#i}}–{{beyond/design/seeds_hi#i}}) gave two findings.

Where the loss comes from

Problem. The gate measured a large loss: in strong current, the actor trained on the critic of the log after the removal (the gapped critic) scores {{beyond/anatomy/strong_gapped/mean#f1}} points, against {{beyond/anatomy/strong_full/mean#f1}} for the actor of the critic trained on the full log. The data contain no strong current, so where does the actor learn the commands that lose these points?

Reasoning. At training states in the full-throttle region, the gapped critic is right up to 80% throttle and wrong past it. Compared with thruster 1 at 80%, the critic's value changes by {{beyond/anatomy/critic_090#d2}} at 90% throttle, where the true value changes by {{beyond/anatomy/truth_090#d2}}, and by {{beyond/anatomy/critic_100#d2}} at full throttle, where the true value changes by {{beyond/anatomy/truth_100#d2}}. Past 80% its value changes by {{beyond/anatomy/slope_gapped/mean#d1}} per unit of thruster 1's throttle (one unit is the whole span from zero to full throttle), where the true value changes by {{beyond/anatomy/slope_true/mean#d1}}. The actor reads the critic only at training states, at its own command. There it learns to push past 80% at {{beyond/anatomy/past80_region_gapped/mean#p0}} of the training states in that region (the full-log critic's actor: {{beyond/anatomy/past80_region_full/mean#p0}}). In strong current, where every state lies beyond the recorded conditions, it pushes past 80% at {{beyond/anatomy/strong_past80_gapped#p0}} of the states it visits. The same actor with its commands capped at 80% scores {{beyond/anatomy/strong_gapped_capped#f1}}.

Outcome. The loss comes from one wrong belief, held at states that are in the data: past 80% the gapped critic rates more throttle as better, where the truth is that it is worse. The actor learns this belief at training states and carries it outward into strong current. Everything else it learned is sound.

Why BCA as built cannot touch that belief

Problem. BCA adds caution where it judges that the critic may be wrong. Here the critic is badly wrong, so why would BCA not repair the loss?

Reasoning. Two things stand in the way. First, BCA reads its band only at recorded commands, where this critic is accurate. The band is fitted, calibrated and read at recorded (state, command) rows, and there the gapped critic judges how much better the recorded command is than the stall-aware controller's command with an average error of {{beyond/anatomy/recorded_abs_error_gapped/mean#f2}}, smaller than the full-log critic's {{beyond/anatomy/recorded_abs_error_full/mean#f2}}. Nothing in BCA reads the band at the actor's own command, past 80%, where the error is. Second, BCA's dose acts on the pull toward the recorded command. At region training states the recorded thruster 1 throttle averages {{beyond/anatomy/throttle1_region_recorded/mean#f2}}, while the actor's averages {{beyond/anatomy/throttle1_region_gapped_actor/mean#f2}}, so a harder pull says "drive like the recorder", not "stop at 80%". The room check set this pull from the exact harm, and no situation passed its mark: in each, the pull placed at the harm was about level with even caution of the same strength.

Outcome. As built, BCA neither sees this error nor acts where it does harm. Helping here needs a change in where BCA reads its band and in what its caution acts on. Stage 1 tests whether caution acting there can work at all.

Stage 1: can caution reach the harm through the value the actor climbs?

The actor's loss (see Methods) has two parts: the critic's value of the actor's command, which the actor climbs, and the pull \(b_i\) toward the recorded command. The room check tested the pull. Stage 1 tests the value: every penalty arm lowers the value of the actor's own command by a penalty \(W\), at training states only (the even-extra-pull control below has no \(W\): it multiplies the pull by \(1 + \nu\) instead, with \(\nu\) set by the strength matching),

\[ \begin{aligned} L_{\text{actor}} = {} & -\lambda\,\operatorname{mean}_i \Big[ Q_1\big(x_i, \pi(x_i)\big) \\ & \qquad {} - k\,W\big(x_i, \pi(x_i)\big) \Big] + \operatorname{mean}_i\, b_i \end{aligned} \]

with \(\lambda\) computed from \(Q_1\) alone, as before. \(W\) itself never changes during training (the penalties built from networks keep their settings frozen), so it can affect the actor only through the actor's command. Everything else is the gate's actor-only run on the gapped critic, unchanged: the frozen gapped critic, the same cloned start, {{beyond/design/actor_steps#i}} steps at \(\alpha = {{beyond/design/alpha#t0}}\), the same rows in the same order, gate seeds {{beyond/design/seeds_lo#i}}–{{beyond/design/seeds_hi#i}}.

The main arm is an oracle. An oracle here is a penalty built from the exact answer, which no method that learns from data could have. If even it cannot beat untargeted caution of the same strength, no version of BCA can. It uses the critic's exact one-step error at any command, found with one exact simulator step from the training state:

\[ \begin{aligned} \delta^*(x, a) = {} & r(x, a) + {{beyond/design/gamma#t2}}\,\min_{j=1,2} Q_j\big(x', c(x')\big) \\ & - \min_{j=1,2} Q_j(x, a) \end{aligned} \]

where \(x'\) is the exact next state after command \(a\), \(c\) is the stall-aware controller (the controller this critic scores) and \(Q_1, Q_2\) are the frozen critic's two estimates. A negative \(\delta^*\) means the critic rates the command too highly. The penalty is how much more the critic overrates the actor's command \(a\) than the row's recorded command \(a_i\), with \(k = {{beyond/design/k_main#t0}}\):

\[ W(x_i, a) = \big[\delta^*(x_i, a_i) - \delta^*(x_i, a)\big]_+ \]

Here \([z]_+\) is \(z\) when it is positive and 0 otherwise. The penalty is zero at the recorded command and grows only where the critic is more wrong about the actor's command than about the recorded one.

Why the error is measured from the recorded command (a change from the plan, made before any training)

Problem. The plan's oracle was the plain size of the error, \(|\delta^*(x, a)|\). A check on the frozen critic alone, run before any training on {{beyond/design/check_e_states#i}} region training states per seed, showed that this form rewards going just past 80%: from 80% to 81% throttle the penalty shrinks at {{beyond/check_e/lit_fall/mean#p0}} of these states, and the throttle that the critic minus this penalty rates best lies past 80% at {{beyond/check_e/lit_best_past80/mean#p0}} of them, more often than for the critic alone ({{beyond/check_e/q1_best_past80/mean#p0}}).

Reasoning. At the recorded commands \(\delta^*\) already averages {{beyond/check_e/delta_at_recorded/mean#d2}}, for two reasons that have nothing to do with the harm: the critic was trained toward targets with random noise on the next command, while the oracle uses the noiseless command; and the oracle subtracts the lower of the critic's two estimates at the command, which sits below \(Q_1\): both estimates were trained toward the same targets, so the lower one tends to fall short of them. As the critic's false preference grows past 80%, \(\delta^*\) first falls from this level toward 0, so its size shrinks exactly where the penalty should grow.

Outcome. Measuring from the recorded command removes this starting level of {{beyond/check_e/delta_at_recorded/mean#d2}}: the penalty then shrinks at only {{beyond/check_e/main_fall/mean#p0}} of the states, and the best throttle lies past 80% at {{beyond/check_e/main_best_past80/mean#p0}}. The plan's form still ran, as a reported arm.

Untargeted controls of matched strength. Each is tuned, blind to any return, to change the actor as much as the oracle does, to within {{beyond/derived/match_dev_max#p1}} on every seed (the strength \(S\) the room check used: the root-mean-square change of the command on {{beyond/design/strength_states#i}} held-out states):

  • even extra pull: the pull toward the recorded command made stronger on every row (multiplied by \(1 + \nu\)), the caution the room check tried;
  • push less everywhere: a penalty on the size of every command;
  • two random penalty surfaces: frozen networks with random settings, of the same shape as the small network BCA uses to predict the size of the critic's error (its error-size network; Methods, BCA step 1): the same kind of penalty, placed at random.

Reported beside them, not used in any mark: the oracle at half strength (\(k = {{beyond/design/k_half#t1}}\)); a two-sided form, \(|\delta^*(x_i, a) - \delta^*(x_i, a_i)|\); the plan's two literal forms, the size of the error \(|\delta^*(x_i, a)|\) and its overrating side \([-\delta^*(x_i, a)]_+\); the exact long-run harm (the critic's error over {{beyond/design/harm_rollout_steps#i}} steps instead of one); the oracle taken from another training state; the underrating side of the error, \([\delta^*(x_i, a) - \delta^*(x_i, a_i)]_+\); and the clip to the recorded range, a steep penalty on any thruster command outside the range that holds the middle {{beyond/design/range_share#p0}} of the training commands. The clip uses no signal at all.

Locked before any return. Every arm, formula, control and mark was written into a file and committed to the repository (commit {{beyond/design/spec_commit#s}}) before any return of a trained arm was computed. The controls were matched from strengths alone. A lock file holding every actor's fingerprint was then written, and only after it was checked were exact returns computed, on the full 1,000-start bank of each situation. Seeds {{beyond/design/futility_seeds#s}} ran first, with a futility look: an early check, fixed in advance, that would have stopped the test if the oracle had clearly failed (the first row of the marks below). It said continue. The results were committed in commit {{beyond/design/results_commit#s}}.

What it means

Caution acting on the value reaches harm outside the data

Problem. The room check showed that a harder pull toward the recorded command, even placed at the exact harm, does not recover the loss in strong current. Can caution that lowers the value the actor climbs do better?

Reasoning. Applied only at training states, at the actor's own command, the oracle recovers {{beyond/pooled/recovery/mean#f1}} of the {{beyond/marks/recovery_harm#f1}} points lost in strong current, a situation the data never contain, and it gains {{beyond/derived/oracle_gain_other_lo#f1}} to {{beyond/derived/oracle_gain_other_hi#f1}} points in the other three situations.

Outcome. Yes, given exact information: caution reaches this harm when it acts on the value at the actor's own command, where the pull could not reach it.

Placement is almost the whole effect

Problem. The oracle might help only because it is caution of some strength, not because it sits where the critic is wrong.

Reasoning. The four untargeted controls of the same strength leave strong current between {{beyond/derived/untargeted_strong_lo#f1}} and {{beyond/derived/untargeted_strong_hi#f1}} points, about where no extra caution leaves it ({{beyond/stall/none/strong_current/mean#f1}}). The same exact information taken from another training state scores {{beyond/stall/state_shuffled_band/strong_current/mean#f1}}: {{beyond/derived/shuffled_minus_none_strong/mean#f1}} points above no extra caution, but {{beyond/derived/oracle_minus_shuffled_strong/mean#f1}} below the oracle (this arm is not matched in strength: it is {{beyond/derived/shuffled_below/lo#p0}} to {{beyond/derived/shuffled_below/hi#p0}} weaker than the oracle). The underrating side of the same error scores {{beyond/stall/underrating_half/strong_current/mean#f1}}.

Outcome. What repairs the harm is a signal that finds the actor's overrated commands at the state where they occur. Untargeted caution of the same strength recovers at most {{beyond/derived/untargeted_gain_strong_hi#f1}} points (mean over seeds), and the right information read at the wrong state recovers {{beyond/derived/shuffled_minus_none_strong/mean#f1}} of the oracle's {{beyond/pooled/recovery/mean#f1}}.

The oracle is cautious only where caution is needed; a plain clip to the recorded range is not

Problem. In the stalling world the clip to the recorded range, which uses no signal, scores {{beyond/stall/range_hinge/strong_current/mean#f1}} in strong current, well above the oracle's {{beyond/stall/perfect_band/strong_current/mean#f1}} (by {{beyond/derived/hinge_minus_oracle_strong/mean#f1}} points, and ahead on every seed). Does that make a signal unnecessary?

Reasoning. The clip wins in the stalling world because there every command past 80% is bad. In the no-stall world, where the missing commands help, the clip costs {{beyond/no_stall_cost/range_hinge/strong_current/mean#f1}} points in strong current and the oracle {{beyond/no_stall_cost/perfect_band/strong_current/mean#f1}}.

Outcome. No. A signal that detects only novelty can look as good as the oracle, or better, in the stalling world and pay for it in the other. This is why the claim that BCA identifies the harm, not just novelty, needs the trace test bed below.

The reading must be paired

Problem. The plan's literal oracle, the plain size of the error, still scores {{beyond/stall/perfect_band_literal/strong_current/mean#f1}} in strong current, close to the paired oracle's {{beyond/stall/perfect_band/strong_current/mean#f1}}. Is the pairing with the recorded command needed?

Reasoning. The check before training showed that the plain size rewards going just past 80%. Training shows the same lean: in strong current, the actor trained with the plain size is past 80% at {{beyond/stall_past80/perfect_band_literal/strong_current/mean#p0}} of the states it visits, against {{beyond/stall_past80/perfect_band/strong_current/mean#p0}} with the paired oracle and {{beyond/stall_past80/none/strong_current/mean#p0}} with no extra caution.

Outcome. The pairing is needed. A signal has to be read at the actor's command relative to the same signal at the recorded command, at the same state. For BCA this means its band width at the actor's command against its width at the recorded command, not the width alone.

At deployment, with no training: a distance check that falls back to a clip

A second route acts when the actor is used, not when it is trained. This diagnostic used the gate's saved actors, with no training and no BCA. At each state the actor visits, a check measures how far the (state, command) pair lies from the training data: the distance to its {{beyond/deploy/neighbours#i}}th-nearest training pair, after each number is put in standard units (its training mean subtracted, then divided by its training standard deviation). If that distance is larger than {{beyond/deploy/level#i}}% of the same distances measured on held-out recorded rows, the command at that state is clipped into the recorded range; otherwise the actor's own command is used. It was scored on the first {{beyond/deploy/n_starts#i}} starts of each bank, where the harm (the full-log critic's actor minus the gapped critic's actor, in strong current) is {{beyond/deploy/harm_on_starts#f1}} points.

The check recovers about half the harm, and costs nothing in the recorded conditions

Problem. Can a check at deployment, with no training, catch the actor's unsupported commands and repair the harm?

Reasoning. In strong current the check gains {{beyond/deploy/check_gain/strong_current/mean#d1}} ± {{beyond/deploy/check_gain/strong_current/se#f1}} points (± is the standard error over seeds), which is {{beyond/deploy/check_share_of_harm#p0}} of the harm. It changes the command at {{beyond/deploy/changed/strong_current/mean#p0}} of the states visited. Clipping at randomly chosen states, at the same rate, gains {{beyond/deploy/random_gain/strong_current/mean#d1}}; the check beats it by {{beyond/deploy/check_over_random/strong_current/mean#d1}} ± {{beyond/deploy/check_over_random/strong_current/se#f1}}, {{beyond/deploy/targeting_share_of_gain#p0}} of its gain. In the recorded conditions the check changes the command at {{beyond/deploy/changed/recorded/mean#p0}} of states and gains {{beyond/deploy/check_gain/recorded/mean#d1}} points. Falling back to the cloned actor instead of the clip would cost {{beyond/deploy/clone_fallback_cost/recorded#f1}} points there and {{beyond/deploy/clone_fallback_cost/big_kicks#f1}} with big starting pushes.

Outcome. It recovers about half the harm at no cost in the recorded conditions, and about a third of its gain comes from where it fires rather than how often. The fallback should be the clip, not the cloned actor.

Its limit is detection

Problem. Why does the check recover only about half the harm?

Reasoning. Clipping at every state, with no check at all, scores {{beyond/deploy/clip_all/strong_current/mean#f1}} in strong current and {{beyond/deploy/clip_all/recorded/mean#f1}} in the recorded conditions: in strong current it gains {{beyond/deploy/clip_all_gain/strong_current/mean#d1}}, as much as a perfect detector of past-80% proposals ({{beyond/deploy/perfect_gain/strong_current/mean#d1}}). The check catches only {{beyond/deploy/recall_strong/mean#p0}} of the actor's past-80% proposals in strong current, although {{beyond/deploy/precision_strong/mean#p0}} of the times it fires are on such proposals.

Outcome. In this world the clip itself is free; the check loses points because it misses most of the proposals it should catch, not because it fires at the wrong ones.

It finds unrecorded commands, not harm

Problem. Does the check find harmful commands, or only commands the data do not show?

Reasoning. In the no-stall world, where the missing commands help, the same check costs {{beyond/deploy/no_stall/cost/check_cost#f1}} points in strong current, {{beyond/deploy/no_stall/cost/check_cost_over_random#f1}} more than random clipping at its rate, and clipping at every state costs {{beyond/deploy/no_stall/cost/clip_all_cost#f1}}.

Outcome. It finds the commands the data do not show, in both worlds. Only the true outcome tells harmful ones from helpful ones.

What must change in BCA

The results point to a new version of BCA. Its signal stays BCA's own: the same one-step error, error-size network, calibration rows and rule for setting the band multiplier \(\beta\). What changes is where its band is read and what the band acts on. Because of that, results of the new version will not speak for BCA as published, nor the reverse.

  1. Read the band where the actor acts: in training at (training state, the actor's own command); at deployment at the command about to be carried out.
  2. Act on the value the actor climbs. In place of a dose on the pull, the actor climbs the critic's value minus a penalty from the band, at its own command. In the main version the penalty is read paired (see the next item):
\[ \begin{aligned} & Q_1\big(x_i, \pi(x_i)\big) \\ & \qquad {} - k\,\Big[ U\big(x_i, \pi(x_i)\big) - U(x_i, a_i) \Big]_+ \end{aligned} \]

where \(U\) is the band width of Methods, \(a_i\) the row's recorded command and \(k = {{beyond/status/stage2_k#t0}}\) is fixed in advance. The plan's unpaired form, the band's lower edge \(Q_1\big(x_i, \pi(x_i)\big) - k\,U\big(x_i, \pi(x_i)\big)\), runs beside it as a declared secondary arm. The dose's cap does not apply on this route.

  1. Read it paired: the width at the actor's command counts relative to the width at the recorded command at the same state, as Stage 1's oracle was before any training.
  2. Treat "cannot certify" as the strongest caution. BCA "cannot certify" a row when its calibration finds no finite band that covers the critic's error with the required confidence. BCA as published then gives that row dose 1, no caution at all; the new version gives it the widest band instead. This can act command by command only in the weighted second version, described two items below. With BCA's usual equal weights it would hold for every row at once or for none, so it says nothing about the command, and the pre-registration declares it inactive there.
  3. Let the actor learn from how the band's width changes with its command, just as it already learns from how the critic's value changes, with the error-size network's settings still frozen. This breaks the published design rule that the actor's training never reaches back through the calibration, which is why it is declared as a new method version.
  4. Show first that the width rises at unrecorded commands (a detection test, before any actor is trained with it). If it does not, a declared second version switches on the row weights that BCA's own rule for setting \(\beta\) already accepts: likelihood ratios, which say how much more likely a (state, command) pair is under the actor than in the calibration rows.
  5. Add the deployment check with the same band and a clip fallback, and score everything in both worlds, against the clip to the recorded range and against signals computed from the data alone.

The known shift: how often each current appears

Only one thing changes between training and test: how often each strength of current c shows up. The current is fixed for a whole episode and the controller can read it. Given the current, everything else (waves, starting positions, the recording controller, the physics) is the same in training and test. So the weight of any recorded step, the ratio of how likely it is at test to how likely it was in training, depends only on its current, and it can be written down exactly.

Each distribution of the current is a simple tilt over its range 0.1 to 1.1: the density is proportional to eλc. Negative λ favours weak currents, λ = 0 makes every current equally likely, positive λ favours strong ones. Training uses λ = −2. The current now ranges up to 1.1 in training too, so strong currents are rare in the data but present: weighting needs every test situation to appear at least sometimes in training.

Why weighting should matter

BCA sets its band width from the calibration bank so that 90% of errors fall inside. If the critic's errors are larger at strong currents, and the test sees strong currents more often than the bank did, the unweighted band is too narrow at test. Weighting each bank row by ω(c) makes the bank look like the test distribution, and the 90% holds again.

What runs, step by step

  1. Data with exact truth. For each of 5 seeds: a training log (500 episodes, about 42,500 rows after the gap), a calibration bank (1,000 rows, about 843 after the gap), held-out episodes, and 300 test episodes per test setting. Every row gets its exact true value by simulation.
  2. Train TD3+BC on the training log: 100,000 updates, standard settings. A second copy trains on the full log with nothing removed, to show what the gap costs.
  3. The user's go-ahead is needed here. Everything below fits or uses BCA's band.
  4. Mechanics check, no shift. Fit BCA's band on the trained critic. Measure coverage on held-out rows, whether band width ranks with the critic's exact error, and whether the dose follows the width.
  5. Weighting check. Coverage on the test episodes for the unweighted band, the exactly weighted band, and the band weighted by an estimated ratio (a classifier that sees only observations from the two distributions). Steps inside the removed cluster are reported separately.
  6. Room check, without BCA. Does caution placed by the exact critic error raise the test return at all, against no caution? If not, weighting cannot either, and coverage is the result.
  7. Improvement. Train TD3+BC with BCA unweighted and weighted, and compare exact returns on the test distribution, plus the cost with no shift.
